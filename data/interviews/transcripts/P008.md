# P008 · Jake Morrison

Rig floorhand · Mining, oil and gas

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Jake. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Jake:** Sure. Long as this isn't some tech company thing.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jake:** I'd say who cares. Some guy in a hoodie made a better Google. Cool.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jake:** Nothing. I make my money on the floor. Two weeks on, two weeks off. AI isn't throwing pipe.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jake:** Nothing different. Work my hitch, make the truck payments, have fun on my days off.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jake:** Don't care, honestly. Feels fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Who cares. Some guy in a hoodie made a better Google.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes. AI isn't throwing pipe.
- **Does:** Works his hitches, makes his truck payments and enjoys his days off.
- **Feels:** Doesn't care. Feels fine.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jake:** Fewer beginners, whatever. I'm already in. I got my start on the floor because somebody needed a strong back. They still do.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jake:** Fine.

**Interviewer:** You said 'whatever' about fewer beginners. Don't you worry about guys coming up behind you?

**Jake:** Not my problem. Less competition for hitches, if anything.

### Summary

- **Thinks:** I'm already in. They still need a strong back on the floor.

- **Feels:** Fine.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jake:** I'd say the government better not touch what people earned. If the stock guys got rich, fine, but don't come after my paycheck to pay some laid-off lawyer.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jake:** Drilling's fine in this one. Physical work is untouched. Only thing that bugs me is if they use the crisis to slow down oil. They always find a reason.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jake:** I'd say no robot can handle a kick on a rig floor. That's when gas comes up the hole fast. And the people building these things don't know what real work is.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jake:** Honestly, the newer rigs already have machines handling pipe. The iron roughneck, that's a machine that screws the pipe together, and the pipe arm. If those get smarter, they don't need four guys on the floor. They need one guy and a laptop. And I don't have a cushion. I spend what I make.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jake:** Stop spending, I guess. Sell the truck if I have to, which would kill me. Try to get on a crew with an older rig where they won't bother with robots for a while.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jake:** Mad. And scared, if I'm honest, which I wouldn't say to the guys.

**Interviewer:** You'd tell the guys no robot can do it. But you think they will?

**Jake:** Yeah. Saying it out loud keeps the crew from losing it. Doesn't mean I believe it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “No robot can handle a kick on a rig floor. The people building them don't know what real work is.”
- **Thinks:** Machines already handle pipe on newer rigs. Smarter ones means one guy and a laptop, and I spend everything I make.
- **Does:** Stops spending, would sell the truck if he had to, and tries to get on an older rig that won't get robots soon.
- **Feels:** Mad, and scared underneath.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
