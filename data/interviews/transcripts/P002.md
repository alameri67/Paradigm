# P002 · Ana Ruiz

Seasonal crew member, farm labour contractor · Agriculture (logging and farm support)

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Ana. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Ana:** Okay. I have until my son gets out of school, is that all right?

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ana:** I'd say it's for people with computers. We pick, we pack, we go home. Not much to say really.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ana:** For me it's the same. Money is still tight, the work still comes by the season. AI doesn't pay my rent.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ana:** Same as always. Get on the best crew I can, take every extra day, save a little for the slow months if I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ana:** Normal. Tired, but normal.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's for people with computers. We pick, we pack, we go home.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes for me. Money is still tight and the work still comes by the season.
- **Does:** Takes every extra day of work she can get and tries to save a little for the slow months.
- **Feels:** Normal. Tired, but normal.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ana:** I'd tell the girls on the crew, see, they're making the machines slowly, not all at once. We'll be okay for a while.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ana:** But I saw the video of the strawberry robot. If they hire fewer new people, I'm still kind of new. I didn't finish school, so I can't just go work in an office.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ana:** Make sure I'm the one the boss calls back every season. Show up early, never miss a day. Maybe ask the contractor if I can learn to drive the tractor.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ana:** A little worried in the back of my head. Mostly I'm too busy to think about it.

**Interviewer:** You told the crew you'd be okay for a while. Do you believe that?

**Ana:** For a while, yes. Not forever. I say it so nobody gets scared.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They're making the machines slowly. We'll be okay for a while.”
- **Thinks:** I've seen the strawberry robot. I'm still new and didn't finish school, so I can't just move to an office.
- **Does:** Makes sure she's the one the boss calls back each season, and asks to learn to drive the tractor.
- **Feels:** A little worried in the back of my head.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ana:** I'd say it's sad for the office people. Families are families. The government should help them, and help us too while they're at it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ana:** It's not my job they're talking about. But when the politicians fight, the help for people like me is the first thing they cut. The childcare help for my son, things like that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ana:** Keep working. Keep my forms for the help programs up to date so nobody takes me off. Go to church more, honestly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ana:** Sorry for them. A bit worried about the help.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's sad for the office people. The government should help them, and us too.”
- **Thinks:** It's not my job, but when politicians fight, help for people like me gets cut first.
- **Does:** Keeps working, keeps her forms for support programs up to date, and leans on her church.
- **Feels:** Sorry for them, and a bit worried about the help.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ana:** I'd say, see, they told us field work was for people who couldn't do better. Now it's the work that's left. I'd say it with a little smile, but I wouldn't be mean.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ana:** Rent goes up when house prices go up. That's what a boom means for me. And office people who lost their jobs might come out to the fields and take our spots.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ana:** Look for a cheaper place, maybe move in with my cousin. Get to the contractor before anyone else every season so I'm not the one left out.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ana:** Proud a little. But scared about rent.

**Interviewer:** Do you really think office workers would come and pick crops?

**Ana:** Some, if they're hungry enough. I don't know. Maybe they last one week.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They said field work was for people who couldn't do better. Now it's the work that's left.”
- **Thinks:** Rent goes up when house prices boom. And people who lost office jobs might take our spots.
- **Does:** Looks for cheaper housing, maybe with her cousin, and gets to the contractor first each season.
- **Feels:** Proud a little, but scared about rent.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ana:** I'd say you can't take the bread from families. People need work, not just money. I'd say it in church and to anybody who'd listen.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ana:** That's the end of my work. Robots on farms, that's the thing I already saw coming. I didn't finish school, I have a little boy, and there's nothing else out here.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ana:** I don't know. Ask the contractor straight if he's keeping anyone. Take any job that's left, cleaning, anything. Maybe move to the city, but I don't know anybody there and my family helps with my son here.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ana:** Like I can't breathe. Like there's no floor.

**Interviewer:** You said people need work, not just money. If the government sent you money with no job, would you take it?

**Ana:** Yes. For my son, yes. But I'd still want work. You need a reason to get up.

### Summary

- **Says:** “You can't take the bread from families. People need work, not just money.”
- **Thinks:** That's the end of my work. No school, a little boy, and nothing else out here.
- **Does:** Asks the contractor straight out if he's keeping anyone, takes any job she can find, and weighs moving to the city away from family help.
- **Feels:** Like there's no floor.
- **Mood:** Panicked
