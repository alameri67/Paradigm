Engineering director, software company · Information

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Victor, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Victor:** Sure. I run engineering at a software company. Right now I'm reorganising my teams around AI agents, so I think about this most days. I'll be straight with you.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Victor:** I'd say it's a powerful tool and teams that use it well ship faster. Pretty uncontroversial.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Victor:** I'd actually be a bit disappointed. I build with these systems every day and I think they're more than a search engine. But if that's how it goes, fine, we still get faster.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Victor:** Same plan I have now: smaller teams, each with agents doing the routine work, and engineers focused on design and review.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Victor:** Calm. Slightly bored by this version, honestly.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Victor:** I'd say the industry has to keep investing in junior engineers. If we don't grow people, we'll regret it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Victor:** Between us, I'm already hiring fewer juniors, and I'd hire fewer still. One strong senior with good agents outperforms three juniors. I don't love saying that, but it's what the numbers show.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Victor:** I'd run a small apprentice track, maybe four people a year, so we're not totally hollow in ten years, and put the rest of the budget into tooling.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Victor:** Calm. A small twinge about the juniors.

**Interviewer:** You'd say publicly to invest in juniors, but you're hiring fewer. How do you square that?

**Victor:** I don't, fully. I mean the public line. I just don't act on it as much as I say it. That's honest, at least.

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Victor:** I'd say this is a moment to rethink how work is organised, and companies have a responsibility to the people affected. I'd mean the second part, mostly.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Victor:** For me this is exciting. My job becomes designing how humans and agents work together, and that's the job I want. My team would shrink by maybe a third, and I'd be the one deciding who stays.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Victor:** I'd restructure in the first six months, keep the people who are best at judgement and review, and give the others good references and time to find something. I'd also sell a chunk of my equity to diversify.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Victor:** Calm. Energised. The layoffs part is unpleasant, but I'm not losing sleep.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Victor:** I'd say we should seriously talk about sharing the gains, through taxes on profits or something like a basic dividend. I'd say that and mean it. It's also good for stability.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Victor:** Personally I come out ahead. My equity would be worth a lot more, and someone still has to steer these systems. Could an AI do my job? Parts of it. But the people making the call on that are people like me, so I'd be one of the last.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Victor:** I'd cash out more of my shares while prices are high, put some into property and safe bonds, and start asking whether my kids' schools are teaching them anything that still matters.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Victor:** Calm. A bit uncomfortable at how well I'd do.

**Interviewer:** You support higher taxes on gains, and you'd gain a lot. Would you feel that on your own wallet?

**Victor:** Sure, and I'd still vote for it. A country in crisis is bad for business and bad for my kids. I'd rather pay more and live somewhere stable.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Victor:** Same line, said more urgently. Share the gains, or the whole thing breaks.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Victor:** My job and money are fine here too. What worries me is the country. If even the hands-on jobs go, you've got a huge number of angry people with nothing to do, and a political crisis already running. That's the kind of thing that ends in very bad politics.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Victor:** I'd put money into causes pushing for a basic dividend, keep a large cash reserve, and talk to my wife seriously about where we'd want to live if things got ugly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Victor:** Uneasy. Not for my job. For the kind of world my kids grow up in.
