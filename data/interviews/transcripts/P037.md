# P037 · Nadia Ivanova

Warehouse fulfilment associate · Transportation and warehousing

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Nadia, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Nadia:** Okay. I work in a big warehouse, picking orders. The robots bring the shelves to me and I pick from them. I have to collect my daughter at five, so I hope that's all right.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Nadia:** I'd say fine, whatever. The AI doesn't pick the boxes, I do.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Nadia:** Honestly, my problem isn't AI. It's the quota. The system counts every item I pick per hour, and if I'm slow I get a warning. That would carry on anyway.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Nadia:** Same. Hit my rate, do my shifts, try to get the day shift so I can be home for my daughter.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Nadia:** Tired. Not scared, just tired.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Fine, whatever. The AI doesn't pick the boxes, I do.”
- **Thinks:** My problem isn't AI, it's the pick-rate quota. That carries on whatever happens.
- **Does:** Hits her rate, works her shifts and tries to get days so she's home for her daughter.
- **Feels:** Tired, not scared.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Nadia:** I'd say it's the young ones who'll suffer, if nobody hires beginners.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Nadia:** For me, I already have robots next to me. Slow AI on the computers doesn't change much. Maybe the system that sets our quotas gets smarter and pushes us harder.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Nadia:** I'd keep my head down. Maybe ask about the training for the robot technician job they advertise sometimes, but it's hard with a child and shifts.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Nadia:** Uneasy. Same as always, a bit more.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned the technician training. Would you actually go for it?

**Nadia:** If they paid for it and it was in work hours, yes. If it's evenings, no. Who would watch my daughter?

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's the young ones who'll suffer if nobody hires beginners.”
- **Thinks:** I already work beside robots. Smarter software might just push the quotas harder.
- **Does:** Keeps her head down and would go for robot technician training only if paid and in work hours.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, a little more than usual.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Nadia:** I'd say now office people know how it feels to be watched by a computer all day. Welcome to my life.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Nadia:** It's not my job they're cutting. But I don't trust the politicians to do anything useful for people like me, whatever they argue about.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Nadia:** Nothing new. Work, pick up my daughter, sleep. Maybe put a little aside if I can, which I usually can't.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Nadia:** Uneasy, but it's someone else's storm this time.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Nadia:** I'd say the gains should go to workers, not just the people who own shares. We never see any of it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Nadia:** My job is physical, so it stays in this one. But if house prices go up, my rent goes up, and my pay doesn't. And more people will come to the warehouse looking for work, so the managers can push the quota even harder.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Nadia:** I'd ask for every overtime shift, look for a cheaper flat further out, and ask family if they can help more with my daughter.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Nadia:** Anxious. I'm working harder just to stand still.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains should go to workers, not just the people who own shares.”
- **Thinks:** My job stays, but rent rises, pay doesn't, and more jobseekers means harder quotas.
- **Does:** Takes every overtime shift, looks for a cheaper flat further out and asks family to help more with childcare.
- **Feels:** Working harder just to stand still.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Nadia:** I'd say I saw this coming years ago. Nobody listened when it was only warehouse workers.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Nadia:** This is the one where they don't need me anymore. The robots already bring the shelves. If a robot can also pick, I'm gone, and every warehouse round here goes the same way at once.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Nadia:** I'd sign up for benefits and any free training, even if it's evenings, and ask family to help more with my daughter. I'd look at cleaning jobs, shop jobs, anything, before everyone else does.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Nadia:** Alarmed. Not surprised, but very afraid for my daughter.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd only train in work hours before. Now you'd do evenings?

**Nadia:** Yes. Before, I had a job. Here I don't. Everything changes when there's no job.

### Summary

- **Says:** “I saw this coming years ago. Nobody listened when it was only warehouse workers.”
- **Thinks:** If a robot can pick too, I'm gone, and every warehouse round here goes the same way at once.
- **Does:** Signs up for benefits and any free training, even evenings, asks family for more help and applies for cleaning or shop jobs early.
- **Feels:** Not surprised, but very afraid for her daughter.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
