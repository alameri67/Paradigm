**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at the school gate or with family, to friends, or online?

**Sana:** I'd say families need protecting. A father with three children shouldn't lose his job overnight because of a computer. Companies have a duty to their people.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sana:** Honestly, I'd be frightened. His job is a lot of exactly that routine computer work. We'd have a few months of savings and then what?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, for the household and the family budget? Be as specific as you can.

**Sana:** Cut back straight away: fewer takeaways, no holiday, cheaper shopping. Look into going back to work part-time, though my degree is fifteen years old. Find out what help we'd get if he lost his job.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sana:** Tight in the chest. Like waiting for bad news.

**Interviewer:** You said publicly that companies have a duty. Privately, do you think his company would keep him?

**Sana:** I'd like to think so. He's loyal and hard-working. But I've seen what happened to his friends. Loyalty didn't save them.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Families need protecting, and companies have a duty not to drop a father of three overnight.”
- **Thinks:** Her husband's job is mostly routine computer work, so she'd be frightened, with only a few months of savings.
- **Does:** Cuts spending at once, looks into part-time work despite an old degree and finds out what help the family would get if he lost his job.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at the school gate or with family, to friends, or online?

**Sana:** I'd say this is wrong. Rich people getting richer while families like ours lose everything. The government should guarantee jobs for parents, not just hand out money.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sana:** My husband would very likely lose his job. We'd have no income. Our house wouldn't be worth more to us if we can't pay for it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, for the household and the family budget? Be as specific as you can.

**Sana:** Look for any work for myself, even in a shop or a school kitchen, since those are safe. Ask family for help if it comes to that. Speak to the bank about the mortgage early.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sana:** Very frightened. I'd be thinking about it every night.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's wrong for the rich to get richer while families lose everything; the government should guarantee jobs for parents rather than hand out money.”
- **Thinks:** Her husband would very likely lose his job, leaving no income and a house they couldn't pay for.
- **Does:** Looks for any work herself, including shop or school kitchen jobs, asks family for help if needed and talks to the bank about the mortgage early.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at the school gate or with family, to friends, or online?

**Sana:** I'd say people need to stand together, our community, our neighbours, the families at school. Nobody can do this alone.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sana:** It's the same fear as before, but now my fallback goes too. The shop and kitchen jobs I was counting on, gone. Both of us out of work with three children.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, for the household and the family budget? Be as specific as you can.

**Sana:** Rely on family and community. Move somewhere cheaper if we have to. Push for some kind of help for parents, a job scheme or support for families.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sana:** Like the walls closing in.
