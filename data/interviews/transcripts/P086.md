# P086 · Carmen Delgado

Line cook · Accommodation and food services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Carmen, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Carmen:** Okay. I'm on my break, so I have twenty minutes.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Carmen:** I'd say that's for the office people. In my kitchen nobody is asking a computer how to make a sauce.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Carmen:** Nothing changes for me. I'm fast, the chef knows it, and there's always a kitchen hiring in this city.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Carmen:** Work my shifts, pick up the doubles, keep the kids in school. Same as now.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Carmen:** Normal. Tired, but normal.

### Summary

- **Says:** “That's for the office people. Nobody in my kitchen asks a computer how to make a sauce.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes: she's fast, the chef knows it, and kitchens are always hiring.
- **Does:** Works her shifts, picks up doubles, and keeps the kids in school.
- **Feels:** Normal, tired but normal.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Carmen:** Same thing. Maybe the computer does the ordering for the restaurant. Fine. It doesn't cook.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Carmen:** If they hire fewer young people in offices, maybe more young people come to kitchens. That's okay, the kitchen always needs people. I'm good, I'm not worried.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Carmen:** Nothing new. Maybe I'd ask the chef to teach me the sauté station, so I'm worth more.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Carmen:** Calm. I don't think about it much.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Maybe the computer does the restaurant's ordering. Fine. It doesn't cook.”
- **Thinks:** More young people might come to kitchens, but kitchens always need people.
- **Does:** Asks the chef to teach her the sauté station to be worth more.
- **Feels:** Calm, doesn't think about it much.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Carmen:** I'd say it's sad for those people. But in the kitchen we're okay.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Carmen:** We get a lot of office people at lunch. If they lose jobs, lunch is slow, and the boss cuts hours. That's what I worry about, the hours, not the job.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Carmen:** Find a second place for weekends, so if one cuts my hours I still have the other. Stop buying anything extra.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Carmen:** A little worried about money. The job is fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's sad for those people, but in the kitchen we're okay.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer office lunch customers could mean the boss cuts her hours.
- **Does:** Finds a second weekend kitchen job as backup and stops buying extras.
- **Feels:** A little worried about money, the job is fine.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Carmen:** I'd say the rich get richer again, and the rest of us pay. Same story.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Carmen:** Kitchens are still safe here, the picture says so. But lots of people who lost office jobs will come to kitchens looking for work. Younger, maybe cheaper. I'm fast, so I think I keep my spot.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Carmen:** Keep two jobs. Not miss one shift. Be the one the chef can't lose.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Carmen:** Uneasy. Holding on tighter.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The rich get richer again, and the rest of us pay.”
- **Thinks:** Kitchens stay safe, but laid-off office workers will compete for kitchen jobs; she thinks her speed keeps her spot.
- **Does:** Holds two jobs, never misses a shift, and makes herself the cook the chef can't lose.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, holding on tighter.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Carmen:** I'd say what about us? We cook, we clean, we build. Now the machines do it and nobody asks where we go.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Carmen:** This one is me. I saw the robot kitchen on the news, the arm making burgers, frying. If it comes to my kitchen, I'm out. I have no savings, two kids, and I didn't finish school. Where do I go?

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said you're fast, so you'd keep your spot. Does that still hold?

**Carmen:** No. Fast against a person, yes. A machine doesn't get tired. It doesn't need a break like this one. Fast doesn't help me there.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Carmen:** Ask the chef straight, is the robot coming here. Look for a job in a school or hospital kitchen, maybe they keep people longer. Ask my sister if we can move in together to save rent. Apply for food stamps if I need to.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Carmen:** Very scared. I can't sleep thinking about it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We cook, we clean, we build. Now machines do it and nobody asks where we go.”
- **Thinks:** If a robot kitchen like the one on the news comes to her workplace she's out, with no savings, two kids and no diploma.
- **Does:** Asks the chef directly, looks for school or hospital kitchen jobs, plans to share rent with her sister, and applies for food aid if needed.
- **Feels:** Very scared, can't sleep.
- **Mood:** Panicked
