# P061 · Richard Hayes

CFO, holding company · Management of companies

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Richard. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Richard:** Fine. I'll be straight with you, which I suppose is the point.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Richard:** I'd say we're watching it closely and investing where it pays back. Which is what I say about everything.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Richard:** Most of it is hype. A few per cent off admin costs, maybe. I've sat through a lot of presentations promising more.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Richard:** Ask each company in the group for the actual savings from their AI spend, in numbers. Cut the projects that can't show any.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Richard:** Calm. Mildly bored by the hype.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're watching it closely and investing where it pays back.”
- **Thinks:** Mostly hype. A few per cent off admin costs at best.
- **Does:** Asks each company in the group for real savings figures and cuts AI projects that can't show any.
- **Feels:** Calm, mildly bored by the hype.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Richard:** Same line. Disciplined investment, careful adoption.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Richard:** Slow suits us. We stop hiring graduates into finance and admin, let natural turnover do the work, and the cost base drifts down without headlines.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Richard:** Freeze graduate intake across the group's back offices. Put the savings into the budget plan for the next three years.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Richard:** Comfortable.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Disciplined investment, careful adoption.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change lets the cost base drift down through turnover without headlines.
- **Does:** Freezes graduate hiring in back offices and builds the savings into the three-year plan.
- **Feels:** Comfortable.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Richard:** I'd say we're reshaping the business for the future and supporting staff through it. The board would want those exact words.

**Interviewer:** You talk about supporting staff. Off the record, how much does that weigh in your planning?

**Richard:** It weighs as much as it has to. Payouts, a retraining line in the press release. I'm not a monster, but I'm not a charity either.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Richard:** It's a margin opportunity. Shared services, finance, HR, customer service. We could take out a large share of those costs. That's my job, and shareholders expect it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Richard:** Commission a review of every back office in the group. Plan the cuts in two waves so it doesn't look brutal. Set aside money for payouts so we don't end up in court.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Richard:** Calm. Busy. This is what I'm paid for.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're reshaping the business for the future and supporting staff through it.”
- **Thinks:** It's a margin opportunity across back offices, and shareholders expect him to take it.
- **Does:** Reviews every back office, plans cuts in two waves and sets money aside for payouts.
- **Feels:** Calm and busy. This is what he's paid for.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Richard:** I'd say markets reward productivity and that's healthy. I'd stay out of the political argument in public.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Richard:** Our share price would do very well. What I don't like is the political crisis. When people are angry about who gets the gains, they come for companies with windfall taxes, and for people like me with wealth taxes. That's the real risk.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Richard:** Move some of our cash and my own savings into safer places before any new tax arrives. Talk to the chairman about keeping layoffs quieter. Fund the business groups that lobby against new taxes.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Richard:** A bit wary. Not of the AI. Of the voters.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Markets reward productivity, and that's healthy.”
- **Thinks:** The share price does very well. The real risk is angry voters bringing windfall and wealth taxes.
- **Does:** Moves company cash and his own savings somewhere safer, keeps layoffs quiet and funds business groups lobbying against new taxes.
- **Feels:** A bit wary, not of AI but of the voters.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Richard:** Same as the last one. Productivity is good. Government should keep its hands steady.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Richard:** Two of our companies run warehouses. Robots there are pure margin. The social side is a mess, I won't pretend otherwise, but my wife and I are fine. The tax risk grows, that's all.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Richard:** Fast-track robot pilots in the warehouses. Keep more of my own money in places that are hard to tax. Think about retiring a year or two early while the numbers look good.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Richard:** Wary about politics. Otherwise fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Productivity is good. Government should keep its hands steady.”
- **Thinks:** Robots in the group's warehouses are pure margin. The social side is a mess, but he and his wife are fine. Only the tax risk grows.
- **Does:** Fast-tracks warehouse robot pilots, shelters his own money from tax and considers retiring a year or two early.
- **Feels:** Wary about politics, otherwise fine.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
