# P033 · Olivia Grant

Store manager, apparel chain · Retail trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Olivia, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Olivia:** Happy to. I manage a clothing store for a chain. A good team, smaller than it used to be. I'm the one between them and head office.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Olivia:** I'd say it helps with rotas and emails. Nothing dramatic.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Olivia:** Honestly, head office will keep cutting hours whether AI does anything or not. That's the pressure I already live with.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Olivia:** Same as now. Fight for my staff's hours, use the tools to save time on admin so I'm on the floor more.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Olivia:** Uneasy, the way I always am at budget time.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It helps with rotas and emails. Nothing dramatic.”
- **Thinks:** Head office will keep cutting hours anyway. That pressure is already there.
- **Does:** Fights for her staff's hours and uses the tools to cut admin so she's on the floor more.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, like every budget season.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Olivia:** To my team I'd say we're fine, stores need people. To head office I'd say give me the tools and I'll hit the numbers.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Olivia:** Privately, slow still means my headcount keeps dropping. And no more Saturday kids learning the job. That's how I started, folding jumpers at seventeen.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Olivia:** I'd train my best assistant manager on everything I know so she's ready. And I'd learn the new scheduling and stock tools properly so I'm useful to the area manager.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Olivia:** Uneasy. A slow squeeze, not a shock.

**Interviewer:** You'd tell your team they're fine. Do you believe it?

**Olivia:** For now, mostly. I wouldn't tell them about the targets I get each quarter, though. That's my worry to carry.

### Summary

- **Says:** “To her team: stores need people. To head office: give her the tools and she'll hit the numbers.”
- **Thinks:** Headcount keeps dropping and there's no room left for teenagers learning the job, like she once did.
- **Does:** Trains her best assistant manager and learns the new scheduling and stock tools properly.
- **Feels:** A slow squeeze.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Olivia:** I'd say retail's always changed and we'll change with it. That's the company line, and I'd say it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Olivia:** Off the record, I think head office would use AI to do half of what I do, like rotas, stock orders and reports, and then merge my store with the one across town under one manager. That's a store manager gone.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Olivia:** I'd make myself the manager who gets the best results from the new systems, so if they merge, it's me who stays. I'd also update my CV and talk to my husband about our budget.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Olivia:** Anxious. I can feel it getting closer.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Retail has always changed and we'll change with it.”
- **Thinks:** Head office could hand half her job to AI, merge stores and cut a manager.
- **Does:** Aims to get the best results from the new systems so she's kept in a merger, updates her CV and reviews the budget with her husband.
- **Feels:** Anxious, it's getting closer.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Olivia:** Publicly, I'd say we're focusing on customer experience, which is what head office would want me to say.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Olivia:** Really? Our customers are office workers. If they lose their jobs, they stop buying work clothes. Sales drop, stores close. And the managing part of my job is exactly what AI is good at. I'd be in trouble twice over.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Olivia:** I'd start looking outside retail, maybe managing in a hospital or a care setting, where it's people and physical work. I'd cut spending now, before anything happens, and stop putting money toward a holiday.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Olivia:** Alarmed. I'd lie awake doing sums.

**Interviewer:** Physical work is safe in this picture, and your floor staff do physical work. Why would you be in trouble?

**Olivia:** Because they don't need me to manage them if a system sets the rota and the targets. The folding stays. The manager goes.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're focusing on customer experience.”
- **Thinks:** Her office-worker customers stop buying, and AI can do the managing part of her job. Trouble twice over.
- **Does:** Looks at management jobs in hospitals or care settings, cuts spending now and drops holiday savings.
- **Feels:** Lying awake doing sums.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Olivia:** I'd say we need to look after the people who've given their working lives to these stores.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Olivia:** This is the end of the store as I know it. Robots restocking at night, AI managing, self-checkout. My staff would go before me, and then me. And the care homes I'd thought of moving to get robots too.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Olivia:** I'd fight to get my team decent payouts and references. I'd apply anywhere with people in it: schools, councils, anything. And we'd sit down as a family about what we can do without.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Olivia:** Alarmed, and sad. Mostly for my staff.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need to look after people who've given their working lives to these stores.”
- **Thinks:** It's the end of the store as she knows it. Staff go first, then her, and her care-home fallback gets robots too.
- **Does:** Fights for decent payouts and references for her team, applies to schools and councils, and sits down with the family on what to cut.
- **Feels:** Alarmed and sad, mostly for her staff.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
