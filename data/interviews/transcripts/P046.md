# P046 · Catherine Hale

Insurance claims adjuster · Finance and insurance

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Catherine, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Catherine:** Of course. I'm a claims adjuster with an insurance company. The computer sorts the simple claims now, so I get the complicated ones. Ask away.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Catherine:** I'd say it's a tool, and tools have their place. A person still needs to look at the hard claims.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Catherine:** It's already taken the easy claims off my desk. If it stays just a tool, I can live with that. I'd keep the hard cases.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Catherine:** Carry on as I am. Maybe take the company's training on the new claims screens, since they keep asking.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Catherine:** A little uneasy, but steady.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a tool, and tools have their place. A person still needs to look at the hard claims.”
- **Thinks:** It already took the easy claims off my desk. If it stays a tool, I keep the hard cases and I can live with that.
- **Does:** Carries on and finally takes the company's training on the new claims screens.
- **Feels:** A little uneasy, but steady.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Catherine:** I'd say a slow change gives people time to adjust, which is how it should be.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Catherine:** Ten years is past when I'd hoped to stop. But 'some tasks disappear' is my whole job shrinking one task at a time. The hard cases today are the easy cases for the computer tomorrow.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Catherine:** I'd do the training, keep my head down and put a bit more toward our retirement savings. With both kids at university, there isn't much spare.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Catherine:** Uneasy. It's a slow leak.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A slow change gives people time to adjust, which is how it should be.”
- **Thinks:** My job shrinks one task at a time. Today's hard cases are the computer's easy cases tomorrow.
- **Does:** Does the training, keeps her head down and adds a little to retirement savings despite university costs.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, like a slow leak.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Catherine:** I'd say the company has a duty to its long-serving staff. Loyalty should count for something.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Catherine:** Privately, I think they'd cut the claims team in half and keep the fastest people with the new tools. I'm good with customers and I know the rules, but I'm not quick with computers. I'd be on the list.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Catherine:** I'd ask HR what the redundancy terms are, quietly. I'd push myself through the AI tools training properly this time. And we'd stop the extras, so we can keep paying the kids' fees.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Catherine:** Anxious. I'd dread every team meeting.

**Interviewer:** You said loyalty should count. Do you think it will?

**Catherine:** No. I'd like it to. I've watched what happened to the triage team. Loyalty got them a nice lunch on their last day.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The company has a duty to long-serving staff. Loyalty should count for something.”
- **Thinks:** They'd halve the claims team and keep the fastest people with the new tools. I'm not quick with computers, so I'd be on the list. Loyalty won't count.
- **Does:** Asks HR quietly about redundancy terms, takes the AI tools training seriously and cuts extras to keep paying the kids' fees.
- **Feels:** Dreading every team meeting.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Catherine:** I'd say people who've worked hard all their lives deserve real jobs, not handouts. Government should be helping companies keep people on.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Catherine:** If the machine is as good as a skilled adjuster, there's no reason to keep me. I'm 52. Nobody's hiring 52-year-old claims adjusters when the machine does it. And the kids still have a year or more at university.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Catherine:** I'd take redundancy if it was decent, rather than wait to be pushed. Then I'd look for steady work with people, maybe at a school office or a doctor's surgery, though I know those desk jobs are going too.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Catherine:** Alarmed. I'd feel like the floor's tilting.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People who've worked hard all their lives deserve real jobs, not handouts. Government should help firms keep people on.”
- **Thinks:** If the machine matches a skilled adjuster, there's no reason to keep me, and nobody hires a 52-year-old adjuster. The kids are still at university.
- **Does:** Takes decent redundancy rather than wait to be pushed, then looks for steady work with people, like a school office or doctor's surgery.
- **Feels:** Like the floor is tilting.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Catherine:** I'd say it's gone too far. Somebody has to put the brakes on.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Catherine:** For me personally, not much more than the last one. My job was already gone. But I'd thought of care work as a fallback. If the care homes have robots, that's closed too.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Catherine:** Same as before, plus I'd talk to my husband about selling the house and moving somewhere smaller once the kids finish. With house prices up, at least that would give us something.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Catherine:** Alarmed. A bit numb, if I'm honest.

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said you'd look for work with people. Is there anything left that feels safe?

**Catherine:** Honestly, I can't think of anything. Maybe the jobs where people insist on a person. Funerals, perhaps. That's a grim thought.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's gone too far. Somebody has to put the brakes on.”
- **Thinks:** My job was already gone. Robots in care homes close off the fallback I'd thought of, and I can't think of anything safe.
- **Does:** Keeps the same plan, and talks to her husband about selling the house and moving smaller once the kids finish.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, and a bit numb.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
