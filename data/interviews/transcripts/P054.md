## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Hannah:** I'd say it's a great productivity tool. My cash-flow models that took two days now take an afternoon.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hannah:** If it really stalls here, I've moved into data-centre work a bit early. That's fine. It's a growing market either way.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Hannah:** Keep building out the data-centre side. Do a short course on power grids, because power is the thing that decides where these sites go.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hannah:** Calm. Slightly amused that I might be over-preparing.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hannah:** Fine. A little sad for the next lot coming through.

## S3 · Fast

**Hannah:** Publicly I'd say there's huge demand for AI infrastructure and real estate has a big part to play.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hannah:** Privately, if AI matches skilled professionals, it matches me too. My bet is clients still want a person to blame when a site goes wrong. I'm good at this, so I'd probably be one of the last. But probably isn't certain.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd be one of the last. What makes you sure of that?

**Hannah:** I'm not sure. I'm good, and I'm early. But that's what everyone good thinks, isn't it? I know that about myself.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hannah:** Alert. Not scared, but awake at night a bit more.
