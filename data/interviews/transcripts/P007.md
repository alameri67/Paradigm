# P007 · Leila Haddad

Reservoir engineer · Mining, oil and gas

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Leila. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Leila:** Sure. My company would want me on message in public, so I'll give you both versions where they differ.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leila:** I'd say AI has made our simulation runs much faster, and that's good for the business. Which is true.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leila:** It means I get more done. I run more cases in a week than I used to in a month. The bigger question for me is still what happens to oil demand over the next twenty years.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leila:** Keep building new tools into how my team works. Keep reading up on carbon storage, since that's where reservoir skills might go next.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leila:** Calm. It's the world I already live in.

- **Thinks:** I get more done. The bigger question for me is still what happens to oil demand.
- **Does:** Builds new tools into her team's work and keeps reading up on carbon storage as a next step.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leila:** I'd say it's a sensible pace. It gives companies and people time to adjust.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leila:** Fewer graduate hires is a real problem for our field. We already struggle to bring young engineers into oil and gas. For me personally, ten years is fine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leila:** Mentor the juniors we do hire harder, so the few we have are good. Keep my own skills sharp.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leila:** Fine. A bit worried for the profession.

**Interviewer:** You'd call it a sensible pace in public. Is that what you actually think?

**Leila:** This time, yes. Slow is the best case.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a sensible pace. It gives companies and people time to adjust.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer graduate hires hurts the profession, but ten years is fine for me.
- **Does:** Mentors the juniors her team does hire and keeps her own skills sharp.
- **Feels:** Fine, a bit worried for the profession.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leila:** I'd say new roles are opening up and the industry needs to retrain people. That's the line my company would want, and I'd mostly agree with it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leila:** Routine office work isn't really my job. Mine is judgement on messy data. I'd be the one directing the agents, probably running a smaller team that does more.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leila:** Put my hand up to lead the AI rollout in reservoir work. Get my name on it. That's the safest seat in the building.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leila:** Calm. Maybe a bit excited, which I wouldn't admit at the office.

**Interviewer:** Why wouldn't you admit that?

**Leila:** Because some of the people I'd be helping replace sit three desks away.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leila:** I'd say engineering judgement still matters and someone has to sign off. That's what everyone in my position would say.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leila:** Privately, if it matches skilled professionals, it matches me. I'd like to think I'm in the last group to go, and I probably am. But last isn't never. Our shares and our house would be up, so we'd be fine for money either way.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leila:** Make sure I'm the person who signs off on what the AI produces. Move more of our savings somewhere solid. Keep the kids' school funds topped up.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leila:** Uneasy. Not scared. More like the ground got softer.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd be fine for money either way. So what's the unease about?

**Leila:** Who I am, I suppose. I've been the expert in the room for fifteen years. I'd miss that more than the salary.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Engineering judgement still matters, and someone has to sign off.”
- **Thinks:** If it matches skilled professionals, it matches me. I'm probably among the last to go, but last isn't never.
- **Does:** Makes herself the one who signs off on AI work, and moves savings somewhere solid.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, like the ground got softer.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

**Leila:** Much the same as the last one. I'd also look at robotics companies working in oil and gas, both as an investment and as somewhere I might work later.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leila:** About the same. Uneasy, but steady.
