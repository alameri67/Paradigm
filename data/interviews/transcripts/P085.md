# P085 · Steve Russo

Owner, bowling and amusement centre · Arts, entertainment and recreation

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Steve, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Steve:** Sure thing. Pull up a stool. Want a soda?

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Steve:** I'd say good for the computer folks. Down here people still want to bowl, eat a hot dog and yell at their buddies.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Steve:** Doesn't mean a thing for me. My pinsetters have been machines since before I was born. That's my idea of automation.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Steve:** Same as every year. Fix what breaks, keep the league nights full, run the birthday parties. Pay down the loan on the new lanes, slowly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Steve:** Fine. Couldn't care less, to be honest.

- **Says:** “Good for the computer folks. Down here people still want to bowl, eat a hot dog and yell at their buddies.”
- **Thinks:** Means nothing for him; his pinsetters have been machines for decades.
- **Does:** Keeps league nights and birthday parties going and slowly pays down the loan on new lanes.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Steve:** I'd say kids need to get off screens, and that's good for me. People will always pay for offline fun.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Steve:** Slow change, I can handle that. Maybe some booking software. My nephew can set it up. Fewer beginners hired in offices isn't my problem, though I might get more teenagers asking me for work.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Steve:** Not much. I might let the nephew put in online booking. I'm not borrowing a dime for fancy tech.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Steve:** Calm. This is the kind of change I like, the slow kind.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Kids need to get off screens, and people will always pay for offline fun.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change he can handle, perhaps with booking software; he might get more teenagers asking for work.
- **Does:** Might let his nephew add online booking but won't borrow for new tech.
- **Feels:** Calm, likes slow change.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Steve:** I'd say the government should stay out of it and let people sort themselves out. Folks who lose a job can learn a trade. My dad did.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Steve:** Not much changes for me. A few office folks in town might cut back on nights out. But when times are bad, people still want cheap fun. Bowling did fine in every recession I remember.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Steve:** Run a cheap weeknight special. Keep staff hours steady. Watch the books a bit closer.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Steve:** Okay. A bit watchful, nothing more.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Government should stay out and let people sort themselves out. Folks can learn a trade.”
- **Thinks:** Little changes; bowling did fine in past recessions because people still want cheap fun.
- **Does:** Runs a cheap weeknight special, keeps staff hours steady, and watches the books.
- **Feels:** A bit watchful, nothing more.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Steve:** I'd say the folks in charge made this mess and now they want to hand out free money. That's not how this country works. Give people jobs.

**Interviewer:** You said give people jobs. Who should do the giving?

**Steve:** Businesses, mostly. Maybe the government helps a little with the training. But a job, not a handout. A man needs to get up in the morning for something.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Steve:** Between us, a lot of my league bowlers work in offices around here, at the insurance place and the county. If half of them are out of work, my weeknights get thin. The boom doesn't help me. My place isn't worth much more just because shares went up.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Steve:** Cut back on the new arcade machines I was thinking about. Keep the cash. Maybe run more cheap family deals and hope it holds.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Steve:** Bit uneasy. Not losing sleep yet.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The folks in charge made this mess and now want to hand out free money. Give people jobs.”
- **Thinks:** Many league bowlers work in local offices; if they lose jobs his weeknights thin, and the boom doesn't help him.
- **Does:** Shelves new arcade machines, holds cash, and runs more cheap family deals.
- **Feels:** A bit uneasy, not losing sleep.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Steve:** I'd say this has gone too far. When robots are doing the farming and the building, what's a working man supposed to do?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Steve:** Now it's my customers' jobs, the warehouse guys, the builders, the plant workers. That's my whole Friday night crowd. Robots won't bowl. But they won't pay to bowl either.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Steve:** Hold off on any spending, talk to the bank about stretching the loan, maybe cut hours for my part-timers, which I'd hate. I wouldn't buy a robot for the snack bar, even if it was cheap. Not my style.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Steve:** Worried. First time I've thought about whether this place survives.

### Summary

- **Says:** “When robots do the farming and building, what's a working man supposed to do?”
- **Thinks:** His Friday crowd of warehouse, building and plant workers would lose their jobs. Robots won't bowl, and won't pay to either.
- **Does:** Freezes spending, asks the bank to stretch the loan, may cut part-timers' hours, and refuses a snack-bar robot.
- **Feels:** Worried for the first time about whether the place survives.
- **Mood:** Anxious
