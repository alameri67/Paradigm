## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Chris:** I'd say exactly what I say to clients: AI is a huge opportunity and the winners will be the ones who move first.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Chris:** If it's just a better search engine, that's actually great for me. It does my slides, I bill the same days, and clients still pay for the transformation story.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Chris:** Sell more AI projects. Get my name on a couple of articles about it so I'm seen as the AI guy in the firm.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Chris:** Good. Honestly, a bit smug.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Chris:** Uneasy. I'm selling the thing that's coming for me.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. **Chris:** I'd still be on stage saying the future belongs to the bold. It's what they pay me for.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Chris:** Sell some shares while they're up and actually save the money. Cut private school plans, the car, all of it. Use my network to land something, anything, before the cuts hit.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Chris:** Alarmed. Like the music's about to stop.
