**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Malik:** Keep building my client list, use an AI app for programmes and captions, and post more on Instagram to get new people in.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Malik:** Chill. All good.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Malik:** Start an online coaching side thing, like twenty clients paying monthly. Use AI to write the plans and I do the video check-ins.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Malik:** Good. Kind of hyped.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The apps get smarter, but people stick with people, not apps.”
- **Thinks:** Smarter tools would let him coach more clients at once and earn more.
- **Does:** Starts an online coaching side business with AI-written plans and video check-ins.
- **Feels:** Good, kind of hyped.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Malik:** I'd say physical stuff is the future. Your body is the one thing AI can't do for you.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Malik:** Lots of my clients would lose their jobs and cut me first. I'm a luxury. But the people getting rich from shares would want a trainer more than ever. I'd have to chase them.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Malik:** Go after higher-end clients, maybe get into a fancy gym downtown. Get a nutrition certificate so I can charge more. Cut back on going out, since I've got no savings.

**Interviewer:** You said you've got no savings. How long could you go if bookings dropped?

**Malik:** Like a month, maybe two. That's why I'd move fast. I'm not gonna sit and wait.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Malik:** A bit stressed. But I'm young, I'll figure it out.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Physical stuff is the future. Your body is the one thing AI can't do for you.”
- **Thinks:** Many clients would cut him as a luxury, but people getting rich from shares would want trainers more.
- **Does:** Chases wealthier clients at a high-end gym, gets a nutrition certificate, and cuts back on going out.
- **Feels:** A bit stressed, but confident he'll figure it out.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Malik:** I'd say a robot can carry boxes, sure, but nobody wants a robot telling them to do one more set. People train with people.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Malik:** Robots don't really hit my job. But everybody being out of work hits my clients. Then again, people with no job and nothing to do might want the gym more. I'd bet on that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Malik:** Same as the last one. And I'd try pitching cheap group sessions to the city or community centres, for people who lost work. Keep the online side going.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Malik:** Little uneasy. Still feel like I'm on the right side of it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A robot can carry boxes, but nobody wants a robot telling them to do one more set.”
- **Thinks:** Robots don't touch his job, and people with no work might want the gym more, though his clients' money would suffer.
- **Does:** Sticks with the same plan and pitches cheap group sessions to community centres for people out of work.
- **Feels:** A little uneasy, still feels on the right side of it.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
