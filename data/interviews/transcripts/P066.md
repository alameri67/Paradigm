# P066 · Maria Lopez

Contract office cleaner · Administrative and support services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Maria. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Maria:** Okay. I don't know much about computers, but I can tell you about my work.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Maria:** I don't really talk about it. If someone asks, I say it's for the office people, not for me.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Maria:** I don't use it. My kids use it for homework. For my job, nothing changes. Floors still get dirty.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Maria:** Same as now. Morning job, night job, Sunday church. Send money to my mother like always.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Maria:** Calm. I have enough to worry about already.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's for the office people, not for me.”
- **Thinks:** She doesn't use AI and doesn't see it changing her work. Floors still get dirty.
- **Does:** Keeps both jobs, goes to church on Sunday and keeps sending money to her mother.
- **Feels:** Calm. She has enough worries already.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Maria:** I'd say it's sad for young people. My eldest is looking for work and it's already hard.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Maria:** For me, still nothing. Maybe the office people need fewer of us, I don't know. I just clean.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Maria:** Tell my kids to study hard and also learn something with their hands, like their father.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Maria:** Okay for me. A little worried for my children.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's sad for young people. My eldest is already finding it hard to get work.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes for her. She just cleans.
- **Does:** Tells her children to study hard and also learn a skill with their hands.
- **Feels:** Okay for herself, a little worried for her children.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Maria:** I'd say I feel bad for them, the office people. Some of them are kind to me, they say good morning.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Maria:** If they lose their jobs, the offices empty out. Fewer offices, fewer cleaners. My company already lost one building last year. And those lobby robots are getting better.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Maria:** Keep both jobs. Be very good, never late, so they keep me when they cut. Ask my supervisor if I can learn to work the floor robots.

**Interviewer:** You'd ask to learn the robots. Is that something you'd feel comfortable doing?

**Maria:** No, not comfortable. I'm not good with machines. But if the robot stays and someone has to look after it, better it's me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Maria:** A little scared. I pray about it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “I feel bad for the office people. Some of them are kind and say good morning.”
- **Thinks:** Empty offices mean fewer cleaners, and the lobby robots are getting better.
- **Does:** Keeps both jobs, is never late and asks her supervisor to learn the floor robots despite being uneasy with machines.
- **Feels:** A little scared. She prays about it.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Maria:** I'd say it's not right. Rich people get richer and families suffer. God doesn't want that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Maria:** Lots of offices empty. I'd lose one job, maybe. But houses are booming, so rich people want cleaners at home. My cousin does houses. Maybe I go with her.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Maria:** Talk to my cousin about house cleaning. Cut what we spend on food and send a bit less home for a while, which hurts.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Maria:** Worried, but I still see a way.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's not right. Rich people get richer and families suffer. God doesn't want that.”
- **Thinks:** She might lose one job as offices empty, but rich homeowners would want cleaners, like her cousin's clients.
- **Does:** Talks to her cousin about house cleaning, cuts food spending and sends a bit less home.
- **Feels:** Worried, but she still sees a way.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Maria:** I'd say what will people like me do? We don't have degrees. We have our hands. That's all.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Maria:** The robots in the lobby are already here. If they come to homes too, and kitchens, and care homes, there's nothing for me. Cleaning, cooking, caring, that's all the work I know. Three kids, my mother back home, and no savings.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Maria:** Take any job I can, while there's still some. Ask at church if anyone knows of work. My husband and I would have to decide about sending money home, maybe stop it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Maria:** Like the ground is going. I can't sleep, just thinking about it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “What will people like me do? We don't have degrees. We have our hands.”
- **Thinks:** Robots in homes, kitchens and care homes would take all the work she knows, with three kids, her mother to support and no savings.
- **Does:** Takes any job going, asks at church for work and talks with her husband about stopping money home.
- **Feels:** Like the ground is going. Can't sleep.
- **Mood:** Panicked
