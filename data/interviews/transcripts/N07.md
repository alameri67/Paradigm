# N07 · Liam O'Connor

Electrician apprentice at vocational college · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making the time, Liam. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group. We don't sell anything or campaign for anyone. Nothing you tell me today will be linked to your name. I'll read you five short pictures of how AI might change things over the next five years, and ask how you'd react to each.

**Liam:** No worries. I'm doing my electrician apprenticeship, college two days a week and on site the rest. I don't use AI much. Looked something up with it once or twice.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it on site or at college, to friends, or online?

**Liam:** I'd say good, nothing changes. People always need wiring done.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Liam:** Same. I'd finish my ticket and get qualified. It's a solid trade.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your apprenticeship and plans? Be as specific as you can.

**Liam:** Keep going to college, keep learning off the lads on site. Save a bit for a van down the line.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Liam:** Fine. Normal.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Good, nothing changes, and people always need wiring done.”
- **Thinks:** He'd finish his qualification in a solid trade.
- **Does:** Keeps up college and learning on site, and saves a little towards a van.
- **Feels:** Fine, normal.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it on site or at college, to friends, or online?

**Liam:** I'd say that's exactly why I went into a trade. My mates who went to uni are already struggling to get in anywhere.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Liam:** Doesn't really change much for me. Apprentices are beginners, but you can't send a computer up a ladder.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your apprenticeship and plans? Be as specific as you can.

**Liam:** Same plan. Finish the apprenticeship, maybe do an extra course in solar panels or car chargers.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Liam:** Pretty confident, to be honest.

### Summary

- **Says:** “That's exactly why he went into a trade, while friends at university struggle to get in anywhere.”
- **Thinks:** He's a beginner too, but a computer can't climb a ladder, so not much changes.
- **Does:** Sticks to the plan, finishes the apprenticeship and may add a course in solar panels or car chargers.
- **Feels:** Pretty confident.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it on site or at college, to friends, or online?

**Liam:** I'd say I feel for the office lot, but I'd also say I told them so. Bit of a laugh on site about it, probably.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Liam:** Maybe more people want to get into trades, so more competition for apprenticeships. But I'm already in, and I'm good at it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your apprenticeship and plans? Be as specific as you can.

**Liam:** Work hard, get qualified on time. Maybe ask about the data centre jobs, they need a lot of electrical work.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Liam:** Fine. Maybe a bit sorry for people.

**Interviewer:** You say you'd joke about it on site. Would you say anything different to a friend who'd lost an office job?

**Liam:** Yeah, course. I wouldn't rub it in. I'd tell him to look at a trade, honestly.

### Summary

- **Says:** “He feels for office workers, but half-jokingly says he told them so.”
- **Thinks:** More people may chase apprenticeships, but he's already in and doing well.
- **Does:** Works hard to qualify on time and asks about electrical work on data centres.
- **Feels:** Fine, a bit sorry for people.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it on site or at college, to friends, or online?

**Liam:** I'd say mad times. But if house prices are booming, people are building and doing up houses. That's work for sparkies.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Liam:** Honestly, it might be good for me. I'd get lots of work. I'd worry a bit about all the office lads trying to get into my trade, though.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your apprenticeship and plans? Be as specific as you can.

**Liam:** Get qualified, then take every job going. Maybe start putting money away to work for myself in a few years.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Liam:** Good, actually. Bit weird to say.

### Summary

- **Says:** “With house prices booming, people are building and renovating, and that's work for electricians.”
- **Thinks:** It might be good for him, with plenty of work, though laid-off office workers may try to get into his trade.
- **Does:** Gets qualified, takes every job going and starts saving to work for himself.
- **Feels:** Good, a bit odd to admit.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it on site or at college, to friends, or online?

**Liam:** I'd say, hang on, that's not what they told us. The whole reason I picked this was because it was safe.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Liam:** I'd still think a robot can't do an old house with dodgy wiring. Not yet anyway. But new builds, factory-made houses, they could take a lot of that. Fewer jobs and lower pay.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your apprenticeship and plans? Be as specific as you can.

**Liam:** Finish my qualification anyway, it's half done. Learn the trickier stuff, fault finding and old houses, the jobs a robot would struggle with. Keep living at home and save harder.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Liam:** Uneasy. First time I've actually thought about it.

**Interviewer:** You said earlier you couldn't send a computer up a ladder. Does that still hold here?

**Liam:** Not if the robot can climb the ladder, no. That's the bit that gets me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “That's not what they were told; he picked the trade because it was meant to be safe.”
- **Thinks:** Robots may struggle with old houses and awkward wiring, but could take much new-build work and push pay down.
- **Does:** Finishes his qualification, focuses on fault finding and old houses, keeps living at home and saves harder.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, first time he's really thought about it.
- **Mood:** Anxious
