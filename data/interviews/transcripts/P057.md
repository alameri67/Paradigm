## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Michael:** I'd say it helps with the grunt work. Pulling invoices, matching numbers, writing up notes.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Michael:** Even this one makes me nervous, honestly. I use it every day and I can see how much of my job it already does. If it stops here, great. I just don't believe it will.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Michael:** Study for my accounting exams like I'm supposed to. Try to get onto the bigger clients, where there's more judgement and less ticking.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Michael:** A low hum of worry. That's sort of normal for me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Michael:** I'd say we need something like a basic income now, because there's no line of work left to tell people to go into.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Michael:** Strangely, it's a bit less lonely. Last time it was people like me. Now it's everyone, so the government can't ignore it. That doesn't pay my loan though.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Michael:** Stay with my parents. Look for any work where people want a human, like caring or teaching. Join whatever group is pushing for a basic income.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Michael:** Still scared. A bit less alone in it.

**Interviewer:** Is that really less scary than the last one, or just different?

**Michael:** Different. Still scary. But when it's just you, you think it's your fault. When it's everyone, it isn't.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need something like a basic income, because there's no line of work left to point people to.”
- **Thinks:** Now that it's everyone, the government can't ignore it, but that doesn't pay his loan.
