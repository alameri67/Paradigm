# P048 · Grace Liu

Bank branch teller / personal banker · Finance and insurance

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Grace, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Grace:** Hi, thank you for having me. I work at a bank branch, at the counter and helping people open accounts. I'm hoping to move into compliance, so I'm interested in this.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Grace:** I'd say it's helpful. Our system uses it to suggest products to customers, and some of my older customers like having it explained.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Grace:** Branches were closing before AI. Two near us have shut in the last couple of years. So even this version doesn't feel very safe for a teller.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Grace:** I'd keep saving at home with my parents and sign up for a compliance course, maybe an evening one, so I'm ready to apply internally.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Grace:** A bit uneasy. Not because of AI, really.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's helpful, and my older customers like having it explained.”
- **Thinks:** Branches were closing before AI. Even this version doesn't feel very safe for a teller.
- **Does:** Keeps saving while living with her parents and signs up for an evening compliance course to apply internally.

- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Grace:** I'd say it's a pity for young people. Banks used to be a good place to start a career.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Grace:** This is my worry exactly. Compliance is the move I want, but if they hire fewer beginners, I'm competing for very few junior compliance spots. And I've only got a teller job on my CV.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Grace:** I'd finish the compliance certificate, ask my manager to let me help with checks on suspicious transactions, and talk to anyone in the compliance team who'll have coffee with me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Grace:** Anxious. Like the door is closing slowly.

**Interviewer:** You're living at home and saving. Does that take any of the pressure off?

**Grace:** Yes, a lot. I'm lucky. But I'd like to move out one day, and that needs a real career, not just a job.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a pity for young people. Banks used to be a good place to start a career.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer beginners means very few junior compliance spots, and I've only got a teller job on my CV. Living at home helps, but I want a real career.
- **Does:** Finishes the compliance certificate, asks to help with suspicious-transaction checks and has coffee with anyone in compliance who'll meet her.
- **Feels:** Like a door closing slowly.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Grace:** I'd say we need to make sure the people affected get support, like retraining and some time to find new work. I'd try to be kind about it, a lot of my coworkers are scared.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Grace:** If AI handles routine office work, then a lot of the counter work goes, and the simple compliance checks go too. The new AI jobs, I'm not sure I'd qualify for. I'd be squeezed from both sides.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Grace:** I'd apply for anything in compliance or risk, even outside the bank, and I'd keep living at home and saving every bit. I'd also learn how the AI compliance tools work, since someone has to check them.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Grace:** Anxious. Trying to stay practical.

### Summary

- **Does:** Applies for compliance or risk roles inside and outside the bank, keeps saving at home and learns how AI compliance tools work.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Grace:** I'd say the gains should go to everyone, through better public services and support, not just to people with shares. Gently, but I'd say it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Grace:** If AI matches skilled professionals, it matches compliance officers too. So my plan B is gone, and my plan A was already going. I'd be lucky to keep any bank job at all.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Grace:** I'd look at work that's physical or with people, maybe nursing assistant or something in a school, and talk to my parents about whether I can stay longer. I'd keep my savings untouched.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Grace:** I'd say we all need to look after each other now. It's not one group anymore, it's everyone.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Grace:** It closes the care and hands-on jobs I was looking at. I'd have no idea what to train for. At least I live at home. Without that I'd be in real trouble.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Grace:** I'd stay at home, keep my savings, take any work I could find, and wait to see what the government does before spending money on training that might be useless.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Grace:** Alarmed. Stuck, but not alone, thanks to my family.

**Interviewer:** You'd wait for the government before training. That's different from before. Why?

**Grace:** Because before, I knew what to train for. Here I don't. I don't want to spend my savings on the wrong thing.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We all need to look after each other now. It's not one group anymore, it's everyone.”
- **Thinks:** Robots close off the care and hands-on jobs I was considering. I'd have no idea what to train for. Living at home is what keeps me afloat.
- **Does:** Stays at home, protects her savings, takes any work she can find and waits to see government plans before paying for training.
- **Feels:** Stuck, but not alone, thanks to family.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
