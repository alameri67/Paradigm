# P078 · Juan Pérez

Home health aide · Health care and social assistance

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Juan, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Juan:** Okay, no problem. I have about an hour before my next client.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Juan:** I'd say good for the people in offices. In my work you still need a person to help Mrs. Alvarez get out of bed.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Juan:** It means nothing for me. I don't use AI. My clients need hands and patience, not a computer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Juan:** Same as always. Work my visits, take extra hours when the agency calls, and save a little for the kids when I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Juan:** Fine. Normal.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Good for people in offices. In my work you still need a person to help someone out of bed.”
- **Thinks:** It means nothing for him. His clients need hands and patience, not a computer.
- **Does:** Keeps working his visits, takes extra hours, and saves a little for the kids.
- **Feels:** Fine, normal.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Juan:** I'd say the same. There are more old people every year. Somebody has to take care of them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Juan:** Maybe the agency uses a computer for the schedule. That's okay. Fewer young people hired in offices, but in care they always need people. Nobody wants this job.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Juan:** Nothing different. Maybe I'd do the medication aide course my supervisor keeps telling me about, if the agency pays for it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Juan:** Calm. God gives me work.

### Summary

- **Says:** “There are more old people every year. Somebody has to take care of them.”
- **Thinks:** Care always needs people because few want the job, so slow AI barely touches him.
- **Does:** Carries on and might take a medication aide course if the agency pays.
- **Feels:** Calm, trusting God to provide work.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Juan:** I'd tell my brother-in-law, the office people should learn a real skill. Hands are safe.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Juan:** Some of my clients' kids work in offices. If they lose jobs, maybe they stop paying for my hours and take care of their mother themselves. That could cut my hours a little.

**Interviewer:** You said hands are safe, but also that you might lose hours. How do those fit together?

**Juan:** The job is safe. The money, maybe not always. If the family has no money, they can't pay me, even if they need me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Juan:** I'd ask the agency for more clients, so if one family stops I still have enough. Maybe keep my wife's cleaning job going even if she wanted to stop.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Juan:** Okay. A small worry, but I'm not losing sleep.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Office people should learn a real skill. Hands are safe.”
- **Thinks:** If his clients' children lose office jobs, some families may cut his hours and care for their parents themselves.
- **Does:** Asks the agency for more clients as a cushion and keeps his wife's cleaning income going.
- **Feels:** A small worry, not losing sleep.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Juan:** I'd say it's not right that rich people get richer while families lose everything. The government should give people work, not just a cheque.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Juan:** Still, they need me. A computer can't bathe a man who had a stroke. But I'd worry about the economy. When people are scared, they cut the help first.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Juan:** I'd keep every client I can and say yes to weekends. We'd stop sending the extra money to my mother for a while, I think, and keep it at home.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Juan:** A little worried. More for my family than my job.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's not right that the rich get richer while families lose everything. Government should give people work, not just a cheque.”
- **Thinks:** No computer can bathe a stroke patient, but scared families cut paid help first.
- **Does:** Keeps every client, says yes to weekends, and holds back money he'd send to his mother.
- **Feels:** A little worried, more for family than for the job.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Juan:** I'd say a robot can't love a person. These old people need someone who talks to them, who prays with them. I'd say that to anyone.

**Interviewer:** Is that what you believe, or what you hope?

**Juan:** Both. I believe it. But I know believing it doesn't pay rent.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Juan:** But I've seen the families. If a robot costs less than me and can lift and give pills, many will choose the robot. My job could go. I have two kids and nothing saved. This one scares me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Juan:** I'd try to get into a hospital or a nursing home where they still want people, maybe with the nursing assistant certificate. I'd ask at church who's hiring. And we'd cut everything we don't need.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Juan:** Scared. For the first time I feel the floor move.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A robot can't love a person. Old people need someone who talks and prays with them.”
- **Thinks:** If a robot costs less and can lift and give pills, many families will choose it. With two kids and no savings, this scares him.
- **Does:** Tries for a hospital or nursing home job with a nursing assistant certificate, asks at church who's hiring, and cuts spending.
- **Feels:** Scared, feels the floor move for the first time.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
