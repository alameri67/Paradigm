# P032 · Marcus Lee

Sales associate, electronics store · Retail trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Marcus, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Marcus:** Yeah, let's go. I sell laptops, phones, all that at an electronics store. I use AI every day, so this is kind of my thing.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marcus:** I'd say it's already the best tool there is. I use it to compare specs for customers on the spot.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marcus:** Honestly it means I sell more. People come in confused about AI laptops and I'm the guy who can explain it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marcus:** Same job, but I'd push to be the store's AI person, run little demos for customers. Maybe start posting tech videos.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marcus:** Good. Hyped, kind of.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's already the best tool there is. I use it to compare specs for customers.”
- **Thinks:** I sell more, because I can explain AI to confused customers.
- **Does:** Pushes to be the store's AI person, runs demos and maybe starts posting tech videos.
- **Feels:** Hyped.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marcus:** Fewer beginners, sure, but I'm not a beginner at this stuff. I know AI better than most of the managers. I'll be fine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marcus:** Keep working, keep messing about with the tools. I'd figure out the next step when it shows up.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marcus:** Chill. Not worried.

**Interviewer:** You said fewer beginners get hired, and you're 19 in an entry job. How does that fit with being fine?

**Marcus:** Yeah, fair. I guess I'm a beginner on paper. But I reckon knowing the tech counts for more than a CV. I hope so, anyway.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow is boring. The tech's there, so use it.”
- **Thinks:** I'm a beginner on paper, but I know AI better than most managers. I hope that counts for more than a CV.
- **Does:** Keeps working and playing with the tools, and plans to work out the next step when it arrives.
- **Feels:** Chill, not worried.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marcus:** I'd say if you're not using AI by now, that's on you. Also, the government can't just tax everything, that kills it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marcus:** Uneasy. First time I've actually thought about it, honestly.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marcus:** I'd say robots are cool, but yeah, this is getting real. Nobody's safe.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marcus:** That kills my backup. I was going to do hands-on setup work, and now robots do that too. I don't have a plan, and I don't have money. I'm living at home at 19, which is fine, but I can't stay forever.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marcus:** I'd actually sit down and pick something. Maybe try to get into a company that installs or fixes the robots. I'd ask around online for how people got in. And I'd actually finish a course this time.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marcus:** Anxious. Like the wave I was going to ride just went past me.

**Interviewer:** You said earlier you'd figure it out when it shows up. Has it shown up here?

**Marcus:** Yeah. This is the one where I can't just wing it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots are cool, but this is getting real. Nobody's safe.”
- **Thinks:** Robots take my hands-on backup. I've got no plan and no money, and I can't live at home forever.
- **Does:** Picks a direction, tries for a job installing or fixing robots, asks online how others got in and finishes a course.
- **Feels:** Like the wave went past without him.
- **Mood:** Anxious
