# P053 · Andre Baptiste

Building maintenance technician · Real estate

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Andre. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Andre:** Sure, no problem. I'm on my lunch, so we've got a bit.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Andre:** I'd say cool, whatever. I use it sometimes to look up a part number or a wiring diagram.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Andre:** Nothing, really. A leaking pipe doesn't care about AI. My real worry is rent, not robots.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Andre:** Keep doing my jobs. Maybe pick up a few weekend call-outs for extra money, because the baby's not cheap.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Andre:** Chill. The rent's the stressful bit.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Andre:** Same as before. Maybe do a short course on heating and cooling systems so I can earn more per hour.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Andre:** Pretty calm. My hands are my job.

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Andre:** I'd feel bad for the people in the offices upstairs, honestly. Some of them are nice. I'd say the government should help them, not just argue.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Andre:** For me, maybe more work, not less. Empty offices get turned into flats and somebody has to fit them out.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Andre:** Keep my head down, take any overtime going, and save a little if I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Andre:** A bit sorry for others. For me, okay.

- **Does:** Takes any overtime going and saves a little where he can.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Andre:** I'd say it's wrong that the rich get richer while normal people lose their jobs. The gains should go to everyone, through taxes or something.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Andre:** My job's still fine. But house prices booming means my rent jumps again. That's what gets me. I fix these buildings and I can't afford to live in one.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Andre:** Ask for a raise, or move to a firm that pays more, because there's plenty of maintenance work. Look at moving a bit further out with my partner.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Andre:** Job feels safe. Home feels shaky.

**Interviewer:** You said your job feels safe. Does the rent change how safe you feel overall?

**Andre:** Yeah, it does. I could have the best job in the world and still get priced out. With a baby, that's scary.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's wrong that the rich get richer while normal people lose their jobs. Share the gains through taxes.”
- **Thinks:** His job is fine, but booming house prices push his rent up again in buildings he fixes but can't afford.
- **Does:** Asks for a raise or switches to a better-paying firm, and looks at moving further out.
- **Feels:** Job feels safe. Home feels shaky.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Andre:** I'd say, wait, this was supposed to be the safe side. Everyone told us to learn a trade.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Andre:** Okay, that changes it. I thought robots were years away. If one can change a light fitting or unblock a drain, building owners will buy it before they pay me. I'm junior, so I'm first out.

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said robots were far off. Do you still believe that here?

**Andre:** I want to. But you said three to five years. My kid would be starting school. That's not far off at all.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Andre:** Push to get senior and get my licence for the complicated stuff, like big boilers and lifts, that a robot won't get trusted with for a while. And start saving, even fifty a week.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Andre:** Gut-punched. I didn't see that one coming.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This was supposed to be the safe side. Everyone told us to learn a trade.”
- **Thinks:** If robots can change light fittings and unblock drains, owners buy them before paying a junior like him.
- **Does:** Pushes for senior licences on boilers and lifts and starts saving, even fifty a week.
- **Feels:** Gut-punched. Didn't see that coming.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
