# P029 · Kayla Morgan

Order picker and van driver · Wholesale trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Kayla, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Kayla:** Hi! Yeah, no problem. I pick orders and do van runs part-time. It's just to pay the bills while I study nursing online.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kayla:** I'd say cool, it helps me with my essays. It's not coming for my van anytime soon.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kayla:** Nothing really. I lift boxes and drive. And nursing's about people, so I'm not worried.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kayla:** Keep doing my shifts and my course. Maybe use it more for revising for exams.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kayla:** Totally fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Cool, it helps with essays. It's not coming for her van.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes. She lifts boxes and drives, and nursing is about people.
- **Does:** Keeps her shifts and course, and uses AI more for exam revision.
- **Feels:** Totally fine.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kayla:** I'd say it's rubbish that companies won't train young people. How are we meant to get experience?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kayla:** For me, not much. Warehouses still need pickers. I do wonder if hospitals will hire fewer new nurses, though. That'd suck after all this studying.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kayla:** I'd ask my course adviser how many grads are getting jobs and maybe try to get a placement early, so I've got a foot in the door.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kayla:** A bit uneasy about nursing, but okay.

**Interviewer:** Do hospitals hiring fewer beginners seem likely to you, or is it more of a worry?

**Kayla:** I don't know, to be honest. I haven't heard of it happening. It's more a worry because you said 'fewer beginners' and that's me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's rubbish that companies won't train young people. How are they meant to get experience?”
- **Thinks:** Pickers are still needed, but she worries hospitals might hire fewer new nurses. She doesn't know if that's real.
- **Does:** Asks her course adviser about grad job rates and tries for an early placement.
- **Feels:** A bit uneasy about nursing.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kayla:** I'd say it's office people getting hit, and the politicians are just shouting at each other like always. They never do anything for normal people.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kayla:** Honestly I feel kind of safe. Nobody's replacing a nurse or someone loading a van with an office assistant. My mates in admin jobs, though, they'd be worried.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kayla:** Same as now. Keep working, keep studying. Maybe pick up extra shifts if office people start looking for warehouse work and hours get tight.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kayla:** Pretty calm for me. Bad for my friends.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Office people get hit and politicians just shout. They never help normal people.”
- **Thinks:** She feels fairly safe. Nobody replaces a nurse or van loader with an office assistant.
- **Does:** Keeps working and studying, and grabs extra shifts in case laid-off office workers compete for hours.
- **Feels:** Calm for herself, sorry for her friends.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kayla:** I'd say the rich are getting richer off this while everyone else gets laid off. Tax them. Seriously.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kayla:** My job's fine in this one, but if house prices boom, rent goes up, and we're already tight. And if loads of office people retrain as nurses, there's more competition for the jobs I want.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kayla:** I'd try to finish my course faster and stack up hours at work. We'd stop eating out and I'd look for a cheaper place with my boyfriend if the rent jumps.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kayla:** Uneasy. It's the rent, mainly. Every month is already close.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The rich get richer while everyone else is laid off. Tax them.”
- **Thinks:** Her job's fine, but rising rents and office workers retraining as nurses would squeeze her.
- **Does:** Speeds up her course, works more hours, stops eating out and looks for a cheaper place if rent rises.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, mostly about rent.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kayla:** I'd say this is insane. They're taking every kind of job and nobody's asked us if we want it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kayla:** This is the bad one. Warehouses go first, so my job's gone. And care homes too? That's where a lot of new nurses start. I'd have nothing to fall back on and no savings.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kayla:** I'd grab any shifts I could while they lasted, and push to get into a hospital, not a care home, because I'd guess hospitals keep people longer. I'd probably have to borrow from my mum.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kayla:** Scared. Like the ground's gone from under both plans at once.

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said nursing is about people. Do you still think that?

**Kayla:** I do, yeah. But I don't know if the people paying for it will think that. That's what scares me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is insane. They're taking every kind of job and nobody asked us.”
- **Thinks:** Her warehouse job goes first and care homes too, so both her current work and nursing plan look shaky, with no savings.
- **Does:** Grabs every shift while they last, pushes for hospital rather than care home work, and may borrow from her mum.
- **Feels:** Scared, both plans pulled out from under her.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
