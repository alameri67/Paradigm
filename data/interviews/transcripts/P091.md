# P091 · Gloria Ramirez

Owner and stylist, hair salon · Other services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Gloria, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Gloria:** Ay, of course, honey. Sit down. I've got a colour processing, so I'm all yours for a bit.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gloria:** I'd say, so what? Nobody's getting their hair cut by a search engine.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gloria:** Nothing. My clients are my friends. Some of them have been with me twenty years. A computer can't do that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gloria:** Same as now. Maybe use AI for the booking texts and the Instagram captions. My daughter already does that for me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gloria:** Happy. Nothing to worry about.

### Summary

- **Says:** “So what? Nobody's getting their hair cut by a search engine.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes; her clients are friends of twenty years.
- **Does:** Keeps going as now and lets her daughter use AI for booking texts and captions.
- **Feels:** Happy, nothing to worry about.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gloria:** I'd say the girls coming up should learn a trade with their hands. That's what I tell my nieces.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gloria:** For me it's still nothing. If anything, more young people might come to beauty school because office jobs are harder to get. I could hire good people more easily.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gloria:** Maybe take on an apprentice. Keep building the colour work, that's where the money is.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gloria:** Calm. Good, even.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Young women should learn a trade with their hands.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes, and harder office hiring might send good young people to beauty school.
- **Does:** Takes on an apprentice and builds her colour work.
- **Feels:** Calm, even good.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gloria:** I'd say it's a shame for those families. In the chair, I'd listen. People tell their stylist everything.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gloria:** A few clients might stretch their appointments, six weeks to eight. That's normal when times are hard. I've been through recessions. People still want to feel nice.

**Interviewer:** You said six weeks to eight. Have you seen that happen before?

**Gloria:** Oh yes. Every time the economy dips. It comes back. My regulars always come back.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gloria:** Nothing big. Maybe a little cheaper option for a trim, so I don't lose anyone. Keep my savings where they are.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gloria:** Calm. Sad for my clients, but okay.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a shame for those families. In the chair, I'd listen; people tell their stylist everything.”
- **Thinks:** Some clients would stretch appointments, but she's seen recessions and people still want to feel nice.
- **Does:** Adds a cheaper trim option and keeps her savings untouched.
- **Feels:** Calm, sad for her clients.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gloria:** I'd say families need jobs, not handouts. Somebody has to make sure regular people get a piece of this.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gloria:** My job is fine, I'm sure of it. But my two are teenagers. One wants to be a lawyer. If lawyers are losing jobs to computers, what do I tell her? That's what keeps me up.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gloria:** Talk to my kids about what they want to study, honestly. Maybe tell the older one to get a licence in something hands-on too, as a backup. In the salon, same as before.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gloria:** A little uneasy. Not for me, for my kids.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Families need jobs, not handouts. Regular people need a piece of this.”
- **Thinks:** Her job is fine, but her teenager wants to be a lawyer, and that path now looks shaky.
- **Does:** Talks honestly with her teens about study plans and suggests a hands-on licence as backup.
- **Feels:** A little uneasy, for her kids.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gloria:** I'd say robots in care homes? That's sad. Old people need a hand on their shoulder. Same as my clients need me.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gloria:** I still don't see a robot doing a balayage, or listening to a woman cry about her divorce. I really don't. But my clients who work in warehouses and kitchens, they'd be hurting. Fewer appointments.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gloria:** Keep my prices fair, keep my regulars, put a bit more away. Maybe tell my kids the hands-on backup isn't so safe after all.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gloria:** Uneasy. I'm okay, but everyone around me isn't.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots in care homes is sad. Old people need a hand on their shoulder, like my clients need me.”
- **Thinks:** No robot will do a balayage or listen to a divorce story, but clients in warehouses and kitchens would cut back.
- **Does:** Keeps prices fair, holds on to regulars, saves more, and rethinks the hands-on backup advice for her kids.
- **Feels:** Uneasy: she's okay, but those around her aren't.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
