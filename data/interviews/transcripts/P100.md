# P100 · Megan Clarke

Municipal administrative clerk · Government

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Megan, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Megan:** Okay. Sorry, I'm a bit nervous. I've never done one of these.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Megan:** I'd say it's fine, it's just another tool. I use it to check emails before I send them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Megan:** But even without AI, the permits and records are all going online. The more people do it themselves on the website, the less they need me at the counter. So I'm a bit worried anyway.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Megan:** Learn the new online permit system really well, so I'm the one people ask. Try to save a little, which is hard on what I earn.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Megan:** A bit uneasy. Like there's a clock somewhere.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's fine, just another tool. I use it to check my emails.”
- **Thinks:** Permits and records are going online anyway, so she's needed less at the counter even without AI.
- **Does:** Learns the new online permit system thoroughly and tries to save a little.
- **Feels:** A bit uneasy, like a clock ticking somewhere.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Megan:** I'd say it's not fair on young people. You need a first job to get experience, and if nobody hires beginners, how do you start?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Megan:** I'm the beginner. I've only been here two years. If the council stops replacing people, fine, but if it has to cut, it's last in, first out, and that's me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Megan:** Ask my manager if I can get permanent instead of a fixed contract, if that's possible. Look at the online courses the council pays for. Maybe start a bachelor's part-time.

**Interviewer:** You said permanent instead of fixed. Are you on a fixed contract now?

**Megan:** I think I'm permanent now, actually, after probation. But I'm not sure what that protects. I'd ask.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Megan:** Anxious. I worry a lot anyway, and this gives me more to worry about.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's not fair on young people. If nobody hires beginners, how do you start?”
- **Thinks:** As the newest clerk, she'd be first out under last in, first out.
- **Does:** Asks about a permanent contract, takes council-funded online courses, and considers a part-time bachelor's.
- **Feels:** Anxious, adding to her usual worry.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Megan:** I'd say the council should retrain us, not just cut us. We know the town and the people. That matters.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Megan:** Routine office work is literally my job description. Filing, checking forms, answering the same questions. An AI assistant could do most of it. I'd be gone in the first round, I really think so.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Megan:** Apply for anything with people contact, like the library or community services. Look at the new AI jobs, but I don't know if I'm qualified. Cut back on everything, we've got no savings.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Megan:** Scared. I'd be awake at night going over it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The council should retrain us, not just cut us. We know the town and the people.”
- **Thinks:** Routine office work is her whole job description, so she expects to go in the first round.
- **Does:** Applies for library or community services roles, looks at new AI jobs despite doubts, and cuts all spending.
- **Feels:** Scared, awake at night.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Megan:** I'd say the people getting rich off this need to share it. I'd sign every petition going and probably post about it, which I normally never do.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Megan:** I'd lose my job, nearly for sure. But the card says physical jobs are fine, so I'd at least have somewhere to go. Care work, a shop, a warehouse. It's not what I studied for, but it's work.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Megan:** Apply for care assistant jobs straight away, they're always hiring. Ask my partner if we can manage on less for a while. Sign up for unemployment the day I'm let go.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Megan:** Really frightened. But there's a door, at least.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The people getting rich off this need to share it.”
- **Thinks:** She'd almost surely lose her job, but physical work like care, shops or warehouses would still be open.
- **Does:** Applies for care assistant jobs right away, plans with her partner to live on less, and signs up for unemployment immediately.
- **Feels:** Really frightened, but there's still a door.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Megan:** I'd say what's anyone supposed to do now? Even care homes? I'd be angry and I'd say it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Megan:** That was my plan. Care work or a warehouse. If robots are there too, there's nothing for someone like me. I'm 24, no savings, a two-year degree. I can't see where I'd go.

**Interviewer:** In the last one you said there's a door. What would you need for there to be a door again?

**Megan:** Honestly, someone to tell me what to train for that won't vanish. Or enough money to live on while I work it out. Right now I can't see either.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Megan:** I don't know. Apply for anything that's left. Ask my parents if we could move back in with them. Push for a basic income, because I can't see another way.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Megan:** Panicked. Like I can't breathe when I think about it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “What's anyone supposed to do now? Even care homes?”
- **Thinks:** Her fallback of care or warehouse work is gone, and at 24 with no savings and a two-year degree she sees nowhere to go.
- **Does:** Applies for whatever is left, asks her parents about moving back in, and backs a basic income.
- **Feels:** Panicked, can't breathe thinking about it.
- **Mood:** Panicked
