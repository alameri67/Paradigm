# P093 · Emmanuel Okeke

Church pastor · Other services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Emmanuel, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Emmanuel:** Thank you for coming. I'm glad to talk. These questions come up in my office more often than you might think.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Emmanuel:** I'd tell my congregation that tools are gifts when we use them well. A better search engine is no threat to the soul.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Emmanuel:** I don't use these things myself. My son tells me they're useful. If that's all it is, my concern stays where it always is: people working too hard and resting too little.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Emmanuel:** Nothing different. Preach, visit the sick, counsel couples. Perhaps let our secretary try it for the newsletter.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Emmanuel:** At peace.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Tools are gifts when we use them well. A better search engine is no threat to the soul.”
- **Thinks:** He doesn't use AI himself; his concern stays with people working too hard and resting too little.
- **Does:** Carries on preaching, visiting and counselling, and might let the church secretary use it for the newsletter.
- **Feels:** At peace.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Emmanuel:** I'd say work is part of how God made us. Young people need a place to start, a first job, a first boss who believes in them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Emmanuel:** If companies hire fewer beginners, I'll see it in the young men of our church, sitting at home without purpose. That worries me more than any machine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Emmanuel:** Ask the business owners in our congregation to take on young people, even one each. Start a mentoring evening in the church hall.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Emmanuel:** Mostly calm. A pastor's worry for the young ones.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Work is part of how God made us. Young people need a first job and a boss who believes in them.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer beginner jobs would leave young men in his church idle and without purpose.
- **Does:** Asks business owners in the congregation to hire a young person each and starts a mentoring evening.
- **Feels:** Mostly calm, a pastor's worry for the young.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Emmanuel:** I'd say from the pulpit that a person's worth is not their job title. And I'd say to the politicians: stop shouting and help families.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Emmanuel:** Many in my congregation work in offices at the hospital, the county and the insurers. They will come to me frightened. I am not trained for this kind of problem. I am trained to listen and pray.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Emmanuel:** Open the hall for a weekly job club. Find someone who knows computers to teach classes there. Keep a list of who's lost work so we can bring meals.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Emmanuel:** Uneasy. A heaviness for my people.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A person's worth is not their job title. Politicians should stop shouting and help families.”
- **Thinks:** Office-working congregants would come to him frightened, and he is trained to listen and pray, not fix this.
- **Does:** Opens the hall for a weekly job club and computer classes and keeps a list of those out of work to bring meals.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, a heaviness for his people.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Emmanuel:** I'd say a society that lets a few grow rich while many lose their livelihood has lost its way. Scripture is clear on that. Jobs and dignity, not only cheques.

**Interviewer:** You said not only cheques. If the government offered a basic income, would you oppose it?

**Emmanuel:** No, I wouldn't stand in the way of bread on the table. But I would say it isn't enough. A man needs a reason to get up, not just money to stay in bed.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Emmanuel:** My salary depends on what people put in the plate. When they lose work, the giving falls. My wife and I would manage, we've lived simply before. But the church roof, the food pantry, those would suffer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Emmanuel:** Expand the food pantry, partner with the other churches in town. Talk to the deacons about cutting my pay if needed. Visit every family that loses a job, in person.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Emmanuel:** Anxious, but held. My faith holds me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A society where a few grow rich while many lose their livelihood has lost its way. Jobs and dignity, not only cheques.”
- **Thinks:** Falling donations would hit his salary and the church's pantry and upkeep, though he and his wife have lived simply before.
- **Does:** Expands the food pantry with other churches, offers to cut his own pay, and visits every family that loses a job.
- **Feels:** Anxious, but held by his faith.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Emmanuel:** I'd say a care home run by machines is a sign we have forgotten our elders. And I'd say it plainly, though some would call me old-fashioned.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Emmanuel:** Now it is everyone. The builders, the farmers, the care workers in my church. These were the people who thought they were safe. I would be burying hope every Sunday. My own work, no robot will take. But a shepherd with no flock is no shepherd.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Emmanuel:** Organise the churches together to press the council and the state for real work programmes. Start visiting the care homes more often. Keep the doors open every day, not just Sunday.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Emmanuel:** Grieved. Steady, but grieved.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A care home run by machines is a sign we have forgotten our elders.”
- **Thinks:** No robot takes his work, but his builders, farmers and care workers lose their safe place, and a shepherd needs a flock.
- **Does:** Unites local churches to press for work programmes, visits care homes more, and opens the church daily.
- **Feels:** Grieved, but steady.
- **Mood:** Anxious
