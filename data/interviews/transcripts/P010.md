# P010 · Samuel Adeyemi

CEO, mid-size oilfield services firm · Mining, oil and gas

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Samuel. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Samuel:** Sure. I'll give you the version I'd say on stage and the version I'd say to my board.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Samuel:** I'd say AI is a productivity tool and we're using it to serve clients better. The standard line.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Samuel:** Honestly, a little disappointing. If everyone gets the same small boost, nobody gains an edge. I want an edge.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Samuel:** Keep rolling out tools in bidding and scheduling. Keep costs tight. Watch what the big players do.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Samuel:** Calm. A bit bored, if anything.

- **Says:** “AI is a productivity tool, and we're using it to serve clients better.”
- **Thinks:** A little disappointing. If everyone gets the same boost, nobody gets an edge.
- **Does:** Rolls out tools in bidding and scheduling, keeps costs tight and watches the big players.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Samuel:** I'd say we're taking a measured approach. Clients like hearing 'measured'.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Samuel:** Slow suits a mid-size firm fine. The danger is a big rival moving faster than everyone else and pricing me out. Ten years is plenty of time to lose.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Samuel:** Hire fewer juniors and let the tools do the routine work. Put real money into automating our bids, because winning tenders is where speed pays.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Samuel:** Focused. Not worried yet.

**Interviewer:** You'd hire fewer juniors yourself. Does that bother you?

**Samuel:** Not really. It's a business. I'd keep the best ones.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're taking a measured approach.”
- **Thinks:** Slow suits us, unless a big rival moves faster and prices me out.
- **Does:** Hires fewer juniors and puts real money into automating bids.
- **Feels:** Focused, not worried yet.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Samuel:** I'd say we're investing in our people and in new roles. Which we would be, a little.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Samuel:** This is good for us if we move first. My back office could run on a third of the staff. I'd cut it, win more bids on price and put the savings back in. The politicians can argue.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Samuel:** Cut the admin, finance and planning teams down over the year. Use the savings to undercut rivals on two or three big tenders.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Samuel:** Good, honestly. This is the opening I've been waiting for.

**Interviewer:** In public you'd say you're investing in your people. In private you'd cut two thirds of the back office. How do those fit?

**Samuel:** We'd invest in the ones who stay. The rest get a fair package. That's how business works.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Samuel:** I'd say the market will sort out who gets the gains, and government should be careful not to kill growth.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Samuel:** My shares and property go up a lot, so personally I'm fine. What worries me is the big service companies automating their engineering faster than me, and the political fight ending in heavy taxes on people like me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Samuel:** Push automation hard across engineering and bidding. Sit down with my advisers about protecting the family's assets.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Samuel:** Uneasy. Not about money. About losing the race.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Samuel:** I'd say robots will make field work safer and more efficient. Every CEO would say that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Samuel:** This is the race I was afraid of. Robots on rigs and in the yard cost a lot up front. The giants can buy whole fleets. I'd have to borrow or partner to keep up, or sell to one of them.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Samuel:** Talk to the robot makers in the first month. Trial a few units on our biggest contract. Quietly sound out a buyer in case we can't keep up.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Samuel:** Wired. Part excited, part worried I'll be too slow.

**Interviewer:** Would selling the company feel like a loss?

**Samuel:** Money-wise, no. I'd be fine. Pride-wise, yes. I built it to win, not to sell.
