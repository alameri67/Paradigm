# P017 · Luis Hernández

Carpenter / framer · Construction

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Luis. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Luis:** Okay, sure. I have a job later, but I have time now.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Luis:** I'd say it's for computers, not for framing. I don't use it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Luis:** Nothing changes for me. What matters is if people keep building houses. If they build, I eat.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Luis:** Same as now. Take every job I can, send money to my family, keep a little for the kids.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Luis:** Normal. Good.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's for computers, not for framing.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes. If people keep building houses, I eat.
- **Does:** Takes every job he can, sends money to his family and keeps a little for the kids.
- **Feels:** Normal. Good.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Luis:** I'd say slow is good, people have time. Same as my work. You learn slow and you get good.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Luis:** I don't think it touches me. Nobody does my work from a computer. Fewer beginners in offices, I don't know, maybe more young guys come to construction.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Luis:** Keep working. Maybe teach the young guy on our crew to frame properly, so he has a trade.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Luis:** Fine. Not worried.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow is good. You learn slow and you get good.”
- **Thinks:** It doesn't touch me. Nobody does my work from a computer.
- **Does:** Keeps working and teaches the young guy on the crew to frame properly.
- **Feels:** Fine. Not worried.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Luis:** I'd say it's hard for those families. Nobody should lose work like that. I'd pray for them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Luis:** My worry is, if office people lose their jobs, they don't buy houses. No houses, no framing. I get paid by the job, so I feel it the same week.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Luis:** Look for steadier work with a bigger builder, not just job by job. Spend less, stop eating out. Keep sending money to my family. That I can't stop.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Luis:** A little worried. Watching how much work comes.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's hard for those families. Nobody should lose work like that.”
- **Thinks:** If office people lose their jobs, they don't buy houses. I get paid by the job, so I feel it the same week.
- **Does:** Looks for steadier work with a bigger builder, cuts spending, and keeps sending money to his family.
- **Feels:** A little worried, watching how much work comes.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Luis:** I'd say the people at the top shouldn't keep everything. Workers build everything. Families need something too.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Luis:** House prices go up, maybe more building, maybe good. But if a lot of people lose jobs, maybe fewer buyers. I don't know which. My work goes up and down with the market.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Luis:** Take every job, weekends too. Try to save one month of money, which is hard with three kids.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Luis:** Worried, but I'm strong. I can work.

**Interviewer:** You said maybe good, maybe bad. Which do you think is more likely?

**Luis:** Honestly, I don't know. I'm not a money person. I watch how often the builder calls me. That tells me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The people at the top shouldn't keep everything. Workers build everything.”
- **Thinks:** Maybe more building, maybe fewer buyers. I don't know which. I watch how often the builder calls.
- **Does:** Takes every job, weekends too, and tries to save one month of money.
- **Feels:** Worried, but strong. He can work.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Luis:** I'd say a robot can't do what my hands do. A robot doesn't know when the wood is bad.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Luis:** But I've seen videos of machines that build walls in a factory. If robots come to the site too, framing is one of the first things. It's repeat work. I have three kids and I send money to my family. There's no cushion.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Luis:** Learn the finish work, trim and cabinets, the fine work that's harder for a machine. Ask the builder straight if he plans to buy robots. Talk with my wife about her finding work too.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Luis:** Scared for the kids. I don't sleep good thinking about it.

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said nobody does your work from a computer. Does that still hold?

**Luis:** From a computer, no. From a robot, maybe yes. That's different.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A robot can't do what my hands do. It doesn't know when the wood is bad.”
- **Thinks:** Framing is repeat work, so it's one of the first things robots take. Three kids, family to support, no cushion.
- **Does:** Learns finish carpentry, asks the builder straight about robots, and talks with his wife about her finding work.
- **Feels:** Scared for the kids. Not sleeping well.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
