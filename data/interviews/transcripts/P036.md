# P036 · Bill Jackson

Long-haul truck driver, owner-operator · Transportation and warehousing

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Bill, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Bill:** Yep. I own my truck, well, the bank owns most of it still. Long haul. I'm calling from a truck stop, so if I go quiet it's the signal.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Bill:** I'd say I don't use it and don't need it. The road doesn't care about search engines.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bill:** Means nothing. I drive, I deliver, I get paid. A computer that answers questions doesn't change that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bill:** Same as now. Run my loads, pay down the truck.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bill:** Fine. Got bigger worries, like diesel prices.

### Summary

- **Says:** “I don't use it and don't need it. The road doesn't care about search engines.”
- **Thinks:** It means nothing. I drive, I deliver, I get paid.
- **Does:** Runs his loads as usual and keeps paying down the truck.
- **Feels:** Fine. Diesel worries him more.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Bill:** I'd say they've been promising driverless trucks for years and I'm still here.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bill:** But it's the one thing I do worry about. You see it on the news, trucks with nobody in them. Slow or not, if that comes in before my loan's paid off, I'm in a hole.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bill:** I'd push harder to pay the truck off early. Take the extra loads, fewer days at home. I'd hate that, and so would my wife.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bill:** Uneasy. It sits in the back of my head on long nights.

**Interviewer:** You'd say they've been promising for years. But you'd still work extra to pay it off. Which is it?

**Bill:** Both. I say it to keep my spirits up. I pay the truck off because I'm not stupid.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They've been promising driverless trucks for years and I'm still here.”
- **Thinks:** If driverless trucks come before my loan's paid off, I'm in a hole. I say they won't to keep my spirits up.
- **Does:** Pushes to pay off the truck early by taking extra loads and spending fewer days at home.
- **Feels:** Uneasy on long nights.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Bill:** I'd say serves 'em right, the office types who said everyone should learn to code. Now look.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bill:** My job's not office work. But the freight brokers and dispatchers, if they're all bots, I don't know if I get a fair rate or worse. Could go either way.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bill:** I'd keep driving and keep paying off the truck. I'd ask the other drivers on the radio which brokers are going to bots and how the pay's working out.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bill:** Uneasy. Not my fight yet, but close.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Serves 'em right, the office types who said everyone should learn to code.”
- **Thinks:** My job isn't office work. If the freight brokers are all bots, I don't know if my rates get better or worse.
- **Does:** Keeps driving, keeps paying off the truck and asks other drivers which brokers have gone to bots and how pay is.
- **Feels:** Uneasy. Not his fight yet, but close.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Bill:** I'd say the country's being run by people who've never done a day's real work. Let them sort out their own mess.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bill:** Physical work's safe here, you said, so maybe I'm all right. But if all those office folks are out of work, they're not buying as much stuff, and less stuff means fewer loads. And half of them will try for a truck licence.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bill:** I'd take every load going while the work's there. Cut back at home. Put off anything that isn't the truck or the kids.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bill:** Uneasy. Freight's always up and down, but this feels bigger.

**Interviewer:** You said maybe you're all right. Is that what you really believe?

**Bill:** For a year or two, yeah. After that it depends how many new drivers come chasing the same loads.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The country's run by people who've never done a day's real work. Let them sort out their own mess.”
- **Thinks:** I might be all right, but fewer office jobs means less freight, and a lot of laid-off people will try for a truck licence.
- **Does:** Takes every load he can, cuts back at home and puts off anything that isn't the truck or the kids.
- **Feels:** Uneasy. Bigger than the usual ups and downs.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Bill:** I'd say this is the end of the working man and nobody in charge cares.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bill:** If they've got robots in warehouses and on farms, driverless trucks are next. They'd load themselves and drive themselves. I'd be the guy with a truck he can't sell, a loan he can't pay, out in the middle of nowhere with nothing else to do.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bill:** I'd try to sell the truck before the price drops, even at a loss, and get out from the loan. Then I don't know. Maybe local delivery, or something on a farm, if there's anything left. My wife would have to pick up more work.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bill:** Scared stiff. Like the road just ends.

**Interviewer:** The card doesn't mention trucks. Why are you sure they'd come?

**Bill:** Because it's the same thing, isn't it? If a robot can run a warehouse, it can drive a straight road. That's what everyone says. I could be wrong, but I wouldn't bet my house on it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is the end of the working man, and nobody in charge cares.”
- **Thinks:** Driverless trucks would be next. I'd be stuck with a truck I can't sell and a loan I can't pay, with nothing else out here.
- **Does:** Tries to sell the truck before prices fall, even at a loss, then looks for local delivery or farm work while his wife takes on more work.
- **Feels:** Scared stiff, like the road just ends.
- **Mood:** Panicked
