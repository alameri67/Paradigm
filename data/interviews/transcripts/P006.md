# P006 · Gareth Owens

Underground mine shift supervisor · Mining, oil and gas

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Gareth. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Gareth:** Right. Fire away. I've got about forty minutes before I head in.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gareth:** I'd say we've had smart kit on site for years. The haul trucks drive themselves already. A better search engine won't change much underground.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gareth:** Nothing much for me. My crew still needs someone watching the ground and the gas readings. That's me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gareth:** Same as now. Run my shift, keep an eye on the new starters, chip away at the mortgage.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gareth:** Fine. Normal.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gareth:** Slow is how mining goes anyway. Every bit of new kit takes years to bed in.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gareth:** Fewer beginners worries me for the crew, not me. We already took on fewer drivers when the trucks went driverless. The young ones in town don't have much else.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gareth:** Keep pushing management to hold on to the apprentice spots. Learn the planning software better, so I'm useful in the control room as well as underground.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gareth:** Steady. A bit of a nag about the young ones.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow is how mining goes. New kit always takes years to bed in.”
- **Thinks:** I'm fine. It's the young ones in town I worry about, with fewer jobs for new starters.
- **Does:** Pushes management to keep apprentice places, and learns the planning software so he's useful in the control room too.
- **Feels:** Steady, with a nag about the young ones.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gareth:** I'd say they should've seen it coming. We've lived with automation for years and nobody in an office cared.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gareth:** If the office side gets cut, that's the planners and the admin people on site. Some of them are mates. Doesn't change my job much, but the place gets thinner.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gareth:** Not much. Do my shifts. Put a bit more aside, because three kids eat a lot.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gareth:** A bit uneasy for the mates in the office. I'm all right.

**Interviewer:** You said nobody cared when it hit you. Do you feel any of that now it's them?

**Gareth:** Bit of it, yeah. Then I think of the blokes from the site office who buy a round on a Friday, and it goes away.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They should've seen it coming. We've lived with automation for years and nobody in an office cared.”
- **Thinks:** My job's fine, but some of the office people are mates, and the place gets thinner.
- **Does:** Carries on with his shifts and puts a bit more aside for the family.
- **Feels:** A bit uneasy for his mates in the office.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gareth:** I'd say good luck replacing a miner with a chatbot. Physical work is what keeps the lights on.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gareth:** In this one we're the safe ones, for now. But I've seen the trucks. I know they're working on the rest of the underground jobs. If the office goes in two years, the company has money to spend on robots next.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gareth:** Pay the mortgage down hard while the money's good. Take overtime when it's there. Put the new kitchen off.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gareth:** Uneasy. Like weather coming in.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Gareth:** I'd say a robot can't feel the ground move. I'd say it loud, and I'd be half right.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Gareth:** Truth is, underground is exactly where they want robots. It's dangerous and it's costly to keep people down there. My crew is the first thing they'd cut, and you don't need a shift supervisor for robots. Nothing else in this town pays like the mine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Gareth:** Sit the crew down and tell them straight what I know. Get on any course for running remote kit from the surface. Talk to my wife about whether we'd move, which with three kids at school is the last thing either of us wants.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Gareth:** Gutted. Lying awake about my crew, and about the mortgage.

**Interviewer:** You've said you worry about the crew more than yourself. Is that still true here?

**Gareth:** Mostly. But I've got three kids and a mortgage, so no, it's not just the crew this time.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A robot can't feel the ground move.”
- **Thinks:** Underground is exactly where they want robots. My crew goes first, then me, and nothing else in town pays like the mine.
- **Does:** Tells his crew straight what he knows, gets on any course for running remote kit from the surface, and talks with his wife about moving.
- **Feels:** Gutted. Lying awake about the crew and the mortgage.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
