# P044 · Mei Lin Zhao

Data centre technician · Information

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Mei Lin, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Mei:** Okay. I'm a technician at a data centre. I swap out parts, run cables, keep the servers running. I'm not much of a talker, but I'll answer.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mei:** I'd say it's useful. I use it to look up error codes sometimes.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mei:** Not much changes. We'd still be building more server halls, just maybe not as fast.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mei:** Keep doing my job. Maybe finish the next certification on power and cooling.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mei:** Fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's useful. I use it to look up error codes sometimes.”
- **Thinks:** Not much changes. We'd still be building server halls, just more slowly.
- **Does:** Keeps working and finishes her next certification on power and cooling.
- **Feels:** Fine.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mei:** I'd say it's hard on new graduates. Not much more than that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mei:** For me it's good. Slow or fast, AI runs on hardware, and hardware needs people like me to plug it in. The company's been hiring technicians all year.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mei:** Same. Get the certification, ask about a senior tech role in a year or two.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mei:** Calm.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's hard on new graduates.”
- **Thinks:** AI runs on hardware and hardware needs people like me. We've been hiring technicians all year.
- **Does:** Gets her certification and plans to ask about a senior technician role in a year or two.
- **Feels:** Calm.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mei:** I'd say I feel for office workers. It's rough. I'd probably keep my opinions to myself at work, though.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mei:** Honestly, this is good for me. More AI means more data centres, more overtime, maybe better pay. There are a few sites around town and they all want staff.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mei:** I'd take the overtime, ask for a raise, and maybe help my husband look at what he could retrain in if he needed to.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mei:** Calm. A little guilty, maybe, that it's good for me.

**Interviewer:** Guilty how?

**Mei:** Just, other people are losing their jobs because of the thing I keep running. I don't think about it too hard.

### Summary

- **Says:** “I feel for office workers. It's rough.”
- **Thinks:** This is good for me: more data centres, more overtime, maybe better pay. A bit guilty that I keep the thing running that costs others their jobs.
- **Does:** Takes the overtime, asks for a raise and helps her husband think about retraining if needed.
- **Feels:** Calm, a little guilty.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mei:** I'd say the government should figure out how to share it out. I don't follow politics closely.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mei:** My job is safe and probably busier than ever. Physical work is untouched here, and data centres would be everywhere. I'd be fine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mei:** Lots of overtime. Put more into savings while it's good. Maybe look at a second certification so I could move up to supervisor.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mei:** Calm. Busy.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government should figure out how to share it out. I don't follow politics closely.”
- **Thinks:** My job is safe and busier than ever. Data centres would be everywhere.
- **Does:** Works lots of overtime, saves more and studies for a second certification to move up to supervisor.
- **Feels:** Calm and busy.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mei:** I'd say it's a lot of change at once. People are scared, I get it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mei:** This is the first one that touches me. A lot of my work is swapping drives and parts in racks, and that's exactly the kind of thing a robot could learn. I'd still have work, I think, fixing the robots or the tricky problems. But fewer of us.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mei:** I'd ask my manager straight out what the plan is for robots on the floor. I'd try to become the person who looks after the robots, not the one they replace. And I'd keep saving.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mei:** Uneasy. First time I've felt it.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd still have work. How sure are you?

**Mei:** Maybe seventy percent. Somebody has to fix things when they go wrong. But I'm not the best tech on the team, so I'd want to move fast.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a lot of change at once. People are scared, I get it.”
- **Thinks:** Swapping drives and parts in racks is exactly what a robot could learn. I'd probably still have work fixing robots and tricky problems, but there'd be fewer of us.
- **Does:** Asks her manager about plans for robots on the floor, aims to become the one who looks after them and keeps saving.
- **Feels:** Uneasy for the first time.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
