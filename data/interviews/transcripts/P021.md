# P021 · Frank Müller

CNC machinist · Manufacturing

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Frank. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Frank:** Fine. I'm not one for long answers.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Frank:** Nothing. Not my business.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Frank:** Doesn't change my machines. I've seen two waves come through this shop. This isn't one.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Frank:** Same as every year. Run my parts, teach the young fella his setups, count down to retirement.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Frank:** Calm.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Nothing. Not my business.”
- **Thinks:** Doesn't change my machines. I've seen two waves, and this isn't one.
- **Does:** Runs his parts, teaches the young machinist setups and counts down to retirement.
- **Feels:** Calm.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Frank:** Slow suits me fine.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Frank:** Ten years, I'm long gone by then. Hiring fewer beginners has been true in this shop for years already. Hard to find young people who want to stand at a machine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Frank:** Nothing different. Maybe write down some of my tricks for the next guy.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Frank:** Fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow suits me fine.”
- **Thinks:** I'll be gone in ten years. Fewer beginners has been true here for years anyway.
- **Does:** Carries on and starts writing down his tricks for the next machinist.
- **Feels:** Fine.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Frank:** I'd say office people are learning what we learned in the nineties. Nobody stopped it for us either.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Frank:** Doesn't touch the shop floor. Maybe the programmer upstairs gets help writing programs. Fine.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Frank:** Keep my head down. Same as always.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Frank:** Not my worry.

**Interviewer:** You sound a little bitter about that. Is that fair?

**Frank:** Maybe a bit. I watched good men lose their jobs and nobody made speeches for them.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Office people are learning what we learned in the nineties. Nobody stopped it for us either.”
- **Thinks:** It doesn't touch the shop floor.
- **Does:** Keeps his head down, same as always.
- **Feels:** Not his worry. A bit bitter.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Frank:** I'd say maybe people will respect a trade now. Don't need to say much more.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Frank:** Physical work is safe here. But if AI writes the machine programs and plans the jobs, the shop needs fewer skilled people for setups. I'm the setup man. Still, I'd likely make it to retirement.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Frank:** Pay off what's left. Keep the savings where they are. Maybe ask about going part-time at 62.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Frank:** A little uneasy. Not much.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Maybe people will respect a trade now.”
- **Thinks:** If AI writes the programs, the shop needs fewer setup men like me. I'd likely still make it to retirement.
- **Does:** Pays off remaining debts, keeps his savings put and asks about going part-time at 62.
- **Feels:** A little uneasy. Not much.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Frank:** I'd say robots have been in the shop for years. These are just smarter ones.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Frank:** This is the third wave, and it's the one I hoped to miss. A robot that can load, set up and check parts doesn't need me. In four or five years I'm 62 or 63. Close, but maybe not close enough. And there's nothing else in this town for a machinist.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Frank:** Talk to my wife about money if I have to go early. Ask the boss straight what he's planning. Offer to train whoever runs the robots, so I'm useful to the end.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Frank:** Worried. First time in a long time.

**Interviewer:** You've lived through two waves already. Is this one different?

**Frank:** The first two took the boring jobs. This one takes mine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots have been in the shop for years. These are just smarter ones.”
- **Thinks:** This is the wave I hoped to miss. A robot that loads, sets up and checks parts doesn't need me, and I might not reach retirement.
- **Does:** Talks to his wife about going early, asks the boss straight about his plans, and offers to train the robot operators.
- **Feels:** Worried, for the first time in a long time.
- **Mood:** Anxious
