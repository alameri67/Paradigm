# P022 · Rosa Martínez

Assembly line worker, electronics · Manufacturing

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Rosa. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Rosa:** Okay. I don't know if I'll be any help, but I'll tell you how it is.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rosa:** I'd say they always call it just a tool, and then they cut a shift. That's what happened last time with the robots. I'd say it to anyone.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rosa:** Even if AI stays small, the robots keep coming to our line anyway. The company doesn't need some big new thing to cut us. They already did it once.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rosa:** Keep my shift. Never be late, never complain to the wrong person. Take extra hours when they offer. Try to keep a little for the kids' school things.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rosa:** Already scared. That doesn't change.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They always call it just a tool, and then they cut a shift.”
- **Thinks:** The robots keep coming to our line anyway. They already cut us once.
- **Does:** Guards her shift, takes extra hours when offered, and saves a little for the kids' school costs.
- **Feels:** Already scared. That doesn't change.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rosa:** I'd say slow for who? For us it's already here. And the plant stopped hiring young people years ago.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rosa:** Slow doesn't help me. Every time a new machine comes, they cut more people. I'm 41, I'm not the fastest, and I have nothing saved.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rosa:** Talk to the others on the line about getting organised. Look at the job board in town, even though it's mostly empty.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rosa:** Tired of being scared all the time.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow for who? For us it's already here.”
- **Thinks:** Every new machine means more cuts. I'm 41, not the fastest, and I have nothing saved.
- **Does:** Talks to others on the line about getting organised and checks the town's thin job board.
- **Feels:** Tired of being scared all the time.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rosa:** I'd say now the office people see how it feels. And the bosses get richer either way. The money from these machines should go to the workers, not the owners.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rosa:** If the office side gets cut, the managers get even more pressure to cut costs. That pressure comes down on the line. It always comes down on us.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rosa:** Keep my head down at work. Go to any meeting about a union or workers' rights. Find someone to help with the kids if I need more shifts.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rosa:** Angry. And still scared.

**Interviewer:** You'd keep your head down and also go to union meetings. Can you do both?

**Rosa:** I have to. Quiet at work, loud at the meeting. I can't lose this job.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The money from these machines should go to the workers, not the owners.”
- **Thinks:** Office cuts mean more pressure on managers, and that pressure always comes down on the line.
- **Does:** Stays quiet at work, goes to union and workers' rights meetings, and lines up childcare for extra shifts.
- **Feels:** Angry, and still scared.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rosa:** I'd say the rich got richer while everyone else lost. Take the money from the share boom and give it to people.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rosa:** For once it's not us losing our jobs first. But I don't believe it lasts. The company that cut our shift for robots will do it again the first chance it gets. And rents go up with the boom.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rosa:** Save anything I can while I still have the job. Look for a cheaper place. Push my kids to study something machines can't do, if there's anything.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rosa:** Scared, and a bit of 'I told you so'.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The rich got richer while everyone else lost. Take the money from the boom and give it to people.”
- **Thinks:** For once it's not us first, but it won't last. And rents go up with the boom.
- **Does:** Saves what she can, looks for a cheaper place and pushes her kids toward work machines can't do.
- **Feels:** Scared, with a bit of 'I told you so'.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rosa:** I'd say it's a crime. They take everything from people who work with their hands and give it to the people who own the machines.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rosa:** That's the end for me. The robots already took one shift. This takes the rest. There's no other factory in this town. I have two kids and nothing saved.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rosa:** I don't know. Ask for any help that exists. Food, rent, anything. Look for cleaning or care work, if robots don't take that too. Maybe move to a city, but how, with two kids and no money?

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rosa:** Like I'm drowning. Like nobody's coming.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a crime. They take everything from people who work with their hands.”
- **Thinks:** That's the end for me. No other factory in town, two kids and nothing saved.
- **Does:** Asks for any food or rent help going, looks for cleaning or care work, and can't see how she'd afford to move.
- **Feels:** Like drowning, with nobody coming.
- **Mood:** Panicked
