# P097 · Mark Davies

Military logistics officer · Government

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Mark, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Mark:** Understood. I'll speak for myself, not for the service. That's the only way I can do this.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mark:** I'd say we should be using these tools faster than we are. Our rivals certainly are.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mark:** If it stays a tool, that's fine for me. It helps with planning and paperwork. My worry is we fall behind other countries because we treated it as a toy.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mark:** Push my unit to adopt the planning tools we've been trialling. Get my people trained on them.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mark:** Calm. A little impatient.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We should be using these tools faster than we are. Our rivals certainly are.”
- **Thinks:** A tool is fine for him; the danger is falling behind other countries by treating it as a toy.
- **Does:** Pushes his unit to adopt trialled planning tools and trains his people on them.
- **Feels:** Calm, a little impatient.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mark:** I'd say this is a national-security race and slow is dangerous. We can't wait ten years while others don't.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mark:** For me personally, slow is comfortable. Logistics planning gets better, I keep my post. But professionally, slow worries me more than fast. It means someone else gets there first.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mark:** Write a paper up the chain arguing for faster adoption in supply planning. Take whatever AI courses the service offers.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mark:** Calm about me. Frustrated about the country.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is a national-security race and slow is dangerous.”
- **Thinks:** Slow is comfortable personally, but professionally it means another country gets there first.
- **Does:** Writes a paper urging faster AI adoption in supply planning and takes the service's AI courses.
- **Feels:** Calm about himself, frustrated for the country.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mark:** I'd say we need to win the race and look after our people at home. Retraining, apprenticeships, not just handouts.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mark:** The military is one of the last places to cut people, and logistics officers who understand AI would be in demand. I think I'm well placed. My plan was to go into civilian logistics after I leave the service, and I'd say that still looks good.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mark:** Volunteer to lead the AI planning pilot. Keep building the savings. Look at what civilian certificates would help me later.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mark:** Calm. Confident, even.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Win the race and look after our people at home: retraining and apprenticeships, not just handouts.”
- **Thinks:** The military is slow to cut people and AI-literate logistics officers would be in demand; his planned civilian logistics career still looks good.
- **Does:** Volunteers to lead the AI planning pilot, keeps saving, and researches useful civilian certificates.
- **Feels:** Calm, even confident.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mark:** I'd say the country must stay united and strong. A political crisis at home is exactly what our rivals want.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mark:** Between us, this is where I'd start to worry. Not about my job, but about order at home. Unrest, angry people, troops being asked to do things at home that we shouldn't. And my three kids' futures. I don't know what to tell them to study.

**Interviewer:** You said troops being asked to do things they shouldn't. What do you mean?

**Mark:** Policing their own people. If things get bad enough, someone will ask. I'd rather not say more than that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mark:** Keep doing my job well. Talk with my wife about the kids' plans, maybe steer them toward trades or service. Keep our savings in safe places, not chase the stock boom.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mark:** Uneasy. Steady, but watching.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The country must stay united and strong. A political crisis at home is what our rivals want.”
- **Thinks:** He worries less about his job than about unrest at home, troops being misused, and what his three kids should study.
- **Does:** Keeps doing his job well, discusses steering the kids toward trades or service, and keeps savings safe instead of chasing the boom.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, steady but watching.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mark:** I'd say the country that masters robots in logistics wins the next war. We need to lead, and we need a plan for the workers left behind.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mark:** Robots in warehouses is my world. Military logistics would automate fast. My post might be fine, but that civilian logistics career I'd planned on, that's the warehouse business. It wouldn't be there in the same way.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mark:** Rethink the second career. Maybe stay in longer than I'd planned. Push for national apprenticeship schemes in the briefings I write. Tell my kids that trades aren't safe either, which I hate saying.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mark:** Uneasy. My plan has a gap I didn't expect.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The country that masters robots in logistics wins the next war. We need to lead, and plan for workers left behind.”
- **Thinks:** His post may hold, but the civilian logistics career he planned would shrink as warehouses automate.
- **Does:** Rethinks his second career, considers serving longer, pushes for national apprenticeships, and tells his kids trades aren't safe either.
- **Feels:** Uneasy about an unexpected gap in his plan.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
