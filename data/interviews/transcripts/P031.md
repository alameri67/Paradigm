# P031 · Linda Foster

Supermarket cashier, long tenure · Retail trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Linda, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Linda:** Hello, love. I've been on the tills at the supermarket a long time. These days they've got me minding the self-checkouts. I don't know much about computers, mind.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Linda:** I'd say it's nothing to do with me. I don't use it. I'd leave it to the young ones.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Linda:** It doesn't touch me, this AI. What touches me is those self-checkout machines. They'll keep putting more in whatever happens.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Linda:** Keep my head down and work my shifts. Try to stay on the tills as long as there are tills.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Linda:** A bit of a worry, but that's not new. It's always there.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's nothing to do with me. I leave it to the young ones.”
- **Thinks:** AI doesn't touch me. It's the self-checkout machines I worry about, and they'll keep coming either way.
- **Does:** Keeps her head down, works her shifts and stays on the tills as long as there are tills.
- **Feels:** The usual low worry in the background.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Linda:** I'd say it's a shame they won't take on young people. That's how I started, at sixteen.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Linda:** I worry for my grandson, more than me. If shops and offices won't hire beginners, where does a lad like him start?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Linda:** Nothing different for me. I'd tell him to work hard at school and maybe look at a trade.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Linda:** Uneasy. For him, mostly.

**Interviewer:** Would a slow change like that reach your own job at all?

**Linda:** Not the computer side, I don't think. It's the machines at the front of the shop I watch. They come at their own pace.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a shame they won't take on young people. I started at sixteen.”
- **Thinks:** I worry more for my grandson than for me. Where would a lad like him start?
- **Does:** Carries on as normal and tells her grandson to work hard and look at a trade.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, mostly for my grandson.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Linda:** I'd say it's the office folk getting it this time. I feel sorry for them, I do.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Linda:** It's not my job they're after, is it? Nobody's putting a computer program on my till. Though if lots of people lose jobs, they'll come looking for shop work too.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Linda:** I'd keep doing every shift going and be extra good, never late, so they'd keep me over someone new. I'd stop buying anything we don't need.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Linda:** Uneasy. Watching the door, like.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's the office folk getting it this time. I feel sorry for them.”
- **Thinks:** My till isn't the target, but people who lose jobs will come looking for shop work.
- **Does:** Takes every shift, stays spotless on attendance so she's kept over newcomers, and stops buying extras.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, watching the door.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Linda:** I'd say it's not right. People worked hard for those jobs and now they're out. Somebody ought to look after them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Linda:** Funny thing is, people like me are safe in this one, you said. But I don't own a house or shares, so the boom does nothing for me. Prices go up and my wage doesn't.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Linda:** Same as before. Every shift, keep my head down, cut back on food and the heating where I can. I'd make sure my grandson's school things are covered first.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Linda:** Uneasy, and a bit bitter. It's always the same people who do well.

**Interviewer:** You said you're safe here, but you sound worried. Which is it?

**Linda:** My job's safe, I suppose. My money's not. Those are two different things when you're on what I'm on.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's not right. People worked hard for those jobs. Somebody ought to look after them.”
- **Thinks:** My job's safe but my money's not. I don't own a house or shares, so the boom just pushes my costs up.
- **Does:** Takes every shift, cuts back on food and heating, and covers her grandson's school things first.
- **Feels:** Uneasy and a bit bitter.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Linda:** I'd say they've taken everything now. What's left for people like me?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Linda:** That's me finished. If robots can stack shelves and they've already got the self-checkouts, they won't need me at all. There's nothing else in this town. I'm 54. Who'll hire me?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Linda:** I'd ask the manager straight out how long I've got. I'd go to the council about benefits before I need them, and look for somewhere cheaper for me and the boy.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Linda:** Frightened. I can't see a way through it. I've got the boy to think of.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd go to the council early. Do you trust they'd help?

**Linda:** Not really. But what else is there? I've never asked anyone for anything. I'd have to now.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They've taken everything now. What's left for people like me?”
- **Thinks:** With robots on the shelves and self-checkouts at the front, that's me finished. There's nothing else in this town for a woman of 54.
- **Does:** Asks her manager how long she has, goes to the council about benefits early and looks for a cheaper home for her and her grandson.
- **Feels:** Frightened, no way through, with the boy to raise.
- **Mood:** Panicked
