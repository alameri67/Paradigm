**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**David:** I'd say the market's doing what markets do. Fewer analysts, leaner teams. Nobody's owed a seat.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**David:** The bit that worries me is the pyramid. Fewer analysts below me means fewer associates needed, which means fewer of us get to VP. The competition for those slots was already brutal.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**David:** I'd work on my client relationships now, not later. Get in front of the managing directors' clients, take the senior bankers to lunch, make myself someone who brings in deals, not just models them.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**David:** Uneasy. A competitive itch.

### Summary

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Interviewer:** You say let people adapt. Would you want any help yourself if you were cut?

**David:** No. I'd figure it out. That's the deal I signed up for. Ask me again if it actually happens, though.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**David:** I'd say the boom proves markets work. Redistribution schemes will kill the golden goose.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**David:** Privately, this is bad for me. Deals would be booming, but done by AI and a few senior people with client relationships. I'm 34, not senior enough to own clients, too expensive to be a junior. I'm exactly the layer that goes. And I've got almost nothing saved, because I spent like I'd be a VP.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**David:** I'd slash spending right away and move to a cheap place. I'd try to get to a fund or a startup with equity, so I own a piece of the boom instead of being paid by it. And I'd put what I can into the market while it's running.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**David:** Alarmed. I'd feel stupid for spending the way I did.

**Interviewer:** You'd argue against redistribution, but you might be one of the people who needs it. Does that bother you?

**David:** A bit. I still think it's the wrong fix. But I'd notice the irony every time I checked my bank balance.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The boom proves markets work. Redistribution schemes will kill the golden goose.”
- **Thinks:** Deals would boom, done by AI and a few seniors with clients. I'm exactly the middle layer that goes, with almost nothing saved.
- **Does:** Slashes spending and moves somewhere cheap, chases a role with equity at a fund or startup, and invests what he can.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, and stupid for how I spent.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
