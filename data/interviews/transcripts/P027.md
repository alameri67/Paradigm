# P027 · Fatima Zahra Benali

Purchasing and inventory analyst · Wholesale trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Fatima, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Fatima:** Of course. I work on purchasing and stock levels for a distributor. I should say I'm expecting my first baby, so the future is on my mind a lot anyway.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fatima:** I'd say it's useful. The new forecasting tool already does in minutes what took me a day in spreadsheets.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fatima:** It means I get to do the more interesting part of my job. Less copying numbers, more asking why the numbers look strange.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fatima:** I'd ask my manager to put me in charge of setting up the forecasting tool properly, and I'd take an online course on it before the baby comes.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fatima:** Good, actually. Calm and a bit excited.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's useful. The forecasting tool does in minutes what took her a day.”
- **Thinks:** She gets to do the interesting part of the job: asking why the numbers look odd.
- **Does:** Asks to own the setup of the forecasting tool and takes an online course before the baby arrives.
- **Feels:** Calm and a little excited.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fatima:** I'd say we should use it carefully and train people, not just cut the junior roles. Someone has to learn the basics somewhere.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fatima:** For me it's probably fine. I'm already the one people come to when the tool gives a weird answer. But I feel for the new graduates. I was one of them not long ago.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fatima:** I'd offer to mentor the one junior we still have and write down how I check the forecasts, so the team keeps that knowledge. And I'd keep learning the tool.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fatima:** Mostly settled. A small worry for the people starting out.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Use it carefully and keep training people. Juniors have to learn somewhere.”
- **Thinks:** She's well placed because she's the one who checks the tool, but new graduates will struggle.
- **Does:** Mentors the remaining junior and writes down how she checks forecasts, while she keeps learning the tool.
- **Feels:** Settled, with a small worry for beginners.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fatima:** I'd say companies owe their staff a fair chance to move into the new roles, and that leave and job protection for parents matter more than ever.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fatima:** Privately, the timing scares me. If the cuts come while I'm on maternity leave, I'm not there to show what I do. Out of sight is out of mind.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fatima:** I'd get my name on the AI project in writing before I go on leave. I'd also build up our savings, maybe skip the bigger flat we were looking at, and keep in touch with the team while I'm off.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fatima:** Anxious. It's the baby and the job worries landing at the same time.

**Interviewer:** You said you're the one people come to. Why would that not protect you?

**Fatima:** Because if I'm away for months, they'll find a way without me. I'm good at my job, but I'm not irreplaceable on paper.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Firms owe staff a fair shot at the new roles, and parents need leave and job protection.”
- **Thinks:** If cuts come while she's on maternity leave, nobody will see her value. Being good isn't the same as being irreplaceable.
- **Does:** Gets her role on the AI project in writing before leave, builds savings, skips the bigger flat and stays in touch while off.
- **Feels:** Anxious, with the baby and job fears landing together.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fatima:** I'd say the gains have to be shared. If machines do the work, the profits shouldn't all go to shareholders. Families need something steady to rely on.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fatima:** Honestly, if the AI can do what a skilled analyst does, then running the tools isn't a job for ten people. It's a job for one, maybe. I'd hope it's me, but I can't count on it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fatima:** I'd apply for the few roles that oversee the systems, in my company and others. The city has a lot of firms, so I'd cast wide. I'd also talk to my husband about which of our costs we could cut if one of us lost work.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fatima:** Alarmed. I'm thinking about the baby every time I picture it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains must be shared. Families need something steady to rely on.”
- **Thinks:** If AI matches skilled analysts, running the tools is a one-person job. She hopes it's her but can't count on it.
- **Does:** Applies widely for system oversight roles across the city and plans with her husband which costs to cut.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, thinking of the baby each time.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fatima:** Same as before. Share the gains, protect families, and don't let a few companies take everything.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fatima:** The robots don't change much for me directly. My work was already the one under threat. But it does mean there's nowhere safe to tell people to go, and that makes the whole country feel shakier.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fatima:** Same plan as the last one, just more urgent. I'd keep applying, keep savings high and try to learn how warehouse robots feed data into stock systems, since that's close to what I know.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fatima:** Still alarmed. Not much worse, but heavier somehow.

**Interviewer:** You said it doesn't change much for you, but it feels heavier. Why?

**Fatima:** Because in the last picture I could tell myself, worst case, there's other work out there. Here, that's gone for everyone.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Share the gains, protect families and don't let a few firms take everything.”
- **Thinks:** Robots don't hit her job directly, but there's nowhere safe left for anyone to go, so it feels heavier.
- **Does:** Keeps applying, keeps savings high and learns how warehouse robots feed data into stock systems.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, and heavier with no fallback anywhere.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
