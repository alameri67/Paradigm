# P076 · Rajesh Kumar

Radiologist · Health care and social assistance

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Rajesh, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Rajesh:** Happy to. I read scans with AI every day, so I've thought about this more than I'd like to admit.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rajesh:** I'd say what I say now. The AI flags things, I check them, and the patient gets a better read. It's a good second pair of eyes.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rajesh:** Honestly, that's the world I'd be most comfortable in. My work gets a bit faster and nobody's asking whether we still need radiologists.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rajesh:** Nothing dramatic. I'd keep using the tools, maybe push our department to try the newer chest and stroke models. Same job, same hours.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rajesh:** Relaxed. A bit like being told the storm went round us.

### Summary

- **Says:** “AI is a good second pair of eyes. It makes our reads better, not us less needed.”
- **Thinks:** This is the world he'd be happiest in: faster work and no question about his job.
- **Does:** Keeps using AI reads and pushes his department to trial newer models.
- **Feels:** Relaxed, like the storm went round him.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rajesh:** I'd say the radiologist who uses AI will replace the one who doesn't. That line gets a lot of nods at conferences.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rajesh:** Ten years is long enough for me. I'm 47. The people I worry about are the trainees, because if hospitals hire fewer beginners, who checks the machine in twenty years?

**Interviewer:** You said the trainees worry you. Does any of that worry land on you?

**Rajesh:** Not really, if I'm honest. By the time it matters I'll be near the end. That's a slightly selfish thing to say, isn't it?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rajesh:** I'd keep doing what I do, and I'd spend more time teaching the residents to question the AI rather than trust it. That's the skill that'll matter.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rajesh:** Mostly fine for me. A small worry for the young ones.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The radiologist who uses AI will replace the one who doesn't.”
- **Thinks:** Ten years is long enough for him at 47. He worries more about trainees, since someone still has to check the machine later.
- **Does:** Keeps working as usual and spends more time teaching residents to question the AI.
- **Feels:** Fine for himself, a small worry for trainees.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rajesh:** I'd say medicine isn't routine office work. Our reports carry legal weight and someone has to sign them and answer for them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rajesh:** Privately, the routine reads are a big part of what we bill. If the admin side gets automated, the managers will start asking why the reading side can't be. I don't love that question.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rajesh:** I'd talk to our financial adviser and make sure the kids' university money is set aside. And I'd start doing more of the hands-on procedures, the biopsies and drains, because a model can't do those.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rajesh:** A small knot in the stomach. Not fear, just a nag.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Medicine isn't routine office work. Someone has to sign the report and answer for it.”
- **Thinks:** The routine reads are a big part of what he bills, and managers will start asking why those can't be automated too.
- **Does:** Checks with his financial adviser that the kids' university money is set aside and shifts toward hands-on procedures.
- **Feels:** A small nag in the stomach, not real fear.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rajesh:** Publicly I'd say AI will make imaging cheaper and faster for patients, and radiologists will move to oversight and procedures. That's the professional answer.

**Interviewer:** That sounds like a careful answer. Is it what you believe?

**Rajesh:** Half of it. The patients part I believe. The part where we all neatly move to oversight, I'm less sure. There won't be enough oversight jobs for all of us.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rajesh:** Off the record, that headline I've been hearing for ten years would finally be true for most of the reading. I think I'd be one of the last to go, because hospitals still want a human name on the report. But the job I trained for would be gone, even if I'm not.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rajesh:** I'd go hard into interventional work, maybe a short fellowship if they'd take me. The money side is fine, our shares and the house would be up. I'd also sit down with my kids and talk honestly about what to study.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rajesh:** Strange. Financially safe, but like losing part of who I am.

### Summary

- **Says:** “AI will make imaging cheaper and faster for patients, and radiologists will move into oversight and procedures.”
- **Thinks:** The reading job he trained for would mostly go, though he expects to be one of the last, since hospitals still want a human name on the report.
- **Does:** Pushes into interventional work, possibly a short fellowship, and talks honestly with his kids about what to study.
- **Feels:** Financially safe, but losing part of who he is.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Rajesh:** I'd say the same as before about my own field. The robots don't change the reading room, the AI already did that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Rajesh:** For me it doesn't add much. I don't lift patients or cook. If anything my investments would do well. What it changes is the people around me, the porters and the techs. I'd feel for them.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Rajesh:** Same plan as the last one. I'd also probably give more to the hospital hardship fund, because the staff who push the beds would be the ones in trouble.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Rajesh:** About the same as before. A bit guilty that I'm fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The robots don't change the reading room. The AI already did that.”
- **Thinks:** Robots add little for him, and his investments would do well. The real change is for porters and techs around him.
- **Does:** Sticks to the same plan and gives more to the hospital staff hardship fund.
- **Feels:** Much the same, with a little guilt at being fine.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
