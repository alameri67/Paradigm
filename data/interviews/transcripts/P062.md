# P062 · Laura Bennett

Corporate strategy manager · Management of companies

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Laura. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Laura:** Great, I'm glad you're doing this. I spend all day writing about it, so it's nice to be asked how I feel for once.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Laura:** I'd say it's a solid productivity gain and we should roll it out widely and sensibly. That's basically page one of my strategy.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Laura:** If it stops at a better search engine, my strategy is a bit over the top. Fine by me. I'd rather over-plan than under-plan.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Laura:** Get every team using the tools for drafting and research. Track the time saved so the board sees real results.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Laura:** Calm. A bit relieved, actually.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a solid productivity gain, and we should roll it out widely and sensibly.”
- **Thinks:** If it stops here, her strategy is a bit over the top, and she's fine with that.
- **Does:** Gets every team using the tools and tracks time saved for the board.
- **Feels:** Calm and a bit relieved.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Laura:** I'd say we have time to do this well, and we should use it to reskill people, not just cut.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Laura:** This is the version where I look good. Slow change, steady savings, a strategy that works. The fewer-beginners part means our talent pipeline gets thin, so I'd flag that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Laura:** Build a reskilling programme into the plan and fight for its budget. Set up a small graduate scheme that's mostly about learning to work with AI.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Laura:** Good. Confident, even.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We have time to do this well, and we should reskill people, not just cut.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change makes her strategy look good, but the talent pipeline thins out.
- **Does:** Builds a reskilling programme into the plan and sets up a small AI-focused graduate scheme.
- **Feels:** Good, even confident.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Laura:** Publicly: this is a once-in-a-generation chance to redesign how we work, and we'll bring our people with us.

**Interviewer:** You said 'bring our people with us'. Do you believe that, from what you've seen in the numbers?

**Laura:** Some of them. Not all. I believe it more than the CFO does, put it that way.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Laura:** I know what the numbers say, because I wrote them. The biggest savings are in middle management, the layer that checks and passes on information. That's me, if I'm honest. Strategy managers are on that list.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Laura:** Make sure I'm the one presenting the plan to the board, so I'm seen as the architect, not the target. Start talking to headhunters quietly. Keep saving the way we already do.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Laura:** A tight knot. I'm drawing the map that might have my name on it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is a once-in-a-generation chance to redesign how we work, and we'll bring our people with us.”
- **Thinks:** She wrote the numbers. The biggest savings are in middle management, and strategy managers are on that list.
- **Does:** Presents the plan to the board herself so she's seen as the architect, and quietly talks to headhunters.
- **Feels:** A tight knot. She's drawing the map that may have her name on it.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Laura:** I'd say companies must share the gains with the people who built them, through training, payouts and, honestly, some public policy. I'd say that on a panel.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Laura:** If AI matches skilled professionals, it can write my strategy. The executives above me keep their seats because the board likes faces. The layer I'm in mostly goes. My savings would carry us a while, and my husband works, but I'd be out.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Laura:** Push to move up into a real executive role fast, or out to a smaller company that needs someone to lead this. Pay down the mortgage with the share gains. Talk to the kids about what's happening, gently.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Laura:** Anxious. Still in control, but only just.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Companies must share the gains through training, payouts and some public policy.”
- **Thinks:** AI could write her strategy. Executives keep their seats, but her layer mostly goes.
- **Does:** Pushes for an executive role or a lead role at a smaller firm, pays down the mortgage and talks gently with her children.
- **Feels:** Anxious. In control, but only just.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Laura:** I'd say this needs a national plan, not company by company. No one firm can fix it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Laura:** Personally, the robots don't change my picture. I'm in an office. But it does mean there's no simple story I can tell my kids about where to aim. And our company would have to redo the whole strategy.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Laura:** Same as the fast version. Plus I'd volunteer for the group robotics plan, because it's new and no one owns it yet.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Laura:** Same worry. Plus a strange buzz from the new problem.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This needs a national plan. No one company can fix it.”
- **Thinks:** Robots don't change her own picture, but there's no simple story about where her kids should aim.
- **Does:** Keeps the same plan and volunteers to lead the group's robotics plan, since no one owns it yet.
- **Feels:** Same worry, plus a strange buzz from a new problem.
- **Mood:** Anxious
