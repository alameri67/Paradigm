# P098 · Lisa Romero

Police patrol officer · Government

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Lisa, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Lisa:** Sure. Off the record's good. I've got a few things I can't say at roll call.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Lisa:** I'd say it's handy for writing reports. Saves me an hour a shift. That's about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Lisa:** Doesn't change my job. You still need a person to knock on a door at 2 a.m. and calm down a family fight.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Lisa:** Use the report tool, get home a bit earlier, see my kids more. Nothing else.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Lisa:** Fine. Kind of nice, actually.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's handy for writing reports. Saves me an hour a shift.”
- **Thinks:** Her job doesn't change; a person still has to knock on doors at 2 a.m.
- **Does:** Uses the report tool, gets home earlier, and sees her kids more.
- **Feels:** Fine, kind of nice.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Lisa:** I'd say tools are fine but they can't make decisions about people. I've seen the body-cam software flag the wrong guy.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Lisa:** Slow is fine for me. My worry is the department buying some system that tells us who's a risk and then we're stuck following it. That's how good cops get in trouble.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Lisa:** Speak up in training when they roll out the analytics. Ask who checks the computer's call. Keep doing my job.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Lisa:** Calm. A little wary of the brass.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Tools are fine, but they can't make decisions about people. The body-cam software has flagged the wrong guy.”
- **Thinks:** Her worry is a system that rates who's a risk and that officers are then stuck following.
- **Does:** Speaks up in analytics training and asks who checks the computer's call.
- **Feels:** Calm, a little wary of the bosses.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Lisa:** I'd say folks need to stay calm and keep their families together. And people who lose jobs should get retraining, not just money.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Lisa:** Layoffs mean stress, and stress means more calls. Domestics, drinking, fights. My job's safe, but it gets harder. And the politicians yelling makes the protests bigger.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Lisa:** Pick up overtime, since it'll be there. Put it in savings. Talk to my husband about cutting back a bit just in case.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Lisa:** Uneasy. Work gets rougher.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Folks need to stay calm and keep their families together. People who lose jobs need retraining, not just money.”
- **Thinks:** Layoffs bring more stress-related calls and bigger protests; her job is safe but harder.
- **Does:** Takes the extra overtime, saves it, and plans small cutbacks with her husband.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, work gets rougher.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Lisa:** I'd say our job is to keep the peace, whatever side people are on. I'd keep my politics to myself in uniform.

**Interviewer:** You keep your politics to yourself in uniform. What would you say out of it?

**Lisa:** That the guys who got rich off this should pay into job programmes, not just walk away. I'm not a big government person, but that's fair.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Lisa:** Honestly? A political crisis with lots of angry people out of work is the hardest thing to police. I'd be on crowd lines. And I'd worry the city brings in drones and face-matching to cope, and starts arresting people based on what a machine says.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Lisa:** Get the crowd-control training. Work the overtime, bank it. Make sure my kids know where to go if things get bad downtown.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Lisa:** Anxious. More for the street than for my paycheck.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Our job is to keep the peace, whatever side people are on.”
- **Thinks:** Angry crowds out of work are the hardest to police, and she fears the city leaning on drones and face-matching to make arrests.
- **Does:** Gets crowd-control training, banks overtime, and makes a safety plan with her kids.
- **Feels:** Anxious, more for the street than her pay.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Lisa:** I'd say there's a line. A robot can carry boxes. It shouldn't be patrolling a neighbourhood or deciding who to stop.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Lisa:** If robots take the warehouse and building jobs too, there'd be even more people with nothing to lose. And I'd bet some city council buys patrol robots to save money. That'd make my job worse and maybe smaller, and I don't trust them with people.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Lisa:** Fight any patrol-robot plan through the police union. Keep the overtime money in savings. Maybe look at moving into training or detective work, where you deal with people face to face.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Lisa:** Anxious. Still in control, but it's a lot.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A robot can carry boxes. It shouldn't patrol a neighbourhood or decide who to stop.”
- **Thinks:** More people with nothing to lose, and councils buying patrol robots, would make her job worse and possibly smaller.
- **Does:** Fights patrol-robot plans through the police union, saves overtime, and looks at training or detective roles.
- **Feels:** Anxious, still in control.
- **Mood:** Anxious
