# P070 · Ngozi Eze

Data entry and document clerk · Administrative and support services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Ngozi. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Ngozi:** Thank you. I'm a little shy, but I'll answer honestly.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ngozi:** I'd say it's useful. I use it sometimes to check my coding homework.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ngozi:** Even as a tool, it reads documents faster than me. My job is typing what's on paper into a system. I know that's not a job for the future.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ngozi:** Keep studying for the coding certificate in the evenings. Finish it this year if I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ngozi:** A little worried, but I have a plan.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's useful. I use it to check my coding homework.”
- **Thinks:** Even as a tool, AI reads documents faster than her. Typing paper into a system isn't a job for the future.
- **Does:** Keeps studying for her coding certificate in the evenings and aims to finish this year.
- **Feels:** A little worried, but she has a plan.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ngozi:** I'd say young people need a way in. I have a degree and I'm doing data entry, because that's what was there.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ngozi:** Slow gives me time. Finish the certificate, get a junior coding job, move up. But fewer beginner jobs is exactly what I'd be applying for.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ngozi:** Finish the certificate. Build a few small projects to show employers. Ask my manager if I can help with the systems side at work.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ngozi:** Hopeful, with some worry.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Young people need a way in. I have a degree and I'm doing data entry because that's what was there.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change gives her time, but the fewer beginner jobs are exactly the ones she'd apply for.
- **Does:** Finishes the certificate, builds small projects for employers and asks to help with systems at work.
- **Feels:** Hopeful, with some worry.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ngozi:** I'd say we should help people move into the new jobs. Training, small grants. Not just argue.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ngozi:** My job would be the first to go. Routine office work is exactly what I do. The new AI jobs might be my way out, if the certificate is enough. I really don't know if it is.

**Interviewer:** You said you don't know if the certificate is enough. What makes you unsure?

**Ngozi:** Everyone is doing these certificates now. And the AI writes code too. I'd be competing with it and with a lot of people like me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ngozi:** Apply for every junior tech or AI support role I can find. Cut my spending to almost nothing. Tell my family back home I may have to send less for a while, which would be very hard to say.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ngozi:** Pressure in my chest. But I'm still moving.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We should help people move into the new jobs with training and small grants, not just argue.”
- **Thinks:** Her job would go first. New AI jobs might be her way out, but she's unsure the certificate is enough when everyone is doing one.
- **Does:** Applies for every junior tech role, cuts spending to almost nothing and warns her family she may send less.
- **Feels:** Pressure in her chest, but still moving.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ngozi:** I'd say the gains should go to ordinary people, not only those who own shares and houses. That's what's fair.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ngozi:** If AI matches skilled professionals, it matches junior coders easily. My plan was coding. That plan breaks. I'd have a degree, a certificate and no job.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ngozi:** Look for physical work that's still safe, maybe in a hospital or a care home, because people always need care. Pray a lot. Ask the church for help finding something.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ngozi:** Frightened. My plan stopped working.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains should go to ordinary people, not only those who own shares and houses.”
- **Thinks:** AI that matches professionals easily matches junior coders, so her coding plan breaks.
- **Does:** Looks for care or hospital work, prays and asks her church for help finding a job.
- **Feels:** Frightened. Her plan stopped working.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ngozi:** I'd say people need a guaranteed job or training, something with dignity. Not just a cheque.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ngozi:** Care homes were my backup. If robots go there too, I don't know. But I'm young, I learn fast, and I've changed plans before. I'd find something. I have to, because my family depends on me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ngozi:** Keep applying everywhere. Look at jobs that need a person to be trusted, like working with children. Share a room to cut rent.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ngozi:** Very scared. But I won't give up.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People need a guaranteed job or training, something with dignity, not just a cheque.”
- **Thinks:** Care homes were her backup and robots threaten them too, but she's young, learns fast and her family depends on her.
- **Does:** Keeps applying everywhere, looks at trusted work with children and shares a room to cut rent.
