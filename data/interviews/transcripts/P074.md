# P074 · Daniel Reyes

School district edtech lead · Educational services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Daniel. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Daniel:** Absolutely. This is my favourite topic, so stop me if I get carried away.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Daniel:** I'd say what I say to every principal: used well, these tools give teachers back hours every week.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Daniel:** That's roughly what my pilots show. Teachers save time on planning and marking. If it stays a tool, I keep running pilots and it's a good, steady job.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Daniel:** Expand the pilots to a few more schools. Write proper guidance for teachers on what to use and what to avoid.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Daniel:** Good. Energised.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Used well, these tools give teachers back hours every week.”
- **Thinks:** His pilots show time saved on planning and marking. If it stays a tool, he has a good, steady job.
- **Does:** Expands the pilots and writes clear guidance for teachers.
- **Feels:** Good and energised.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Daniel:** I'd say this is our window to do it right in schools, before the pressure arrives.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Daniel:** Slow is the best case for my work. Time to test things, find what actually helps kids, and drop what doesn't.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Daniel:** Set up a proper study with a university partner across the district. Train a group of lead teachers in each school.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Daniel:** Calm. Pleased, honestly.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is our window to do it right in schools, before the pressure arrives.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change is his best case, with time to test what really helps children.
- **Does:** Sets up a district-wide study with a university and trains lead teachers in each school.
- **Feels:** Calm and pleased.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Daniel:** I'd say students need to learn to work alongside AI, and our district is ready because we started early.

**Interviewer:** You said the evidence is more mixed than you say in public. Why not say so?

**Daniel:** Because funding follows confidence. If I sound unsure, the pilots get cut and we lose the chance to learn. It's a balance I'm not proud of.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Daniel:** My job gets bigger, not smaller. Everyone suddenly wants an AI plan. Between us, the evidence on learning is more mixed than I let on in public. Saving teachers time is clear. Better results for kids, less so.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Daniel:** Ask for a bigger team and budget while there's political will. Be more honest in my reports about what isn't working, because a big rollout on weak evidence would come back on me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Daniel:** Excited, with a small nag about the evidence.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Students need to learn to work alongside AI, and our district is ready because we started early.”
- **Thinks:** His job grows, but the evidence on learning is more mixed than he says in public.
- **Does:** Asks for more team and budget, and reports more honestly on what isn't working.
- **Feels:** Excited, with a small nag about the evidence.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Daniel:** I'd say schools have to change what they teach, fast. Less memorising, more judgement, more working with people.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Daniel:** If AI matches professionals, someone on the school board will ask why we need a human edtech lead when a vendor's AI can plan the rollout. And with a political crisis, budgets swing wildly. My job is safer than most, but it isn't safe.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Daniel:** Make myself the person who knows the teachers and the families, not just the software. Put the share gains towards paying off debt. Keep a list of vendor jobs I could move to.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Daniel:** Uneasy. Less sure of my footing.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Schools have to change what they teach fast, towards judgement and working with people.”
- **Thinks:** A vendor's AI could plan the rollout, and budgets would swing in the crisis. His job is safer than most, but not safe.
- **Does:** Makes himself the person who knows teachers and families, pays down debt with share gains and lists vendor jobs he could move to.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, less sure of his footing.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Daniel:** I'd say schools are now the most important public service we have, because they're where people learn what to do next.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Daniel:** Robots don't change much for me. If anything, a society that needs to retrain everyone needs people like me to run it. I'd probably come out of this okay. My worry is for families in the district who won't.

**Interviewer:** Earlier you said your job wasn't safe. Now you say you'd come out okay. Which is it?

**Daniel:** Both, I think. My current title might go. But someone who can help a whole community learn new tools will be needed. I'm betting on that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Daniel:** Pitch the district on running evening retraining for parents in our school buildings. That's a real use for what I know.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Daniel:** Mostly steady. A bit uneasy for others.

- **Says:** “Schools are now the most important public service, because they're where people learn what to do next.”
- **Thinks:** A society retraining everyone needs people like him. His title might go, but he'd probably come out okay.
