# N04 · Bianca Lima

Job seeker, marketing graduate with no first job yet · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making the time, Bianca. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group. We don't sell anything or campaign for anyone. Nothing you tell me today will be linked to your name. I'll read you five short pictures of how AI might change things over the next five years, and ask how you'd react to each.

**Bianca:** Sure, this is literally my life right now. I finished my marketing degree last year and I still haven't found a first job. The junior roles I trained for just aren't being posted any more.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it to friends, to friends, or online?

**Bianca:** I'd say the job market is just brutal right now, and it's not my fault. I'd probably post something a bit sarcastic about needing three years' experience for an entry-level job.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bianca:** Honestly, part of me would be relieved, because then it's just a bad patch. But I don't really believe it. I use AI every day and I can see it writes the stuff I was hired to write.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bianca:** Keep applying, maybe 20 a week. Take shifts at a café or in a shop so I'm not totally broke. Do some free social media work for a local business to get something on my CV.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bianca:** Stuck. Like everyone else got a start and I missed the door.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd be relieved, but also that you don't believe it. Which is closer to how you feel?

**Bianca:** The not believing it. I've seen what it can do with a caption or a campaign brief. It's good. That's the scary bit.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The job market is brutal right now, and it's not her fault.”
- **Thinks:** She'd like it to be just a bad patch, but from daily use she can see AI already writes what she trained to write.
- **Does:** Applies to about 20 jobs a week, takes café or shop shifts and does free social media work for a local business to build her CV.
- **Feels:** Stuck, like she missed the door everyone else got through.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it to friends, to friends, or online?

**Bianca:** I'd say, that's me, that's my whole generation. Companies saving money by not training anyone. In ten years they'll wonder where all the experienced people went.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bianca:** This is exactly what's happening to me. Slow for everyone else maybe, but for beginners it's already here.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bianca:** Stop waiting for a marketing job and widen it. Sales, customer service, anything with a training scheme. Maybe a short course in data or digital ads, if it's cheap.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bianca:** Pretty bleak. I worked hard for that degree and I'm still at my parents' kitchen table.

### Summary

- **Says:** “That's her whole generation, with companies saving money by not training anyone.”
- **Thinks:** It may be slow for everyone else, but for beginners like her it's already happening.
- **Does:** Widens her search to sales, customer service and anything with a training scheme, and looks for a cheap course in data or digital ads.
- **Feels:** Bleak, a hard-earned degree and still at her parents' table.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it to friends, to friends, or online?

**Bianca:** I'd say the government needs to step in, because companies won't. And I'd call out the tech bosses who are getting rich while nobody under 25 can get hired.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bianca:** Weirdly, this could open a door. If there are new AI jobs, I'm young and I use these tools all day. I might be better at it than the people being laid off.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bianca:** Build a portfolio of campaigns I made with AI tools, show I can run them start to finish. Apply for every AI marketing or content operations role I can find, even in the city.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bianca:** Nervous, but for once a bit hopeful.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government needs to step in because companies won't, and tech bosses are getting rich while young people can't get hired.”
- **Thinks:** New AI jobs could be her way in, since she uses the tools all day and may beat the people being laid off.
- **Does:** Builds a portfolio of campaigns made with AI tools and applies for every AI marketing or content role, including in the city.
- **Feels:** Nervous, but for once a bit hopeful.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it to friends, to friends, or online?

**Bianca:** I'd be really angry, and I'd say it. House prices booming means I'll never move out. The gains need to be shared, through taxes, a basic income, something.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bianca:** If AI matches skilled professionals, nobody's paying a 23-year-old to do marketing. My degree is basically gone. I'd have to start again doing something with my hands or with people.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bianca:** Look at nursing or hairdressing or a trade, something physical. Ask my parents if I can stay longer while I train. Keep a café job to pay my way.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bianca:** Lost. Like I did everything right and it didn't count.

### Summary

- **Says:** “House prices booming means she'll never move out, and the gains need sharing through taxes or a basic income.”
- **Thinks:** Nobody would pay a beginner to do marketing, so her degree is basically gone and she'd start again in hands-on or people work.
- **Does:** Looks at nursing, hairdressing or a trade, asks her parents if she can stay longer while she trains and keeps a café job.
- **Feels:** Lost, she did everything right and it didn't count.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it to friends, to friends, or online?

**Bianca:** I'd say there's literally nowhere left to go. They told us to get degrees, then to learn a trade. Now what?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Bianca:** The backup plan from last time is gone too. Even the café work. I'd be living with my parents with no idea for how long.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Bianca:** Go for care or nursing, because people might still want a human there. Get involved in some political group pushing for a basic income. Keep my parents' house running, help out.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Bianca:** Scared and really tired of being told to adapt.

### Summary

- **Says:** “There's nowhere left to go: first get a degree, then learn a trade, now what?”
- **Thinks:** Her backup plan of hands-on work is gone too, and she'd be living with her parents with no end in sight.
- **Does:** Aims for care or nursing work, joins a group pushing for a basic income and helps keep her parents' house running.
- **Feels:** Scared and tired of being told to adapt.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
