# P090 · Jasmine Taylor

Server and bartender · Accommodation and food services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Jasmine, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Jasmine:** Yes, love this. Ask me anything. I've got opinions.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jasmine:** I'd say cool, but nobody wants to be served by a tablet. People come to a bar for the bartender.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jasmine:** Honestly it doesn't touch me. My worry is rent and slow Tuesdays, not robots.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jasmine:** Keep working five nights, keep my regulars happy, try to save something for once. I use AI to help with my resume and cocktail ideas, that's it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jasmine:** Pretty chill.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Nobody wants to be served by a tablet. People come to a bar for the bartender.”
- **Thinks:** It doesn't touch her; rent and slow nights worry her more.
- **Does:** Works five nights, keeps her regulars, tries to save, and uses AI for her resume and cocktail ideas.
- **Feels:** Pretty chill.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jasmine:** I'd say it's messed up that companies stop hiring young people. That's how you get a whole generation stuck in service jobs, like me.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jasmine:** For my job, nothing. People still want a drink and a chat. But I've got the two-year degree and was thinking of going back for more. If entry jobs dry up, why bother?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jasmine:** Stay behind the bar, it's honestly safer than a desk now. Maybe do a proper bartending course to move to cocktail bars with better tips.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jasmine:** Fine. A bit annoyed for my friends.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's messed up that companies stop hiring young people. That's how a generation gets stuck in service jobs.”
- **Thinks:** Her job's untouched, but fewer entry jobs make going back to study look pointless.
- **Does:** Stays behind the bar and takes a bartending course to move to higher-tip cocktail bars.
- **Feels:** Fine, annoyed for her friends.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jasmine:** I'd say tax the companies doing the layoffs and give workers a real say. I'd post about it, go to the rallies.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jasmine:** Office people are my best tippers at happy hour. If they're scared about their jobs, they order one drink, not three, and tip less. My income is tips. So yeah, it hits me sideways.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jasmine:** Pick up more weekend shifts when the money is better. Get involved with the hospitality workers' group in the city. Keep living with roommates, no way I'm moving out alone.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jasmine:** A bit uneasy. Mostly mad on other people's behalf.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Tax the companies doing the layoffs and give workers a real say.”
- **Thinks:** Scared office workers drink less and tip less, which hits her income sideways.
- **Does:** Takes more weekend shifts, joins a hospitality workers' group, and stays with roommates.
- **Feels:** A bit uneasy, mostly angry for others.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jasmine:** I'd say this is the moment. Billionaires get richer, everybody else gets fired. We need a wealth tax and basic income, now.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jasmine:** My job is weirdly safe. Rich people with booming shares drink a lot and tip well, and I'd move to a fancy bar to catch that. I'd feel gross about it, but I've got rent.

**Interviewer:** You'd serve the people you're protesting against. How does that sit with you?

**Jasmine:** Badly. But that's always been the job. I've poured drinks for people I can't stand forever. At least I'd be taking their money for the cause.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jasmine:** Get hired somewhere upscale, serve the people getting rich, and spend my days off organising. Probably canvass for whoever runs on taxing the gains.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jasmine:** Uneasy and kind of guilty, but energised.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is the moment. Billionaires get richer while everyone else gets fired. We need a wealth tax and basic income.”
- **Thinks:** Her job is oddly safe, and she'd chase tips from the newly rich, though she'd feel bad about it.
- **Does:** Moves to an upscale bar, organises on days off, and canvasses for candidates who want to tax the gains.
- **Feels:** Uneasy and guilty, but energised.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jasmine:** I'd say robots in kitchens and care homes is where it gets inhuman. People need people. And the gains need to go to everyone.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jasmine:** Bars might be one of the last places with humans, honestly. But the kitchen staff I work with, the dishwashers, the cooks, they're gone. Restaurants with fewer staff close more often. My world gets a lot smaller.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jasmine:** Same as before, but I'd also start saving like crazy and look at a backup, maybe something in social work or organising as a real job. Push harder on the politics.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jasmine:** Anxious. Not for me exactly, for everyone around me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots in kitchens and care homes is where it gets inhuman. People need people, and the gains must go to everyone.”
- **Thinks:** Bars may keep humans longest, but her kitchen coworkers would go and her world would shrink.
- **Does:** Saves hard, looks at social work or organising as a paid backup, and pushes harder on the politics.
- **Feels:** Anxious for everyone around her.
- **Mood:** Anxious
