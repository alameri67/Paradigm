# P052 · Sofia Rossi

Owner, property management firm · Real estate

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Sofia. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Sofia:** Good. I've filled in plenty of surveys, but a proper conversation is nicer. Go ahead.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sofia:** I'd say it's useful for the paperwork. Lease renewals, maintenance notices, that sort of thing.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sofia:** It means my admin gets a bit cheaper. That's all. Property is still property.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sofia:** I'd ask my office manager to try a couple of the tools for letters and rent reminders. Nothing dramatic.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sofia:** Perfectly fine. I've seen fax machines come and go.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's useful for the paperwork, like lease renewals and maintenance notices.”
- **Thinks:** Her admin gets a little cheaper and property is still property.
- **Does:** Has her office manager try AI tools for letters and rent reminders.
- **Feels:** Perfectly fine. She's seen fax machines come and go.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sofia:** I'd say a good business adapts slowly and sensibly. No need to rush.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sofia:** Slow suits me. When someone retires I won't replace them, and the tools will do the work. No one gets a nasty surprise.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sofia:** Stop hiring junior admin. Let the team shrink by retirement over a few years.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sofia:** Comfortable. It's the way I'd choose to do it myself.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A good business adapts slowly and sensibly.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change lets her shrink the office by retirement with no one getting a nasty surprise.
- **Does:** Stops hiring junior admin and lets the team shrink as people retire.
- **Feels:** Comfortable. It's how she'd choose to do it.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sofia:** Publicly I'd say we look after our people, and we do. We'd retrain where we can.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sofia:** Between us, I have eleven staff and I could probably run the portfolio with six. The software would pay for itself in a year.

**Interviewer:** You said you look after your people. How does that sit with going from eleven to six?

**Sofia:** Looking after people means a fair payout and a good reference. It doesn't mean paying salaries the business doesn't need. My husband taught me that, and he was right.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sofia:** Bring in one of those AI property systems for tenant enquiries and arrears. Offer a few people early retirement with a decent payout. Keep the ones tenants trust.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sofia:** Practical. I don't enjoy letting people go, but I'm not losing sleep.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We look after our people, and we'd retrain where we can.”
- **Thinks:** She could run the portfolio with six staff instead of eleven, and the software would pay for itself.
- **Does:** Brings in AI software for tenant enquiries and arrears, and offers early retirement with fair payouts.
- **Feels:** Practical. Doesn't enjoy it, but isn't losing sleep.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sofia:** I'd say property has always been a safe place for savings, and I'd keep my head down.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sofia:** My buildings would be worth a lot more. But a political crisis over who gets the gains means people like me are the target. Rent caps, new taxes on landlords. That's what would keep me up.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sofia:** Talk to my accountant and my lawyer early. Maybe sell one or two of the weaker flats while prices are high and pay down debt.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sofia:** Well off but watched. A bit wary.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Property has always been a safe place for savings.”
- **Thinks:** Her buildings gain value, but landlords become the political target for rent caps and new taxes.
- **Does:** Sees her accountant and lawyer early and sells a couple of weaker flats while prices are high.
- **Feels:** Well off but watched. A bit wary.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sofia:** I'd say the government needs to steady things down. People need work, and tenants need to pay rent.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sofia:** Robots fixing gutters would cut my maintenance bills, I'll admit that. The worry is tenants. If the cleaners and builders who rent my flats lose work, arrears go up. But I'd still be fine. I'm not the one in trouble here.

**Interviewer:** You're cutting maintenance costs with robots while your tenants lose work to them. How does that feel to you?

**Sofia:** Not lovely, put like that. But I didn't invent the robots. I'll do what I can for tenants who've always paid on time.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sofia:** Tighten tenant checks. Hold more cash in the business for missed rent. Keep the buildings in good shape so the better tenants stay.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sofia:** Concerned for others more than me. A little uneasy.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government needs to steady things down. People need work and tenants need to pay rent.”
- **Thinks:** Robots would cut her maintenance bills, but tenant arrears would rise. She knows she'd still be fine.
- **Does:** Tightens tenant checks, keeps more cash for missed rent and looks after the buildings so good tenants stay.
- **Feels:** More concerned for others than herself. A little uneasy.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
