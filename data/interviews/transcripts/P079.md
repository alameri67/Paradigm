# P079 · Brenda Collins

Medical billing and coding specialist · Health care and social assistance

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Brenda, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Brenda:** All right. I'm not much of a talker, but I'll do my best.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Brenda:** I'd say I hope that's how it goes. I'd probably keep it to myself at work, though. People there are touchy about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Brenda:** Even in that picture, the coding software keeps getting better. It already suggests the codes and I check them. So I'd still wonder how long they need two of us checking.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Brenda:** Keep my head down. Do my numbers, don't make mistakes, and keep paying off the car. Maybe put a little aside if my son chips in more for groceries.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Brenda:** Uneasy. I'm a worrier, always have been.

- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Brenda:** I'd say we've been through changes before. We went from paper to computers and I learned it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Brenda:** But I was 30 then. Now I'm 56. If they hire fewer beginners, fine, but they'll also let the older ones retire and not replace them. And I'm not near enough to retire.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Brenda:** I'd ask my supervisor straight out if there's a plan for our team. And I'd look at the audit side, checking the computer's codes, since somebody has to.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Brenda:** Nervous. Like waiting for a test result.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We've been through changes before. We went from paper to computers and I learned it.”
- **Thinks:** At 56 she's too far from retirement, and she expects older staff to be let go without replacement.
- **Does:** Asks her supervisor directly about the team's future and looks at moving into auditing the software's codes.
- **Feels:** Nervous, like waiting for a test result.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Brenda:** I'd say people who've done this job for twenty years deserve a chance to retrain, not a box and a security guard.

**Interviewer:** You said retraining. If they offered it, what would you want to retrain as?

**Brenda:** I honestly don't know. That's the trouble. I'd want something with steady hours and no selling. Maybe patient scheduling, if that's not gone too.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Brenda:** I think I'd be in the first round. Routine office work is exactly what I do. There's nothing else like it in this town, and I'm not moving at my age.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Brenda:** Cut everything. No eating out, cancel the streaming, talk to my son about paying more of the bills. I'd apply for front desk work at the clinics, anything with health insurance.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Brenda:** Scared. I don't sleep well already, and this would make it worse.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People who've done this job for twenty years deserve a chance to retrain, not a box and a security guard.”
- **Thinks:** She expects to be in the first round of cuts, with nothing similar in town and no plan to move.
- **Does:** Cuts all extras, asks her son to cover more bills, and applies for clinic front desk jobs with health insurance.
- **Feels:** Scared, sleeping even worse.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Brenda:** I'd probably not say much. Everyone would be in the same boat and nobody wants to hear another sad story.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Brenda:** I'd be out, no question. And I'm not one of the people whose house is worth more. I rent. So the boom does nothing for me but push up my rent.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Brenda:** Apply for anything, cleaning, care work, a till at the supermarket. Take unemployment while I look. I'd ask my son if we can manage on his wages for a bit.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Brenda:** Overwhelmed. Like there's no ground under me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Wouldn't say much, since everyone would be in the same boat.”
- **Thinks:** She'd be out for sure, and as a renter the boom only pushes up her rent.
- **Does:** Applies for any job, including cleaning, care work and supermarket tills, claims unemployment, and leans on her son's wages.
- **Feels:** Overwhelmed, no ground under her.
- **Mood:** Panicked

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Brenda:** I'd say what are people supposed to do now? Even the jobs we told each other to fall back on are going.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Brenda:** In the last one I thought I could at least clean or do care work. If the robots take that too, there's nothing left for someone like me. I'm too young to retire and too old to start over.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Brenda:** Same as before, but faster, and I'd stop being picky. I'd go to the church food bank if I had to. I'd look into whether the government has some sort of help for older workers.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Brenda:** Hopeless, to be honest. Like the doors keep shutting.

### Summary

- **Says:** “What are people supposed to do now? Even the jobs we told each other to fall back on are going.”
- **Thinks:** Her backup plan of cleaning or care work disappears too. Too young to retire, too old to start over.
- **Does:** Takes any job faster, uses the church food bank if needed, and looks for government help for older workers.
