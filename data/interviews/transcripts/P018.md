# P018 · Sarah Chen

Project engineer and estimator · Construction

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Sarah. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Sarah:** Sure. I use this stuff every day, so I've got opinions.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sarah:** I'd say it's a useful tool. It already does half my takeoffs, which is counting up materials from the drawings, and most of my schedules. So I spend more time on the interesting parts.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sarah:** Even in the calm version, half my job is software. That's fine as long as the other half grows. I'd want to move toward project management, where it's more about people.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sarah:** Get my project management certificate. Keep paying down the loan. Ask to run a small job myself.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sarah:** Okay, mostly. It's the world I'm already in.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a useful tool. It does half my takeoffs and schedules, so I get the interesting parts.”
- **Thinks:** Half my job is already software. That's fine as long as the other half grows.
- **Does:** Works toward a project management certificate, pays down her loan and asks to run a small job.
- **Feels:** Okay, mostly.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sarah:** I'd say that's how it's already playing out. Firms are hiring fewer grads and expecting juniors to do more with the tools.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sarah:** I'm a junior, so fewer beginners hits close. I'm in, but stepping up gets harder when there are fewer people below you. I'd need to get out of pure estimating.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sarah:** Push to get on site more. Learn the parts of the job that are about people and the physical build, not spreadsheets.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sarah:** Uneasy. I'd want to move before the ground does.

### Summary

- **Says:** “That's already happening. Firms hire fewer grads and expect juniors to do more with the tools.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer beginners hits close. I need to get out of pure estimating.
- **Does:** Pushes to spend more time on site, learning the people and building side of the job.
- **Feels:** Uneasy. Wants to move before the ground does.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sarah:** I'd say the people who learn to direct AI will be fine, and the ones who don't will struggle. Bit harsh, but it's what I see.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sarah:** Estimating and scheduling is exactly the routine desk work in this picture. I'm good, but the agent doesn't need to be good. Just good enough and cheap. And I've still got student debt.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sarah:** Apply for site engineer roles right away. Build up three months of savings. Maybe do estimating for small builders on the side, using the same tools that are replacing me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sarah:** Anxious. Like a clock is running.

**Interviewer:** Your public line is that people who learn the tools will be fine. You know the tools well. Do you feel fine?

**Sarah:** No. Which tells you how much I believe my own line.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sarah:** I'd say the gains have to be shared somehow, and that graduates were sold a promise that's now broken.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sarah:** My job's gone in this one. Project engineering is thinking work. All that's left is the site, the physical side. And I'm still paying for a degree for a career that just disappeared.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sarah:** Get a site role, even a step down in title, because physical work is safe here. Honestly, look at a trade, like an electrician's apprenticeship. Ask about pausing my loan payments.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sarah:** Alarmed. And angry about the debt.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains have to be shared, and graduates were sold a promise that's now broken.”
- **Thinks:** My job's gone. I'm still paying for a degree for a career that just disappeared.
- **Does:** Takes a site role even at a lower title, looks into an electrician's apprenticeship and asks to pause her loan payments.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, and angry about the debt.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Sarah:** I'd say we need a proper plan. Retraining, income support, all of it. Not just 'learn to code' or 'learn a trade'.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Sarah:** Now even the site isn't safe. The backup I had in the last one is gone. My only edge is that I know both the software and the site, so maybe I can be the person who plans the robot work. That's a narrow door.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Sarah:** Go after any role coordinating robots on building sites. Talk to the robot companies directly. Get a flatmate to cut my rent. Keep the loan on the minimum.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Sarah:** Still alarmed. And tired of planning backups.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need a proper plan: retraining and income support, not just 'learn a trade'.”
- **Thinks:** Even the site isn't safe now. Knowing both software and site might let me plan the robot work, but that's a narrow door.
- **Does:** Goes after roles coordinating robots on sites, contacts robot companies, gets a flatmate and keeps her loan on the minimum.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, and tired of planning backups.
