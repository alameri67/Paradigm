# P063 · Omar Farouk

HR business partner · Management of companies

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Omar. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Omar:** Of course, thanks for having me. I'm usually the one asking the questions, so this is a nice change.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Omar:** I'd say it's great for the paperwork side of HR. Job adverts, policy drafts, that kind of thing.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Omar:** Fine for me. HR is about people. If AI just makes the forms quicker, I get more time to actually talk to staff.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Omar:** Use it for the boring documents. Spend the time I save on one-to-ones with managers.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Omar:** Pretty good. Relaxed.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's great for the paperwork side of HR, like job adverts and policy drafts.”
- **Thinks:** If forms get quicker, he gets more time to talk to staff.
- **Does:** Uses AI for documents and spends the saved time on one-to-ones with managers.
- **Feels:** Relaxed.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Omar:** I'd say we need to look after our early-career people, because if we stop hiring them we lose our future leaders.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Omar:** I'd be the one writing the hiring freeze emails for graduates. That bothers me. I was a graduate hire four years ago.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Omar:** Argue for keeping some graduate places. Learn more about workforce planning, because that's where HR is heading.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Omar:** A bit uncomfortable.

### Summary

- **Says:** “If we stop hiring early-career people, we lose our future leaders.”
- **Thinks:** He'd be writing the graduate hiring freeze emails, and he was a graduate hire himself.
- **Does:** Argues to keep some graduate places and learns workforce planning.
- **Feels:** A bit uncomfortable.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Omar:** At work I'd say the right things: we'll treat everyone with respect and support them through the change. And I'd mean it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Omar:** Off the record, I'd be in rooms telling people they've lost their jobs. People I've had lunch with. And part of me would be thinking, how long until they don't need someone like me to do it?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Omar:** Get properly trained on running redundancies fairly, so at least it's done well. Push for real outplacement help, the kind that finds people new jobs. Start saving harder with my wife.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Omar:** Torn. Like I'm holding the knife.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd mean the supportive line. Can you hold both of those at once?

**Omar:** I have to. If I quit, someone colder does it. That's what I'd tell myself, anyway.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We'll treat everyone with respect and support them through the change.”
- **Thinks:** He'd be telling colleagues they've lost their jobs while wondering how long until he isn't needed either.
- **Does:** Trains to run redundancies fairly, pushes for real outplacement help and saves harder with his wife.
- **Feels:** Torn, like he's holding the knife.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Omar:** I'd say workers need a voice in this. Unions, works councils, something. The people who lose out should have a seat at the table.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Omar:** If most office jobs go, there are fewer people for HR to look after. I'd run the layoffs and then be laid off at the end. I'm junior, I'm average. I'd be in the last wave, but I'd be in it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Omar:** Start looking at roles with unions or employment charities, where the work is helping people through this. Talk to my wife about putting off buying a place. Keep our savings in cash.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Omar:** Sick about it. Losing sleep.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Workers need a voice in this. The people losing out should have a seat at the table.”
- **Thinks:** He'd run the layoffs and then be laid off in the last wave. He's junior and average.
- **Does:** Looks at jobs with unions or employment charities, holds off buying a home and keeps savings in cash.
- **Feels:** Sick about it and losing sleep.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Omar:** Same as before, but louder. If robots come for warehouses and care homes too, we need a plan for everyone, not only office workers.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Omar:** For my own job it doesn't change much from the last one. But it means my wife's family, my cousins who work in warehouses, all of them get hit as well. There's nobody left to lean on.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Omar:** Same steps. Plus sit down with family and work out who can help who if things go badly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Omar:** Heavy. It's not just me anymore, it's everyone I know.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need a plan for everyone now, not only office workers.”
- **Thinks:** His own picture is the same, but now family members in warehouse work get hit too and there's nobody to lean on.
- **Does:** Keeps the same steps and sits down with family to plan who can help whom.
- **Feels:** Heavy. It's everyone he knows now.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
