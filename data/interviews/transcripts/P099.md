# P099 · Anil Sharma

Senior policy adviser, ministry of economy · Government

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Anil, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Anil:** Thank you. I spend my days writing the official line, so I'll try to be clear about when I'm giving you that and when I'm not.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Anil:** I'd say AI is a productivity gain and our job is to help firms adopt it safely. That's roughly what the current strategy says.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Anil:** Privately, it would be a relief. The models we run show a lot of uncertainty. A world where it's just a better tool means my policy drafts age well.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Anil:** Keep the current programme going: skills funding, small-business adoption grants, some light rules on safety. I'd use the tools myself to speed up drafting, which I already do.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Anil:** Calm. A quieter life than I expect.

### Summary

- **Says:** “AI is a productivity gain and our job is to help firms adopt it safely.”
- **Thinks:** It would be a relief, since his policy drafts would hold up well in that world.
- **Does:** Keeps the current programme of skills funding, adoption grants and light safety rules, and drafts faster with AI.
- **Feels:** Calm, a quieter life than expected.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Anil:** I'd say we have time to prepare, and we should use it: invest in skills, protect pathways into work for young people.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Anil:** Honestly, this is the scenario we're best built for. The worry is the beginners. If firms stop hiring juniors, we lose a generation's training, and nobody notices until it's too late. That's hard to fix with policy.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Anil:** Push for an apprenticeship and graduate-hiring incentive in next year's budget. Commission a study on entry-level hiring. Make sure the minister is briefed on it every quarter.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Anil:** Calm, with one nagging worry.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We have time to prepare and should invest in skills and protect young people's pathways into work.”
- **Thinks:** This is the scenario government is best built for, but losing junior hiring would cost a generation's training before anyone notices.
- **Does:** Pushes a hiring incentive for apprentices and graduates, commissions a study, and briefs the minister quarterly.
- **Feels:** Calm, with one nagging worry.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Anil:** I'd say government will support affected workers through retraining and income support, and we'll make sure the gains are broadly shared.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Anil:** That line is easy to write and hard to fund. Retraining for what? I'd be honest with the minister that our programmes aren't sized for this. And my own job is mostly drafting, which these agents do well. I tell myself ministers want a human to blame, which keeps me safe.

**Interviewer:** A human to blame. Do you believe that will keep you safe?

**Anil:** For a while. I'm good at what I do. But I've seen how fast budget offices move when there's a saving. I'd rather not test it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Anil:** Draft options for wage insurance and faster unemployment support. Build a team to track layoffs monthly. Use the agents to do three times the analysis, so I stay the one people rely on.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Anil:** Uneasy. Busy and a bit sleepless.

- **Says:** “Government will support affected workers through retraining and income support and make sure gains are shared.”

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Anil:** Publicly, I'd say the government is taking decisive action to make sure the benefits of this transformation reach every household. Words like that.

**Interviewer:** That public line sounds smooth. How would you feel saying it?

**Anil:** Like a liar, a little. It's not false. It's just far more confident than anyone in the building feels.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Anil:** Off the record, we wouldn't be ready. Lots of skilled people out of work in two years, asset prices booming for the people who already own things, and parliament at each other's throats. Every option I'd draft would be too slow or too expensive for someone. I'd feel the weight of getting it wrong.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Anil:** Work around the clock on emergency options: windfall taxes, a public stake in AI firms, a temporary income floor. Keep my family's savings safe. Tell my kids to study something they love, because I can't predict the rest.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Anil:** Alarmed. Like I'm meant to steer a ship in a storm.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government is taking decisive action so the benefits reach every household.”
- **Thinks:** The government wouldn't be ready, every option would be too slow or costly for someone, and he'd feel the weight of getting it wrong.
- **Does:** Works around the clock on windfall taxes, public stakes in AI firms and an income floor, keeps savings safe, and tells his kids to study what they love.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, steering a ship in a storm.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Anil:** I'd say we need a new social contract. That's the phrase ministers would use, and for once I'd mean it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Anil:** Now there's no part of the workforce to point to as safe. Our whole retraining logic was 'move people from desks to hands-on work.' That's gone. I'd honestly think a basic income goes from fringe idea to necessity. Personally I'm fine. The country isn't.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Anil:** Rewrite the strategy from scratch around income support and public ownership of the gains. Push for robot deployment rules in care homes. Probably work myself into the ground for a year.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Anil:** Alarmed. Comfortable at home, frightened at work.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need a new social contract, and for once I'd mean it.”
- **Thinks:** The plan to move people from desks to hands-on work collapses, and a basic income becomes a necessity. He's fine personally; the country isn't.
- **Does:** Rewrites the strategy around income support and public ownership of gains, pushes rules on care-home robots, and works flat out.
- **Feels:** Alarmed: comfortable at home, frightened at work.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
