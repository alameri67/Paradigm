# P075 · Fiona Walsh

Kindergarten teacher · Educational services

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Fiona. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Fiona:** Of course! I've got about half an hour before pick-up, so we're good.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fiona:** I'd say it's handy for writing newsletters to parents. That's about it in my room.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fiona:** My job is wiping noses, settling fights and teaching four-year-olds to share. No computer is doing that. What bothers me is the kids who arrive already glued to a tablet.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fiona:** Keep my classroom screen-free. Run a little evening session for parents on play and talking at home.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fiona:** Calm about work. A bit sad about the screens.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fiona:** I'd say we need to look after families going through layoffs, and fund childcare properly so parents can retrain. That's where my union would be.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fiona:** My job's fine. But parents losing jobs might pull kids out to save money, and our numbers drop. And the AI apps for little kids would be everywhere. That's what really worries me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fiona:** Talk to my director about keeping places for kids whose parents lose work, maybe on lower fees. Keep pushing the screen-free message with parents.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fiona:** A bit worried, more for the families.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fiona:** I'd say it's wrong that house prices boom while families lose their jobs. The gains should pay for things like childcare, schools and decent pay for carers.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fiona:** Physical jobs are untouched, so I'm still needed. But we rent, and booming house prices mean the rent goes up. With a little one and almost no savings, that's the scary part, not the AI.

**Interviewer:** You said 'we rent'. Is buying something you'd still hope for in that picture?

**Fiona:** Not really, no. I'd let that dream go. I'd just want a lease that doesn't jump every year.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fiona:** Ask for a pay rise, or move to a better-paid centre, because there'd be plenty of work. Talk with my partner about moving somewhere cheaper. Join the union campaign.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fiona:** Safe at work. Squeezed at home.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's wrong that house prices boom while families lose jobs. The gains should pay for childcare, schools and carers.”
- **Thinks:** She's still needed, but booming prices push up the rent, and with a child and almost no savings that's the scary part.
- **Does:** Asks for a raise or moves to a better-paid centre, talks about moving somewhere cheaper and joins the union campaign.
- **Feels:** Safe at work, squeezed at home.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Fiona:** I'd say, absolutely not with small children. A robot can't love a child. I'd say that loudly.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Fiona:** Care homes on your list surprised me. If they put robots there, someone will try it in nurseries to save money. I don't think parents would accept it for little ones. I hope not. I'm good at my job, but good doesn't always win against cheap.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Fiona:** Get involved with the union and the parents' groups early, to push for rules on robots around young children. Keep saving whatever I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Fiona:** Shaken. The first time this has felt close.
