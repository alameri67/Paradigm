# N06 · Zara Khan

Undergraduate, computer science · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making the time, Zara. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group. We don't sell anything or campaign for anyone. Nothing you tell me today will be linked to your name. I'll read you five short pictures of how AI might change things over the next five years, and ask how you'd react to each.

**Zara:** Of course. Timing's good, actually. I'm in my second year of computer science and I keep going back and forth on switching to medicine. This is pretty much the question I lie awake over.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at uni, to friends, or online?

**Zara:** I'd say it's a great tool and people panicking about it were wrong. Coders who use it well just get faster.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Zara:** Honestly I'd be relieved. I'd stay in CS, finish the degree and stop doing the maths on medicine every night.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your studies and career choices? Be as specific as you can.

**Zara:** Apply for summer internships, keep my grades up and build a couple of proper projects. Drop the medicine idea, or at least park it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Zara:** Relieved. A bit embarrassed I worried so much.

### Summary

- **Says:** “AI is a great tool and the people panicking were wrong; coders who use it just get faster.”
- **Thinks:** She'd stay in computer science and finish the degree, and stop weighing medicine every night.
- **Does:** Applies for summer internships, keeps her grades up, builds a couple of solid projects and parks the medicine idea.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at uni, to friends, or online?

**Zara:** I'd say graduates are going to have a harder time, and universities should be honest about that with first-years.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Zara:** Fewer junior jobs is the one that gets me. I'm good, but so is everyone in my year. Ten years is slow for the world, but I graduate in two.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your studies and career choices? Be as specific as you can.

**Zara:** Push hard for an internship this summer, because that's how you get in the door. Take a couple of AI modules. Keep the medicine option alive by checking what I'd need to switch.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Zara:** Uneasy. Like I'm on a ladder with the bottom rungs being sawn off.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Graduates will have a harder time, and universities should be honest about that.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer junior jobs is the part that hurts, since she graduates in two years and her whole year is strong.
- **Does:** Pushes hard for a summer internship, takes AI modules and checks what she'd need to switch to medicine.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, like the bottom rungs are being sawn off.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at uni, to friends, or online?

**Zara:** I'd say we need rules on how fast companies can cut staff, and support for the people they cut. I'd probably say it in a seminar and get into an argument.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Zara:** Most routine coding is routine office work, really. The new AI jobs would go to people with masters' or PhDs. Medicine starts to look much safer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your studies and career choices? Be as specific as you can.

**Zara:** Book a meeting with an admissions adviser for medicine. Start the entrance exam prep. Talk to my parents, because it's five or six more years and more loans.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Zara:** Anxious. I'd be deciding my whole life in a few months.

**Interviewer:** You said medicine looks safer. What do you base that on?

**Zara:** Mostly that patients want a person, and the rules for doctors are strict. I could be wrong. AI is already good at reading scans.

### Summary

- **Says:** “There should be rules on how fast companies cut staff, plus support for those who are cut.”
- **Thinks:** Routine coding is office work too, the new AI jobs would go to people with higher degrees, and medicine looks safer.
- **Does:** Meets a medicine admissions adviser, starts entrance exam prep and talks to her parents about more years and more loans.
- **Feels:** Anxious, deciding her whole life in months.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at uni, to friends, or online?

**Zara:** I'd say the gains have to be shared. If share prices boom while graduates can't get work, that's a political failure, not a tech one.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Zara:** My CS degree would be worth very little in two or three years. I'd switch. Doctors have a human, hands-on side and legal protection, so they'd hold on longer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your studies and career choices? Be as specific as you can.

**Zara:** Switch to medicine this year, even if it means starting again. Take the extra loans. My parents would help if their savings are doing well with prices up.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Zara:** Scared, but at least I'd be moving. Doing nothing would be worse.

### Summary

- **Says:** “If shares boom while graduates can't get work, that's a political failure, not a tech one.”
- **Thinks:** Her computer science degree would be worth little within a few years, while doctors' hands-on work and legal protection would last longer.
- **Does:** Switches to medicine this year, takes the extra loans and leans on her parents for help.
- **Feels:** Scared, but better to be moving than stuck.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at uni, to friends, or online?

**Zara:** I'd say this is the point where we need a completely new deal on work and income. Not just retraining.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Zara:** Robots in care homes, not hospitals, so doctors still look okay. If I've already switched, I'd feel I made the right call. My worry would be more about my friends still in CS.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your studies and career choices? Be as specific as you can.

**Zara:** Stick with medicine. Maybe lean towards something very hands-on, like surgery or emergency care.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Zara:** Anxious, but steadier than last time. I'd have a plan.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need a completely new deal on work and income, not just retraining.”
- **Thinks:** Doctors still look relatively safe, so switching would feel right; she'd worry more for her friends in computer science.
- **Does:** Sticks with medicine and leans towards hands-on fields like surgery or emergency care.
- **Feels:** Anxious, but steadier with a plan.
- **Mood:** Anxious
