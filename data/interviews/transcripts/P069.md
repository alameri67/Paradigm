# P069 · Tom Fischer

Security guard · Administrative and support services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Tom. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Tom:** Alright. Keep it short if you can.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tom:** Don't really talk about it. If it came up, I'd say they're already using the smart cameras and cutting guards.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tom:** Two guys on my night shift became one last year. The cameras watch the screens, I walk the floors. If it stays like that, I'll manage.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tom:** Nothing different. Turn up, do my rounds, don't give them a reason.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tom:** Bit wary. Nothing new.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They're already using the smart cameras and cutting guards.”
- **Thinks:** His night shift went from two guards to one. If it stays like that, he'll manage.
- **Does:** Turns up, does his rounds and gives them no reason to cut him.
- **Feels:** A bit wary, nothing new.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tom:** I'd say what I always say. Companies cut corners, then act surprised when something gets stolen.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tom:** Slow is fine. A camera can't stop someone. You still need a body there.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tom:** Same. Maybe get my licence for the bigger sites, because they pay a bit more.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tom:** Fine, mostly.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Companies cut corners, then act surprised when something gets stolen.”
- **Thinks:** A camera can't stop someone. You still need a person there.
- **Does:** Looks at getting a licence for bigger, better-paid sites.
- **Feels:** Fine, mostly.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tom:** I'd say the politicians are useless and always have been. Whatever they argue about, it won't be guys like me.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tom:** Office people getting laid off isn't my problem. Might mean fewer offices to guard, though. Some of my sites are office blocks.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tom:** Ask to be moved to a warehouse or a shop site, not an office one. Put a bit by.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tom:** Same as always. Keeping an eye out.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The politicians are useless, and whatever they argue about won't help guys like me.”
- **Thinks:** Office layoffs aren't his problem, though fewer offices might mean fewer sites to guard.
- **Does:** Asks to move from office sites to warehouse or shop sites and puts a bit of money aside.
- **Feels:** Same as always, keeping an eye out.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tom:** I'd say, see what happens when people lose their jobs and the rich get richer? Trouble. And that's where I come in.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tom:** Honestly, more crime and more protests means more work for security. Rich people with booming houses want guards. Might be good for me, weird as that sounds.

**Interviewer:** You said it might even be good for you. Does that sit right with you?

**Tom:** It's not nice, no. But I didn't make the mess. I just guard it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tom:** Pick up extra shifts. Maybe go for a private security job with a wealthy estate or a gated place.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tom:** Pretty steady. Might even do alright.

### Summary

- **Says:** “When people lose jobs and the rich get richer, you get trouble. That's where I come in.”
- **Thinks:** More crime and protests mean more security work, and wealthy homeowners want guards.
- **Does:** Picks up extra shifts and goes for private security on a wealthy estate.
- **Feels:** Pretty steady. Might even do alright.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tom:** I'd say this is going too far. Somebody should stop it. People need to work for a living.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tom:** Patrol robots. I've seen videos of them already. If they can walk a car park at night and call the police, nobody pays me. A camera couldn't chase anyone. A robot can. That's it for guards.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tom:** Look for anything that still needs a person. Bouncer, maybe, or driving. Cut everything I spend that isn't rent and food. I'm single, so it's just me, at least.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tom:** Bad. Didn't think it'd get to me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is going too far. People need to work for a living.”
- **Thinks:** Patrol robots that walk car parks and call the police would end paying guards. That's it for his job.
- **Does:** Looks for work that still needs a person, like door work or driving, and cuts spending to rent and food.
- **Feels:** Bad. He didn't think it would get to him.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
