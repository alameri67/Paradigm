# P094 · Kim Nguyen

Nail technician · Other services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Kim, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Kim:** Okay, sure. I'm between clients, so I can talk now.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kim:** I'd say it's fine. I use it to make nail art ideas sometimes. Customers show me pictures from the AI and I copy them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kim:** Nothing really changes for me. People still want their nails done. Those painting machines in the mall, they only do simple colours.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kim:** Same. Work six days, send money to my parents, save a little for my daughter. Maybe learn the new gel extensions.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kim:** Okay. Normal.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's fine. Customers show me AI nail art pictures and I copy them.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes; people still want nails done and the mall machines only do simple colours.
- **Does:** Works six days, sends money to her parents, saves for her daughter, and learns gel extensions.
- **Feels:** Okay, normal.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kim:** I'd say people will always want to be pampered. A machine can't massage your hands.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kim:** The kiosks get better slowly. Maybe in ten years they do the art too. But I'm good at detail work and my regulars ask for me. I think I'm okay.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kim:** Get better at the hard designs, the hand-painted ones, so I'm worth more. Maybe post my work online to get new clients.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kim:** Mostly okay. A small worry.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People will always want to be pampered. A machine can't massage your hands.”
- **Thinks:** The kiosks may slowly learn art too, but her detail work and loyal regulars should keep her okay.
- **Does:** Builds skill in hand-painted designs and posts her work online for new clients.
- **Feels:** Mostly okay, a small worry.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kim:** I'd say it's hard for families. A lot of my customers work in offices. I'd listen to them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kim:** When people lose jobs, nails are the first thing they stop. It's extra, not needed. I'd lose some clients. My husband and I have nothing saved, and my parents need money every month.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kim:** Offer cheaper basic service, keep everyone coming even if less often. Work more Sundays. Tell my parents we may send less for a while.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kim:** Uneasy. Money is always tight already.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's hard for families. Many of my customers work in offices, and I'd listen to them.”
- **Thinks:** Nails are the first thing people cut when they lose work, and her family has no savings while her parents need monthly support.
- **Does:** Offers a cheaper basic service, works more Sundays, and warns her parents she may send less.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, money already tight.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kim:** I'd say the government should help families, not only the rich. But people should still work, that's important.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kim:** My customers losing good jobs is bad for me. Some rich ladies would come more, maybe. But in my area, most are normal people. I think I'd lose a third of my money.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kim:** Ask to move to a salon in a richer area. Do house calls for older ladies. Cut our spending, no eating out, no new things for my daughter except school.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kim:** Worried. I think about it at night.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Government should help families, not only the rich. But people should still work.”
- **Thinks:** Her mostly ordinary clients would lose good jobs, maybe costing her a third of her income.
- **Does:** Seeks a salon in a richer area, offers house calls to older women, and cuts family spending.
- **Feels:** Worried, thinks about it at night.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kim:** I'd say no robot should touch my hands. I'd tell customers that real people still do it better.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kim:** But I worry. If the robot arm can cook and build, the nail machine can paint gel and do art too. It would be cheap, and in the mall. Customers with no job would pick cheap. My whole family depends on my hands.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kim:** Learn something more, maybe eyelashes or skin care, things that need a person. Talk to my husband about taking any extra work he can. Stop sending money to my parents for a while, which would hurt them.

**Interviewer:** Lashes and skin care, do you think a machine couldn't do those too?

**Kim:** I don't know. Maybe. I hope it takes longer, because it's close to the face. People get nervous. That gives me some time.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kim:** Scared. For my daughter and my parents.

### Summary

- **Says:** “No robot should touch my hands. Real people still do it better.”
- **Thinks:** If robot arms can cook and build, cheap mall machines can do gel and art too, and her whole family depends on her hands.
- **Does:** Learns lash or skin care work, asks her husband to take extra work, and pauses money to her parents.
- **Feels:** Scared, for her daughter and parents.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
