# N10 · Dorothy Evans

Retiree, ex-retail, state pension only · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thank you for seeing me, Dorothy. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, not the council or any company, and we're not selling anything. What you tell me stays private and won't have your name on it. I'll describe five ways this AI might change things over the next five years and ask what you think.

**Dorothy:** That's all right, love. I'm glad you said you're not selling anything, you can't be too careful these days. I don't use any of this AI. I wouldn't know where to start.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it to your neighbours, to friends, or online?

**Dorothy:** I'd say I don't trust it. There was a lady on the news who got a phone call that sounded just like her grandson, and it was one of these computer voices. She lost her savings.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dorothy:** If it's only a search engine, it doesn't touch me much. It's the scams I worry about. And prices, always prices.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your day-to-day life? Be as specific as you can.

**Dorothy:** Same as now. Shop at the cheap supermarket, keep the heating low. Not answer the phone to numbers I don't know.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dorothy:** A bit nervous. Mostly about the phone.

### Summary

- **Says:** “She doesn't trust it, after hearing on the news of a woman scammed by a fake voice of her grandson.”
- **Thinks:** A better search tool barely touches her; scams and prices are what worry her.
- **Does:** Carries on shopping at the cheap supermarket, keeps the heating low and ignores calls from unknown numbers.
- **Feels:** A bit nervous, mostly about the phone.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it to your neighbours, to friends, or online?

**Dorothy:** I'd say it's a shame for the youngsters. When I started on the tills you learned on the job. They don't give young ones a chance now.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dorothy:** For me it's the same as before. I'm retired. As long as my pension comes in and the prices don't jump, I'll manage.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your day-to-day life? Be as specific as you can.

**Dorothy:** Nothing different. Maybe ask at the library if they do a class on spotting scams.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dorothy:** Sorry for the young ones. Otherwise all right.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a shame for young people, who used to learn on the job and now don't get a chance.”
- **Thinks:** As long as her pension comes in and prices hold, she'll manage.
- **Does:** Changes little, and asks the library about a class on spotting scams.
- **Feels:** Sorry for the young ones, otherwise all right.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it to your neighbours, to friends, or online?

**Dorothy:** I'd say they should look after the people losing their jobs. Not just argue on the telly. Ordinary people always pay.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dorothy:** If lots of people are out of work, there's less money in the pot for pensions, isn't there? That's what I'd worry about. I don't know how it works exactly, but that's how it sounds.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your day-to-day life? Be as specific as you can.

**Dorothy:** Cut back a bit more, just in case. Put a little by each week if I can, even if it’s only small change. Keep an eye on the news about pensions.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dorothy:** Worried in the background. Like when the bills come.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about the AI itself in this picture that worries you, or is it more the money?

**Dorothy:** The money, mostly. And the phone calls. If these assistants can do things on their own, can they ring me up and pretend to be the bank? That frightens me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They should look after people losing their jobs instead of arguing on TV, because ordinary people always pay.”
- **Thinks:** With many people out of work, she fears there's less money for pensions, though she isn't sure how it works, and she fears AI pretending to be her bank.
- **Does:** Cuts back a little more, tries to put a little by each week and watches the news about pensions.
- **Feels:** Worried in the background, like when bills arrive.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it to your neighbours, to friends, or online?

**Dorothy:** I'd say the rich are getting richer off it and the rest of us get nothing. Same as it ever was. They should share it out fairly.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dorothy:** Prices are what I'd watch. If houses and shares go up, everything else seems to go up with them. My pension doesn't.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your day-to-day life? Be as specific as you can.

**Dorothy:** Buy cheaper food, turn the heating off in the bedroom. Ask the advice centre if there's any help I'm missing. Stay off the phone.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dorothy:** Anxious. I can't do much about any of it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The rich are getting richer from it and should share it out fairly.”
- **Thinks:** She'd expect everyday prices to rise along with houses and shares, while her pension doesn't.
- **Does:** Buys cheaper food, stops heating the bedroom, asks the advice centre about help she's missing and avoids the phone.
- **Feels:** Anxious, unable to do much about it.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it to your neighbours, to friends, or online?

**Dorothy:** I'd say it's not right. Robots in care homes? Old people need a kind face, not a machine.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dorothy:** That's me in a few years, maybe. I don't want to be looked after by a robot. And the girls on the tills, there'd be nothing for them.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your day-to-day life? Be as specific as you can.

**Dorothy:** Talk to my doctor about staying in my own home as long as I can. Stick with my friends at the church. Keep counting every penny.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dorothy:** Frightened, to be honest. Of ending up alone with a machine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots in care homes isn't right; old people need a kind face, not a machine.”
- **Thinks:** She may need care herself in a few years and doesn't want a robot doing it, and shop workers would have nothing left.
- **Does:** Talks to her doctor about staying in her own home, sticks close to her church friends and keeps counting every penny.
- **Feels:** Frightened of ending up alone with a machine.
- **Mood:** Anxious
