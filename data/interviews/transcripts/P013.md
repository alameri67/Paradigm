# P013 · Yuki Tanaka

Billing and customer service agent · Utilities

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Yuki. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Yuki:** Okay. Thanks for keeping it anonymous. I get a bit nervous about this stuff, so sorry if I ramble.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Yuki:** I'd say it's already changing my job, even if it's 'just a tool'. The chatbot takes about a third of our calls now.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Yuki:** Even this version isn't great for me. The tool keeps getting better at what I do, and they don't need a person for a simple bill question. I'm only safe until the next upgrade.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Yuki:** Start looking at night courses. Something like bookkeeping or admin in a hospital. Try to save even a little, which is hard with my rent.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Yuki:** Uneasy. Even the calm version doesn't feel calm.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Yuki:** I'd say companies should still hire young people. You can't pull the ladder up behind you.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Yuki:** I'm basically a beginner. Fewer beginner jobs means if I lose this one, there's nothing to step into. Ten years sounds slow, but my kind of call is the easy task they'd automate first.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Yuki:** Apply for a complaints role or team lead, the harder calls the bot can't handle. Actually start a night course, not just look at one.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Yuki:** Anxious. Like I'm on a timer.

### Summary

- **Thinks:** My calls are the easy task they'd automate first, and there'd be no beginner job to step into.
- **Does:** Applies for complaints or team lead roles and starts a night course.

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Yuki:** I'd say this is a choice companies are making, not the weather. They should share the savings, and we need stronger unions and a real safety net. I'd post about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Yuki:** That's my job gone. Routine office work is exactly what I do. And people like me don't have savings to ride it out.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Yuki:** Look for a new job straight away while I still have one. Sign up for any free retraining. Ask my flatmate if we can find somewhere cheaper. Join whatever campaign is pushing for a basic income.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Yuki:** Sick to my stomach. I'd barely sleep.

**Interviewer:** The card mentioned new AI-related jobs. Do you see yourself in one?

**Yuki:** Maybe? I don't know what they'd want. Probably people with degrees. I'd try anyway.

## S3 · Fast

Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. **Yuki:** I'd say it's obscene that share prices boom while people lose everything. Tax the gains. I'd be at the protests.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Yuki:** I'd lose my job, and so would everyone I'd be up against for the next one. There'd be thousands of us. Rent goes up with the boom. I don't see where I land.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Yuki:** Take any job I can get. A café, a shop, a warehouse. Find a cheaper room, even far out. Go to every protest.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Yuki:** Panicked. Like there's no exit.

Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Yuki:** Same as before, louder. Tax the gains and pay people, now.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Yuki:** In the last one, my backup was a physical job. Café, warehouse, care work. If robots take those too, there's no backup at all. I'd be one of millions with nothing to offer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Yuki:** Honestly, I don't know. Apply for every support scheme going. Pool money with friends. Keep protesting, because it's the only thing that might change anything.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Yuki:** Hopeless. Even the backup plan is gone.

**Interviewer:** You said protesting is the only thing that might change anything. Do you believe it would?

**Yuki:** I have to. Otherwise I'm just waiting.
