# P064 · Patricia Gomez

Executive assistant to the CEO · Management of companies

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Patricia. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Patricia:** That's fine. I keep a lot of secrets for a living, so I appreciate you keeping mine.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Patricia:** I'd say it's helpful for the small stuff. The calendar tool books meetings, and I fix what it gets wrong.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Patricia:** It doesn't know that he hates meetings before nine, or that the chairman's wife is unwell and he should call. I know that. That's my job, really.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Patricia:** Let it handle the routine bookings. Keep the important things, like the board and his family matters, in my own hands.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Patricia:** Calm. It's a tool, and I'm the one using it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It helps with the small stuff. The calendar tool books meetings and I fix its mistakes.”
- **Thinks:** The tool doesn't know the people and the private details she knows, and that's her real job.
- **Does:** Lets AI do routine bookings and keeps board and family matters in her own hands.
- **Feels:** Calm. It's a tool and she's the one using it.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Patricia:** I'd say a good assistant is about trust, and you can't download trust.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Patricia:** Slow is fine. I'll be one of the last people this touches. The younger assistants on other floors might not get replaced when they leave, though.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Patricia:** Nothing much. Maybe let the IT people show me the new tools properly, which I've been putting off.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Patricia:** Comfortable. Steady.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A good assistant is about trust, and you can't download trust.”
- **Thinks:** She'll be among the last it touches, though junior assistants may not be replaced.
- **Does:** Finally lets IT show her the new tools properly.
- **Feels:** Comfortable and steady.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Patricia:** I'd say what I always say. I'm here for him and the company, and we'll get through it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Patricia:** The agents could do most of my day, if I'm honest. Diary, travel, letters, notes. What they can't do is be discreet in a room or calm him down before a hard meeting. As long as he's the CEO, I think I'm safe.

**Interviewer:** You said 'as long as he's the CEO'. What happens if he leaves?

**Patricia:** Then I'm an expensive assistant with a computer that can do most of my job. A new CEO brings their own person, or no person. I try not to think about that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Patricia:** Learn the agent tools properly so I'm the one running them, not competing with them. Put a bit more aside for my daughter's college. Keep my head down.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Patricia:** A little worried, underneath.

### Summary

- **Says:** “I'm here for him and the company, and we'll get through it.”
- **Thinks:** Agents could do most of her tasks, but not her discretion. She's safe while her CEO stays, and exposed if he leaves.
- **Does:** Learns to run the agent tools herself and saves more for her daughter's college.
- **Feels:** A little worried underneath.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Patricia:** I'd say loyalty still counts for something. Companies that forget their people will regret it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Patricia:** If the whole office shrinks, the CEO might need me more, as the one person who knows how everything fits together. Or the board might replace him with someone cheaper. My job is tied to one man. That's what scares me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Patricia:** Write down everything I know so I'm valuable even to a new boss. Ask him directly, quietly, where I stand. Cut back at home and stop dipping into savings.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Patricia:** Nervous. I'm good at my job, but it's tied to one person.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Loyalty still counts. Companies that forget their people will regret it.”
- **Thinks:** Her job is tied to one man. He might need her more, or the board might replace him.
- **Does:** Writes down everything she knows, asks her boss quietly where she stands and cuts back at home.
- **Feels:** Nervous. Good at her job, but tied to one person.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Patricia:** I'd say it's sad. People need work. It's not just money, it's dignity.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Patricia:** For me it's no different from the last one. Robots don't do my job. But my daughter would be starting work around then, and I'd always told her to have a practical skill as a backup. That advice wouldn't hold anymore.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Patricia:** Same as before. Talk to my daughter about what she wants to study, and help her pick something with people at the centre of it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Patricia:** Worried, more for my daughter than me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's sad. People need work. It's dignity, not just money.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes for her own job, but her advice to her daughter about having a practical backup no longer holds.
- **Does:** Keeps the same plan and helps her daughter choose study with people at the centre of it.
- **Feels:** Worried, more for her daughter than herself.
- **Mood:** Anxious
