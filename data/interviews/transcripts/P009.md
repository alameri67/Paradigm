# P009 · Marta Kowalski

Site HR and safety compliance manager · Mining, oil and gas

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Marta. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Marta:** Thank you. I'm glad it's private. Some of this I wouldn't say at work.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marta:** I'd say it's a helpful tool for paperwork. I use it now and then to tidy up a policy document.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marta:** If it stays like this, I'm relieved. I'm five years from my pension. A tool that helps me, I can live with.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marta:** Keep doing my job well. Go to the training the company offers. Keep my head down.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marta:** Calm. Relieved, really.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a helpful tool for paperwork.”
- **Thinks:** If it stays like this, I'm relieved. I'm five years from my pension.
- **Does:** Keeps doing her job well and goes to the training the company offers.
- **Feels:** Calm. Relieved, really.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marta:** I'd say the company will handle it carefully. They've always been decent with long-serving staff.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marta:** Some tasks disappearing is exactly my worry. So much of my work is forms, records, incident reports. If those go, what's left is a smaller job, and smaller jobs get merged.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marta:** Make myself the person who knows how the new tools work, even though that doesn't come naturally to me. Ask head office, carefully, what's planned.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marta:** Uneasy. A quiet worry I carry around.

**Interviewer:** You said the company has been decent. Do you trust it to stay that way?

**Marta:** I want to. I've seen them be kind. But I've also written the letters when they weren't, so I know how it goes.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The company will handle it carefully. They've always been decent with long-serving staff.”
- **Thinks:** So much of my work is forms and records. If those go, my job shrinks, and small jobs get merged.
- **Does:** Learns the new tools even though it doesn't come naturally, and asks head office carefully what's planned.
- **Feels:** A quiet worry she carries around.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marta:** I'd say we need to look after people through this, and the company should retrain before it lets anyone go. I'd say it kindly, because that's my job.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marta:** Routine office work is my job. HR records, compliance paperwork. I know what 'streamlined' means because I've used the word on other people. Five years is a long time to hang on.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marta:** Sit down with a financial adviser and work out my pension if I'm let go at 58, 59 or 60. Put my hand up for anything safety-related out on site, because that's harder to automate than paperwork.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marta:** Frightened. I'd be lying awake doing the sums.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need to look after people, and the company should retrain before it lets anyone go.”
- **Thinks:** Routine office work is my job. I know what 'streamlined' means because I've said it to other people.
- **Does:** Works out her pension with a financial adviser for an early exit, and volunteers for hands-on safety work on site.
- **Feels:** Frightened. Lying awake doing the sums.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marta:** Publicly, the same. We look after our people. Retraining. Fairness.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marta:** This is when I'm let go. If AI matches professionals, a site HR manager with ordinary skills isn't kept. I'd be 58 or 59 in a town with one big employer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marta:** Ask about an early retirement package before they offer me a worse one. Cut back hard. Maybe see whether I could move closer to my son, if he'd have me nearby.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marta:** Like the ground giving way, slowly.

**Interviewer:** Would you believe that public line while you said it?

**Marta:** No. But I'd say it anyway. People need someone to stay calm.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We look after our people. Retraining. Fairness.”
- **Thinks:** This is when I'm let go. An ordinary HR manager isn't kept, and I'd be nearly 60 in a one-employer town.
- **Does:** Asks about an early retirement package before a worse offer comes, cuts back hard and thinks about moving closer to her son.
- **Feels:** Like the ground giving way, slowly.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Marta:** I'd say robots on site need more safety rules, not fewer, and someone has to write them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Marta:** There's a small hope in this one. Robots and people working on the same site is a safety problem, and safety is half my job. But if the crew is mostly robots, there isn't much HR left. I think it's still the end of my job.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Marta:** Get any certificate going in robot safety, even at my age. Keep the early retirement plan ready.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Marta:** Still frightened. A thin bit of hope in it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots on site need more safety rules, not fewer, and someone has to write them.”
- **Thinks:** Robot safety gives me a small hope, but with a mostly robot crew there's little HR left. It's still the end of my job.
- **Does:** Goes after a robot safety certificate and keeps her early retirement plan ready.
- **Feels:** Still frightened, with a thin bit of hope.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
