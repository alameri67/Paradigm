# P092 · Mike Sullivan

Auto mechanic · Other services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Mike, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Mike:** Yeah, sure. Mind if I wipe my hands first? Okay, go.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mike:** I'd say we already use it. The scan tool tells you the code, half the time it's wrong, and then you actually fix the car.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mike:** Doesn't change much. The bigger thing for me is EVs. Fewer oil changes, fewer parts to wear out. That's the real squeeze.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mike:** Do the EV high-voltage course the shop's been talking about. Keep my head down. Coach my boy's ball team like usual.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mike:** Fine. Same as any year.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We already use it. The scan tool gives a code, it's often wrong, and then you actually fix the car.”
- **Thinks:** AI changes little; electric cars needing less work are the real squeeze.
- **Does:** Takes the shop's electric vehicle high-voltage course and carries on as usual.
- **Feels:** Fine, same as any year.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mike:** I'd say a computer can tell you what's wrong, it can't take the wheel off. People like that are always gonna need us.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mike:** Slow is fine. The diagnostic stuff gets smarter and maybe a guy with less experience can do what I do. That bugs me a bit. But ten years, I'll adapt.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mike:** Get the EV certificate done. Maybe ask the owner about doing more of the hybrid and EV work, since that's where it's going.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mike:** Calm. A little itch, nothing more.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A computer can tell you what's wrong, but it can't take the wheel off.”
- **Thinks:** Smarter diagnostics might let less experienced mechanics do his work, which bugs him, but he has ten years to adapt.
- **Does:** Finishes the EV certificate and asks to take on more hybrid and EV work.
- **Feels:** Calm, a little itch.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mike:** I'd say maybe those office guys should've learned a real trade. I'm not saying it to be mean. It's just true.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mike:** Thing is, the software side of my job is the part that's growing. If the computer diagnoses better than me, the shop needs fewer senior guys and more parts swappers. And I'm average, not the best guy in the shop.

**Interviewer:** You said you're average. That's an honest thing to say.

**Mike:** Yeah, well. Jimmy in the next bay is better than me. I'm steady, I show up. That counts for something, but not everything.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mike:** Make sure I'm the one in the shop who knows the new systems best. Put a bit more into savings. No new truck this year.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mike:** Bit uneasy. I don't love it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Those office guys should've learned a real trade. Not being mean, it's just true.”
- **Thinks:** If software diagnoses better than him, the shop needs fewer experienced mechanics, and he knows he's average.
- **Does:** Works to know the new systems best in the shop, saves more, and skips a new truck.
- **Feels:** A bit uneasy.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mike:** I'd say see, it's the guys in suits getting hit this time. Working people built this country and we're still standing.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mike:** I'd feel okay about my job, honestly. People still drive, cars still break. The suits losing jobs might fix their own cars less, or keep old cars longer, which is good for me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mike:** Keep working. Maybe more hours, more old cars coming through. Put money aside since everything's going crazy in the news.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mike:** Uneasy about the country. Okay about me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's the guys in suits getting hit this time. Working people are still standing.”
- **Thinks:** His job feels okay, and people keeping old cars longer could mean more work.
- **Does:** Works more hours on older cars and saves as the news gets wild.
- **Feels:** Uneasy about the country, okay about himself.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Mike:** I'd say this is wrong. You take the hands-on work from people, you take their pride. I'd say it at the bar and I'd mean it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Mike:** If a robot can frame a house and work a kitchen, it can swap brakes. Maybe not tomorrow, but the big chains would try. I've got three kids and no other skill. I'm not going to college at 40.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Mike:** Talk to the shop owner about where we stand. Look into the stuff a robot can't easily do, older cars, classic restorations, mobile repairs at people's houses. Cut spending, top up the savings.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Mike:** Worried. First time I've felt like the floor isn't solid.

### Summary

- **Says:** “You take the hands-on work from people, you take their pride.”
- **Thinks:** If robots can frame houses and work kitchens, chains will try them for brakes too; with three kids and no other skill, he won't retrain at college.
- **Does:** Asks the shop owner where they stand, explores classic restoration and mobile repairs, and cuts spending.
- **Feels:** Worried, the floor no longer feels solid.
- **Mood:** Anxious
