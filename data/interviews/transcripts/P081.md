# P081 · Leo Martins

Freelance illustrator and concept artist · Arts, entertainment and recreation

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Leo, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Leo:** Okay. Fair warning, I've got strong feelings about this.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leo:** I'd say that's already a lie for people like me. "Handy tool" is what they call it while it eats my commissions.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leo:** Even if nothing else changes, my work is already gone. Clients who used to pay me for a cover now type a prompt and pay me to fix the hands. For a third of the price.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leo:** Keep doing the fix-up jobs I hate, keep posting my own work, and pick up bar shifts. I won't touch the generators. That's a line for me.

**Interviewer:** You said you won't use the generators. Even if it meant more work?

**Leo:** Even then. They were trained on my art without asking. Using them would be helping someone rob me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leo:** Bitter. Even the calm version isn't calm for me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They call it a handy tool while it eats my commissions.”
- **Thinks:** His work is already gone: clients prompt an image and pay him a third to fix it.
- **Does:** Takes fix-up jobs he hates, keeps posting his own work, picks up bar shifts, and refuses to use generators.
- **Feels:** Bitter, even the calm version isn't calm.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leo:** I'd say it's theft, slow or fast. They scraped millions of artists' work without asking and now they're selling it back to our clients.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leo:** Fewer beginners hired means studios stop taking on juniors like me. I already can't get a staff job. Slow just means a slow death for anyone without a name.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leo:** Join the artists' class action groups, push for opt-out and pay for training data. Keep the bar shifts. Maybe teach a drawing class if a community centre will have me.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leo:** Furious and kind of worn out.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's theft, slow or fast. They scraped artists' work without asking and sell it back to our clients.”
- **Thinks:** Studios hiring fewer juniors means a slow death for artists like him without a name.
- **Does:** Joins artists' legal action groups, campaigns for pay for training data, keeps bar shifts and looks for a teaching class.
- **Feels:** Furious and worn out.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leo:** I'd say welcome to our world. Artists were the warning. Now maybe office people will listen.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leo:** It's grim, but I'd feel less alone. My work's still gone, though. And more laid-off people chasing the bar and café shifts I live on.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leo:** Show up at every protest and union meeting going. Fight for whatever bill puts a tax on the AI firms. Hold onto the bar job tight.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leo:** Angry, with a tiny bit of hope that people finally get it.

- **Does:** Goes to protests and union meetings, backs taxes on AI firms, and holds tight to his bar job.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leo:** I'd say the gains belong to everyone whose work they were built on. That means a basic income, public ownership, all of it. I'd be loud about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leo:** For me it doesn't change much. I'm already at the bottom. If anything, a political crisis is the only chance things get fixed. But I'd be skint, and rent would go up with house prices.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leo:** Work as many bar and warehouse shifts as I can get. Physical stuff at least pays. Organise with other artists. Move back in with my mum if rent goes up again.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leo:** Scared about money, but fired up.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains belong to everyone whose work AI was built on: basic income, public ownership, all of it.”
- **Thinks:** He's already at the bottom, and a political crisis may be the only chance of a fix, but rising rents would leave him broke.
- **Does:** Works as many bar and warehouse shifts as possible, organises with other artists, and might move back in with his mum.
- **Feels:** Scared about money, but fired up.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Leo:** I'd say they've taken the art, the office jobs, and now the bar and the warehouse. What's left? Who is this for?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Leo:** That was my backup. The physical jobs were where I was going to hide while I made art on the side. If robots take those, I have nothing. No savings, no family money.

**Interviewer:** In the last one you sounded almost hopeful about the crisis. What changed?

**Leo:** In that one, there was still somewhere for me to stand while we fought. In this one there isn't. Hope's hard on an empty stomach.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Leo:** I honestly don't know. Apply for whatever support exists. Squeeze into my mum's place. Keep drawing, because it's the only thing that's mine.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Leo:** Panicked. Like the walls are closing in.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They've taken the art, the office jobs, and now the bar and the warehouse. Who is this for?”
- **Thinks:** Physical jobs were his hiding place while he made art; without them he has nothing, with no savings or family money.
- **Does:** Doesn't know; would apply for any support, move in with his mum, and keep drawing.
- **Feels:** Panicked, walls closing in.
- **Mood:** Panicked
