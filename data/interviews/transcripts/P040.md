# P040 · Ahmed Yusuf

City bus driver · Transportation and warehousing

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Ahmed, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Ahmed:** Hello, hello. Yes, I drive a city bus. Many years now. A few more and then my pension, God willing. Please, ask.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ahmed:** I'd say good for the young people with their phones. I don't use it myself.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ahmed:** For me, nothing. The bus still needs a driver, and the passengers still need someone to say good morning.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ahmed:** The same. Drive my routes, go to the union meetings, count the years.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ahmed:** Peaceful. Nothing to worry about.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Good for the young people with their phones. I don't use it myself.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes. The bus needs a driver and the passengers need someone to say good morning.
- **Does:** Drives his routes, goes to union meetings and counts the years to his pension.
- **Feels:** Peaceful.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ahmed:** I'd say it's sad if companies stop giving young people a chance. Everyone deserves a first job.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ahmed:** Ten years? I'll be retired long before that. The union has a strong agreement with the city. They can't just take my seat.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ahmed:** Nothing changes for me. Maybe I'd tell the young ones in the family to learn a trade.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ahmed:** Calm. This is a problem for the next ones, not me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's sad if companies stop giving young people a chance. Everyone deserves a first job.”
- **Thinks:** I'll be retired long before ten years. The union's agreement with the city protects my seat.
- **Does:** Changes nothing at work, and plans to tell the young ones in his family to learn a trade.
- **Feels:** Calm. It's for the next generation.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ahmed:** I'd say the government should step in and protect people. That's what it's for. And the unions should be at the table.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ahmed:** For me, still nothing. I drive a bus, it's not office work. I worry a little for my children, they're grown and working, and I don't know how their jobs would hold up.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ahmed:** I'd ask my children how their work is going and tell them to save. For me, the same routine.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ahmed:** Calm for myself. A small worry for my family.

**Interviewer:** Do you know what kind of work your children do, and whether this would touch it?

**Ahmed:** Some of it is office work, I think, but I'm not sure of the details. They tell me and I don't fully follow, you know how it is.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government should step in and protect people, and the unions should be at the table.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes for me. I worry a little for my grown children, though I'm not sure how their jobs would hold up.
- **Does:** Asks his children how work is going and tells them to save, and keeps his own routine.
- **Feels:** Calm for himself, a small worry for family.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ahmed:** I'd say the gains must be shared. The country should not let a few people take everything. Our leaders will have to find an answer, and I believe they will.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ahmed:** I'm close to pension and my job is physical, so I'm fine. But if my children lose their jobs, they may need help from us, and our savings are not big.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ahmed:** I'd keep driving to the last day I can, maybe a year longer than planned, to put a little more aside in case the children need us.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ahmed:** Uneasy. Not for me. For them.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains must be shared. Our leaders will have to find an answer, and I believe they will.”
- **Thinks:** I'm close to my pension and my job is physical, so I'm fine. But my children may need our help, and our savings are small.
- **Does:** Keeps driving to the last possible day, maybe a year longer than planned, to save more in case his children need help.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, for his children, not himself.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ahmed:** I'd say the union will fight for us, and the government must protect working people. They have before.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ahmed:** Robots in warehouses and kitchens, yes, but a bus in a busy city with old ladies and children? I think that's harder. And even if it comes, I'll be gone on my pension. The union will make sure of that much.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ahmed:** The same as before. Drive, save, retire. Maybe go to a few more union meetings to see what they're planning.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ahmed:** Uneasy, but steady. I trust that I'll make it to the end.

**Interviewer:** Hands-on work isn't safe in this picture. What makes you sure your bus is different?

**Ahmed:** I'm not sure. Nobody can be sure. But I have only a few years left, and I believe the union can hold the line for that long. After that, it's not my seat to worry about.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The union will fight for us, and the government must protect working people. They have before.”
- **Thinks:** A city bus with old ladies and children is harder for a robot. Even if it comes, the union can hold the line until my pension.
- **Does:** Keeps driving, saving and planning to retire, and goes to more union meetings to hear their plans.
- **Feels:** Uneasy but steady, trusting he'll make it.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
