# P026 · Paul Anderson

B2B sales rep, industrial supplies · Wholesale trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Paul, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Paul:** Sure, happy to talk. I sell industrial supplies to plants and contractors, so I spend half my life in my truck and the other half on the phone. Fire away.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Paul:** I'd say it's a good tool. I use it to draft quotes and look up specs faster, and that's about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Paul:** Honestly, it means nothing changes for me. Nobody's buying a pallet of bearings because a chatbot asked nicely. They buy from me because I pick up the phone at six in the morning.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Paul:** Same as always. I'd use it a bit more for emails and call notes, and spend the time I save on more site visits.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Paul:** Fine. Pretty normal, really.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a handy tool for quotes and emails, nothing more.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes. People buy from me because I show up and answer the phone.
- **Does:** Uses AI a bit more for emails and call notes, and puts the saved time into more site visits.
- **Feels:** Relaxed, business as usual.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Paul:** I'd say the companies that adopt it smartly will win, and the ones that drag their feet will lose. That's just how markets work.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Paul:** Ten years is a long runway for me. I'm 47, so I can ride most of that out. The kids coming up behind me are the ones who'll have trouble getting a start.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Paul:** I'd get better with the tools so I'm the guy on the team who knows them. And I'd start putting a little more away, maybe ten percent more a month.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Paul:** A little uneasy for younger people, but for me it's manageable.

**Interviewer:** You said the young ones will struggle. Does that touch your own kids at all?

**Paul:** Yeah, actually. My oldest is talking about business school. I'd probably push her toward something where you deal with people face to face. Funny, I hadn't put it together until you asked.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The firms that use it well will win. That's how markets work.”
- **Thinks:** He can ride out ten years, but young people will struggle to get started, maybe including his own kids.
- **Does:** Learns the tools so he's the go-to person on the team, and saves about ten percent more each month.
- **Feels:** Mostly fine, a bit worried for his kids.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Paul:** Publicly I'd say relationships still close deals, and they do. Buyers want someone to call when a shipment goes sideways.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Paul:** Off the record, this is the one I worry about. If the buyer on the other side has a bot that compares prices from fifty suppliers in a second, my handshake counts for a lot less. Procurement is office work.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Paul:** I'd go hard on my top twenty accounts, the ones where I know the plant manager personally. I'd also cut back on eating out and hold off on replacing my truck. With child support and two kids, I can't run thin.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Paul:** Anxious, I'll admit it. Not panicking, but it's sitting in my gut.

**Interviewer:** You said relationships still close deals, but also that a bot could cut you out. Which one do you believe more?

**Paul:** Both, honestly. Relationships close the big, messy deals. The bot takes the routine reorders. Trouble is, the routine reorders pay a lot of my commission.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Relationships still close deals. Buyers want a person to call when things go wrong.”
- **Thinks:** Procurement bots could cut him out of routine reorders, and those pay much of his commission.
- **Does:** Focuses on his top twenty accounts where he knows the plant manager, cuts back on eating out and delays replacing his truck.
- **Feels:** A knot in his gut, but still in control.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Paul:** I'd say the government should keep its hands off and let people adapt. Bailouts and handouts just slow things down.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Paul:** Really, I think a lot of my buyers would be gone, and the ones left would be told to use the bot. If professional buyers lose their jobs, who am I selling to? A commission job with no one to take to lunch.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Paul:** I'd start looking at moving to the selling side of automation equipment, since somebody's going to sell all that kit. I'd talk to my ex about the kids' costs before anything breaks, and I'd stop all extras.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Paul:** Scared, frankly. I don't sleep great when I think about it.

**Interviewer:** You'd want government to stay out, but you'd be in real trouble. Would you want any help at all?

**Paul:** Help with retraining, sure. A cheque for sitting at home, no. I'd rather earn it. But I'd take a hand to get to the next thing.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Government should stay out and let people adapt. Handouts slow everything down.”
- **Thinks:** If professional buyers vanish, he loses the people he sells to. He'd accept retraining help but not cash for nothing.
- **Does:** Looks for sales jobs in automation equipment, talks to his ex about the kids' costs early, and stops all extra spending.
- **Feels:** Scared, losing sleep over it.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Paul:** I'd say whoever's selling those robots is going to make a fortune, and I'd like to be one of them.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Paul:** This one's odd for me. My job's already in trouble from the last picture, so robots don't make it much worse. If anything, every plant I call on would be buying new kit, and that's a sales opening.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Paul:** I'd push hard to get hired by a robotics or automation distributor. I know the factories, I know the buyers who are left. I'd take a pay cut to get in the door.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Paul:** Still worried, but there's a bit of hope in it too.

**Interviewer:** Is that a real plan, or more of a hope?

**Paul:** Half and half. I've got the contacts. Whether they'd hire a 47-year-old supplies guy, I don't know yet.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Whoever sells those robots will make a fortune, and he'd like to be one of them.”
- **Thinks:** Robots don't hurt him much more than the last scenario did. Plants buying new kit could be a sales opening.
- **Does:** Pushes to get hired by an automation distributor, using his plant contacts, and would take a pay cut to get in.
- **Feels:** Worried, with a thread of hope.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
