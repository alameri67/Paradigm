# P065 · Ben Nakamura

Shared-services finance team lead · Management of companies

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Ben. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Ben:** Sure. Can I ask, this really isn't going back to my employer? Okay. Good.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ben:** I'd say we've been automating invoice processing for years. This is just the next step.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ben:** I'm not great with the new tools, to be honest. If it stays small, I can get by. My team knows them better than I do and I lean on them.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ben:** Send a couple of the team on the AI training so they can show the rest of us. Keep things running smoothly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ben:** A bit edgy. I'm always a bit edgy about work.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We've been automating invoice processing for years. This is just the next step.”
- **Thinks:** He isn't good with the new tools and leans on his team, but he can get by if it stays small.
- **Does:** Sends team members on AI training and keeps things running smoothly.
- **Feels:** A bit edgy, as he usually is about work.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ben:** I'd say experience counts. You can't automate twelve years of knowing how the process works.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ben:** Slowly, the process work goes, and what's left is the hard stuff, the exceptions. My team is better at those than me. Someone will notice that eventually.

**Interviewer:** You said someone would notice. Notice what, exactly?

**Ben:** That I got the lead job because I'd been there longest. I'm not the sharpest on the team. I've never said that out loud.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ben:** Try to take a course, maybe in finance systems. I'd probably put it off, though. With two kids, evenings are gone.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ben:** Nervous, in the background.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Experience counts. You can't automate years of knowing how the process works.”
- **Thinks:** As routine work goes, only hard exceptions remain, which his team handles better. He knows he got promoted on time served.
- **Does:** Means to take a finance systems course but would probably put it off.
- **Feels:** Nervous in the background.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ben:** At work I'd say shared services has always adapted and we'll adapt again. I'd sound confident.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ben:** Our whole floor is routine office work. They'd cut the team to a few people plus the agents, and they'd keep the ones who understand the agents. That isn't me. And I have a mortgage and two kids.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ben:** Cancel the holiday. Stop all extra spending. Finally do that systems course, properly this time. Ask my manager what the plan is, though I'm scared of the answer.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ben:** Losing sleep. Snapping at home.

- **Does:** Cancels the holiday, cuts extra spending, does the systems course and asks his manager about the plan.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ben:** I'd probably not say much. Maybe complain online that the people at the top are getting rich off this.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ben:** I'd be gone. Not in the last wave, in the first. Twelve years in one company and nothing anyone would hire me for. The house is worth more, but we still have to pay it off.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ben:** Look into selling the house and moving somewhere cheaper while prices are high. Apply for anything in finance, even junior. My wife might have to go back to full time.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ben:** Panic. Real panic. I don't know what I'd do.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The people at the top are getting rich off this.”
- **Thinks:** He'd go in the first wave, with twelve years at one company and nothing anyone would hire him for.
- **Does:** Looks at selling the house to move somewhere cheaper, applies for any finance job and talks about his wife working full time.
- **Feels:** Real panic. He doesn't know what he'd do.
- **Mood:** Panicked

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ben:** Same, really. Maybe I'd say the government needs to step in, because there's nowhere left to go.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ben:** In the fast one I thought, worst case, I'd drive a delivery van or work in a warehouse. If robots do that too, that's gone. There's no backup at all.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ben:** Sell the house, for sure. Move closer to family so they can help with the kids. Take whatever job shows up first.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ben:** Trapped. Out of options.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government needs to step in, because there's nowhere left to go.”
- **Thinks:** His worst-case fallback was driving a van or warehouse work. With robots, there is no backup at all.
- **Does:** Sells the house, moves closer to family for help with the kids and takes the first job that comes.
- **Feels:** Trapped and out of options.
- **Mood:** Panicked
