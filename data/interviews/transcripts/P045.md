# P045 · Ethan Cole

Freelance video editor and creator · Information

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Ethan, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Ethan:** Yo, yeah, let's do it. I edit videos for clients and I make my own stuff online too. AI's all over my work already, so I've got thoughts.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ethan:** I'd say the tools are honestly sick. I can do in an hour what used to take a day.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ethan:** The catch is the clients know that too. So they pay me like it took an hour. I'm doing more work for about the same money.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ethan:** Take on more clients to make up for the lower rates, and post more of my own videos to grow my channel.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ethan:** Kind of uneasy. Excited about the tools, annoyed about the money.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ethan:** I'd say companies not hiring beginners is wild. Like, where do people learn?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ethan:** I'm freelance, so nobody was hiring me anyway. But small businesses that used to pay me for a simple promo will just make it themselves with an app. The easy jobs go first, and the easy jobs are most of my jobs right now.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ethan:** I'd try to niche down. Maybe music videos or weddings, stuff where people want a real person with taste. And I'd actually set up a proper rate card instead of winging it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ethan:** Anxious. Kind of a slow squeeze.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd 'actually' do the rate card. Is that a habit, winging it?

**Ethan:** Yeah, guilty. I'm better at the creative stuff than the business stuff. This would force me to fix that.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Companies not hiring beginners is wild. Where do people learn?”
- **Thinks:** Small businesses will make simple promos with an app. The easy jobs go first, and they're most of my work right now.
- **Does:** Niches into music videos or weddings where taste matters, and finally sets up a proper rate card.
- **Feels:** Anxious, a slow squeeze.
- **Mood:** Anxious

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ethan:** I'd say tax the AI companies and give everyone a basic income. Artists and creators especially got robbed here.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ethan:** If AI matches skilled pros, it matches skilled editors. Paid client work would mostly dry up. My own channel is the only thing I'd have, and that's tiny. I'd be living at my parents' place with no real income.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ethan:** I'd go all in on my channel for a few months, try every platform, and get a part-time job somewhere physical, like a café or a warehouse, since that's safe in this one.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ethan:** Alarmed. Like the thing I'm good at stopped being worth anything.

**Interviewer:** You said you'd go 'all in' on your channel and also get a part-time job. Which comes first?

**Ethan:** The job, if I'm being real. My parents would make me. The channel is evenings.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Tax the AI companies and give everyone a basic income. Creators especially got robbed.”
- **Thinks:** Paid editing would mostly dry up. My own channel is all I'd have, and it's tiny.
- **Does:** Gets a part-time café or warehouse job first, and grows his channel in the evenings across every platform.
- **Feels:** Like what he's good at stopped being worth anything.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Ethan:** I'd say the whole economy is being handed to a few companies. Everyone should be in the streets about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ethan:** Robots in cafés and warehouses take my backup job. So I'd have nothing paid at all. Weird thing is, if everyone's out of work, maybe more people watch videos. But views aren't money unless someone's buying ads.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ethan:** I'd stay at my parents' and try to keep making content, maybe teach people how to use the AI tools in videos, since everyone would be trying to learn. And sign up for any support going.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ethan:** Alarmed. Still kind of hoping something clicks, though.
