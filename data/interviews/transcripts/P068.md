# P068 · Denise Carter

Branch manager, temp staffing agency · Administrative and support services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Denise. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Denise:** Oh, lovely. I talk to people about work all day, so I'll try not to talk your ear off.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Denise:** I'd say it's handy. We use it to sort CVs and write job ads. Saves my team a lot of typing.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Denise:** It's fine. Our business is people. Factories and warehouses round here still ring me Monday morning wanting twenty pickers by Wednesday.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Denise:** Keep doing what we do. Maybe use the tools to match people to shifts faster.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Denise:** Calm. Nothing to lose sleep over.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's handy. We use it to sort CVs and write job ads.”
- **Thinks:** Her business is people, and local employers still need workers every week.
- **Does:** Carries on and uses AI to match people to shifts faster.
- **Feels:** Calm, nothing to lose sleep over.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Denise:** I'd say companies will always need flexible workers. Busy seasons don't go away.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Denise:** Fewer beginners hired could even help us. Companies try people out through temp work first. Slow change I can work with.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Denise:** Push our try-before-you-hire service. Talk to the local college about placing young people.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Denise:** Pretty good about it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Companies will always need flexible workers. Busy seasons don't go away.”
- **Thinks:** Companies that hire fewer beginners may try them out through temp work first, which helps her.
- **Does:** Pushes a try-before-you-hire service and works with the local college to place young people.
- **Feels:** Pretty good about it.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Denise:** I'd tell clients we're still here for whatever staff they need, office or warehouse. Stay positive, that's my style.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Denise:** Office temps would dry up. Reception, data entry, admin cover, that's maybe a third of our branch. The warehouse and factory side would carry us. I'm not worried for the branch yet, but I'd watch it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Denise:** Shift my team's time towards warehouse and factory clients. Help the office temps we lose find other work, because I know most of them by name.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Denise:** A little uneasy. Nothing I can't manage.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're still here for whatever staff you need, office or warehouse.”
- **Thinks:** Office temp work, about a third of the branch, would dry up, but warehouse and factory work would carry them.
- **Does:** Shifts her team towards warehouse and factory clients and helps lost office temps find other work.
- **Feels:** A little uneasy, nothing she can't manage.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Denise:** I'd say there's no shame in physical work. We'd help anyone retrain, accountant or not.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Denise:** We'd be swamped with people looking for work. Former office workers wanting warehouse shifts. Physical jobs are still there, so we'd be busy. Busy and sad, I think.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Denise:** Hire a couple more staff to handle all the sign-ups. Set up short safety courses so office people can start on the floor quickly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Denise:** Busy and sad. But my feet are under me.

**Interviewer:** Is it hard, being busy because other people are struggling?

**Denise:** It is. But I'd rather be the one helping them get a shift than the one turning them away.

### Summary

- **Says:** “There's no shame in physical work. We'll help anyone retrain.”
- **Thinks:** The branch would be swamped with laid-off office workers wanting warehouse shifts. Busy, but sad.
- **Does:** Hires extra staff for sign-ups and sets up quick safety courses so office workers can start on the floor.
- **Feels:** Busy and sad, but steady on her feet.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Denise:** I'd say we need to slow this down and think of the people. I'm not one for big government, but someone has to do something.

**Interviewer:** You said you're not one for big government. Would you still vote for someone promising to step in?

**Denise:** Probably, yes. I never thought I'd say that. But I'd want it to be jobs, not just handouts.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Denise:** That's my business gone. Warehouses, kitchens, building sites, that's who rings me. If robots fill those shifts, nobody needs a temp agency. The branch closes, and at fifty-five I'm not sure who hires me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Denise:** Talk to head office early about what's coming. Save every penny. Talk with my husband about him working longer. Look at care work, maybe, though even that's on your list.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Denise:** Properly worried now. This one hits home.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We need to slow this down and think of the people. Someone has to do something.”
- **Thinks:** Warehouses, kitchens and building sites are her clients. If robots fill those shifts the branch closes, and at 55 she isn't sure who'd hire her.
- **Does:** Warns head office early, saves every penny, talks with her husband about working longer and looks at care work.
- **Feels:** Properly worried. This one hits home.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
