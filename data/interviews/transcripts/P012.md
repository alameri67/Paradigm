# P012 · Diego Santos

Electrical lineworker · Utilities

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Diego. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Diego:** Sure thing. Ask away.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Diego:** I'd say cool, it helps me look stuff up. It's not climbing poles.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Diego:** Nothing changes. Overtime's good, the data centres keep needing power. I'm busy.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Diego:** Work the overtime, save for the wedding, maybe put a bit toward a house.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Diego:** Good. Pretty good, actually.

### Summary

- **Does:** Works the overtime and saves for the wedding and a house.

- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Diego:** I'd say it'll be a long time before it touches the trades. And the union's got our back anyway.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Diego:** Fewer beginners in offices doesn't hit apprenticeships much. If anything, more young people want trades now. Maybe more competition for the good crews, but I'm good at what I do.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Diego:** Keep stacking overtime. Start on my next certificate so I can run a crew down the road.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Diego:** Fine. Confident.

### Summary

- **Thinks:** More young people might want trades now, but I'm good at what I do.
- **Does:** Keeps stacking overtime and starts on his next certificate so he can run a crew later.
- **Feels:** Fine. Confident.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Diego:** I'd say every one of those AI tools needs a power line, and we're the ones building them. Join a trade.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Diego:** Honestly, this is good for me. More data centres, more demand, more overtime. I feel bad for the office folks, but it's not my fight.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Diego:** Take every storm call and every big build job. Put money down on a house sooner.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Diego:** Pretty good. A bit guilty saying so.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Diego:** I'd say physical work is the backbone and always was. And the union should make sure workers get a share of this boom.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Diego:** The boom means more building, more power, more work for me. House prices going up hurts, though. We're trying to buy, and that's a race we'd lose.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Diego:** Keep working all the overtime. Maybe buy sooner, before prices climb any higher, even if it's a stretch.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Diego:** Mostly good. Annoyed about house prices.

**Interviewer:** Is buying sooner a stretch you're comfortable with?

**Diego:** Not totally. But waiting looks worse. My fiancée and I would have to talk it through.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Physical work is the backbone, and the union should make sure workers share in this boom.”
- **Thinks:** The boom means more work for me. Rising house prices are the part that hurts.
- **Does:** Works all the overtime and talks with his fiancée about buying sooner, even if it's a stretch.

- **Mood:** Calm

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Diego:** I'd say what I always say. No robot climbs a pole in a storm. I'd say it at the union hall and get a cheer.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Diego:** Off the record, storms aren't most of the job. Routine stuff, new lines, swapping gear on a nice day, a robot on a bucket truck could do a lot of that. The storm work stays. The steady overtime might not.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Diego:** Push the union to get in early on how robots get used, so linemen run them instead of losing to them. Get trained on whatever robot gear shows up first. Hold off on a bigger mortgage.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Diego:** Uneasy. Not scared yet. But I'd stop joking about it.

**Interviewer:** You'd tell the union hall no robot climbs a pole. Do you still believe that?

**Diego:** In a storm, yeah. On a sunny Tuesday, not so sure.

### Summary

- **Says:** “No robot climbs a pole in a storm.”
- **Thinks:** Storms aren't most of the job. A robot on a bucket truck could do the routine work, and the steady overtime goes.
- **Does:** Pushes the union to shape how robots are used, gets trained on the first robot gear, and holds off on a bigger mortgage.
- **Feels:** Uneasy. Not scared yet, but done joking.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
