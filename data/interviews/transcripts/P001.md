# P001 · Tomas Varga

Logging crew foreman · Agriculture (logging and farm support)

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Tomas. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Tomas:** Fine by me. I'll tell you straight, I don't know much about this AI business.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tomas:** I'd say what I say now. It's something for the office folks and the kids on their phones. Out here we still cut trees.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tomas:** Honestly, nothing changes for me. Maybe the dispatcher gets a fancier computer. My crew still goes home in one piece, and that's my job.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tomas:** Same as any year. Keep the saws sharp, keep the new guys from doing something stupid, put a bit aside for winter.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tomas:** Like a normal year. Fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's something for the office folks. Out here we still cut trees.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes for me. My job is getting the crew home in one piece.
- **Does:** Carries on as usual: keeps the crew safe and the gear in shape, and puts a bit aside for winter.
- **Feels:** Like a normal year.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tomas:** Slow is fine. Slow means somebody gets to see it coming. Let the companies try it and we'll see if it works.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tomas:** Fewer beginners bothers me a bit. I'm the one who trains them. If the outfit stops hiring young guys, who runs this crew when I'm done?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tomas:** Nothing different in my own work. I'd push the owner to keep taking on one young fella a year, so we're not all old men.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tomas:** A bit sour about the young ones. For me, it's fine.

**Interviewer:** You said slow is fine, but you sound bothered about the young ones. Which is it?

**Tomas:** Both. It's fine for me. It's not great for the kids around here. There's not much else for them to do.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow means somebody sees it coming. Let them try it and we'll see if it works.”
- **Thinks:** It doesn't touch me much, but fewer young hires means nobody to take over the crew.
- **Does:** Keeps working as normal and pushes the owner to keep hiring at least one young worker a year.
- **Feels:** Fine for me, a bit sour about the young ones.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tomas:** Office jobs, layoffs, politicians shouting. I'd say that's what you get for sitting at a desk all day. And keep government handouts out of it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tomas:** Might even be some good in it. Half the paperwork that slows us down comes from offices. If a computer does it faster, fine. As long as nobody tells me how to fell a tree.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tomas:** Not much. If the office starts sending orders through some new app, I'd get my daughter to show me how it works. That's about it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tomas:** Not my fight. Some head-shaking, that's all.

### Summary

- **Says:** “That's what you get for sitting at a desk all day. Keep government handouts out of it.”
- **Thinks:** Maybe less paperwork for us. As long as nobody tells me how to fell a tree.
- **Does:** Gets his daughter to show him any new app the office brings in, and otherwise changes nothing.
- **Feels:** Not my fight. Some head-shaking.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tomas:** I'd say the people in the cities made this mess and now they want a bailout. Physical work is still real work, and maybe folks will finally respect that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tomas:** What bugs me is the political mess. When there's a fight over who gets the money, guys like me end up paying for it, in taxes or at the pump. House prices going up does nothing for a man who isn't selling.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tomas:** Watch my money closer. Hold off on the new truck, keep some cash where I can get at it, fill the fuel tanks when it's cheap.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tomas:** Uneasy. Not about my job. About the country going sideways.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The cities made this mess. Physical work is still real work, and maybe people will finally respect it.”
- **Thinks:** My job's fine. The political mess is what worries me, because guys like me end up paying for it.
- **Does:** Watches money more closely, holds off on a new truck and keeps some cash on hand.
- **Feels:** Uneasy about the country, not about the job.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Tomas:** A robot in the forest? Good luck to it on a wet slope in November. I'd say that at the bar and I'd mean it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Tomas:** Off the record, if they really work, that's my crew. The big machines already took most of the hand-felling. I'm 52. I'm not about to learn to babysit a robot from a screen.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Tomas:** First thing, I'd ask the owner straight out what he's buying. If they're coming, I want to be the one who says where they go and where they don't. Nobody knows this ground better than me, and nobody has a cleaner safety record.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Tomas:** Like somebody's moving the ground under my crew. I don't like it.

**Interviewer:** At the start you called AI office hype. Has that changed?

**Tomas:** It's hype till I see one. But if one turns up on my site and does the work, I'm not going to pretend it didn't.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Good luck to a robot on a wet slope in November.”
- **Thinks:** If they really work, that's my crew gone. I'm 52 and not about to learn to run one from a screen.
- **Does:** Asks the owner straight out what he plans to buy, and pushes to be the one who decides where the robots can work safely.
- **Feels:** Like the ground is moving under my crew.
- **Mood:** Anxious
