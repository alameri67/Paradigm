# P014 · Robert Klein

Water treatment plant manager · Utilities

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Robert. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Robert:** Fine. I'll say up front I don't use these AI things. My grandson keeps telling me I should.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Robert:** I'd say good for those who use it. We run on written procedures here, and they've kept the water safe for decades.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Robert:** Means nothing for me. I've only got a couple of years left. If it stays a tool, the next manager can decide what to do with it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Robert:** Nothing new. Train my deputy, keep the logs tidy, start planning for retirement.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Robert:** Calm. Same as any year.

- **Thinks:** Means nothing for me. The next manager can decide what to do with it.
- **Does:** Trains his deputy, keeps the logs tidy and plans for retirement.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Robert:** I'd say slow is how a water plant should change. Every new thing gets tested before it touches what people drink.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Robert:** I'd be retired before anything big happens. Fewer beginners worries me a little for the plant, since we already struggle to find certified operators.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Robert:** Make sure the written procedures are up to date for whoever comes after me. Keep the trainee operator post funded if I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Robert:** Calm. A bit concerned for the plant after I go.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow is how a water plant should change. Everything gets tested before it touches what people drink.”
- **Thinks:** I'll be retired first. Fewer beginners worries me for the plant, though.
- **Does:** Updates the written procedures for his successor and fights to keep the trainee operator post funded.
- **Feels:** Calm, a bit concerned for the plant.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Robert:** I'd say a water plant is not routine office work, and the public should be glad of that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Robert:** They'd probably want an agent on our reports and compliance paperwork. Fine. The regulator will still want a human name on it. I'm not worried for me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Robert:** Let the deputy try the new tools if head office wants it. Make sure every change goes through our procedures first.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Robert:** Calm. Not my battle.

**Interviewer:** You don't use AI yourself. How do you judge what it can and can't do?

**Robert:** I don't, really. I go by what I read and what my grandson tells me. That's why I want everything tested.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Robert:** I'd say we need to slow down and check what we're doing. Things that move this fast tend to break.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Robert:** Our savings and the house go up, so I'd retire on time and be fine. What bothers me is a plant like mine run by software with fewer people watching. My grandchildren drink that water.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Robert:** Retire on schedule, maybe a little early. Before I go, put it in writing that the plant keeps a qualified operator on every shift.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Robert:** Uneasy. For the town more than me.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Robert:** I'd say a robot shouldn't be the last line of defence for what comes out of the tap.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Robert:** For me, nothing changes. I'm out the door. For my grandchildren, I don't know what work they'll find, and that worries me more than anything about the plant.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Robert:** Retire. Spend more time with the grandchildren. Help my kids out a bit if their jobs get shaky.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Robert:** Uneasy. For the grandkids, mostly.
