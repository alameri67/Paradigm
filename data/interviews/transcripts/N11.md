# N11 · Kenji Watanabe

Early retiree, ex-tech executive · Outside paid work

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making the time, Kenji. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group. We don't sell anything or campaign for anyone. Nothing you tell me today will be linked to your name. I'll read you five short pictures of how AI might change things over the next five years, and ask how you'd react to each.

**Kenji:** Fine. I'll be direct with you. I ran tech companies for most of my career, I use these tools every day, and I invest in AI start-ups. I think it should be moving faster than it is.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it to other investors or online, to friends, or online?

**Kenji:** I'd say it was a missed chance. People were too cautious, regulators were too slow, and we settled for a fancy search box.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kenji:** Some of my start-ups would fail. I'd lose money, though nothing I can't afford. Mostly I'd be disappointed. I think the technology is better than that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your time? Be as specific as you can.

**Kenji:** Cut the weaker investments, back the two or three teams with real products. Spend more time with my wife, maybe travel.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kenji:** Frustrated. Bored, almost.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it to other investors or online, to friends, or online?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kenji:** My investments would do fine over time. But I'm 66. I'd like to see what this can do while I'm still around.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your time? Be as specific as you can.

**Kenji:** Put money into companies that help big firms adopt AI faster. Mentor a couple of founders.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kenji:** Impatient. Not worried.

**Interviewer:** You said not worried. Does the part about companies hiring fewer beginners bother you at all?

**Kenji:** Not much. People adapt. I started in a world without the internet and I adapted. Young people are better at it than we were.

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. **Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your time? Be as specific as you can.

**Kenji:** Put more into the start-ups building these assistants. Maybe fund a retraining company, because that's a market too.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kenji:** Good. Energised, actually.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it to other investors or online, to friends, or online?

**Kenji:** I'd say the gains are real and they'll spread through cheaper goods and services. Taxing success to pay people not to work is a mistake.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kenji:** Off the record? I'd be extremely rich. My worry is the politics. A crisis over who gets the gains usually ends with people like me getting a big tax bill.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your time? Be as specific as you can.

**Kenji:** Take some money off the table, spread it across countries. Talk to my lawyers about tax. Keep backing founders.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kenji:** Fine. Watchful about the politics.

**Interviewer:** You said the gains would spread. But you also expect a political crisis. Why would there be one, if they spread?

**Kenji:** Because spreading takes time and people are angry now. I understand the anger. I just don't think the answer is to take it from the people who built things.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The gains will spread through cheaper goods and services, and taxing success to pay people not to work is a mistake.”
- **Thinks:** He'd be extremely rich, and his worry is a political crisis ending in a big tax bill for people like him.

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it to other investors or online, to friends, or online?

**Kenji:** I'd say this is the biggest jump in what humans can produce in history. We'll have to work out the politics, but don't stop it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kenji:** Robots I'm less close to, but I'd invest. What bothers me is unrest. When everyone's work is threatened at once, things can get ugly, and money doesn't protect you from a broken society.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with your money and your time? Be as specific as you can.

**Kenji:** Invest in robotics. And, if I'm honest, think about where we live and how secure it is. Maybe give money to some kind of transition fund, on my terms.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kenji:** Uneasy. Not about money. About people getting angry.

### Summary

- **Says:** “This is the biggest jump in human productivity in history; the politics must be worked out, but it shouldn't be stopped.”
- **Thinks:** He'd invest in robots, but with everyone's work threatened at once he fears unrest that money can't protect him from.
- **Does:** Invests in robotics, reconsiders where he lives and how secure it is, and may fund a transition scheme on his own terms.
- **Feels:** Uneasy about people's anger, not money.
