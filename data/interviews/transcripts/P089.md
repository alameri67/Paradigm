# P089 · Antonio Ricci

Owner and chef, family restaurant · Accommodation and food services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Antonio, thanks for making time. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we don't work for any company, union or party. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say out loud and what you really think.

**Antonio:** Sit, sit. You eat while we talk? No? Okay, next time.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Antonio:** I'd say computers don't make ragù. My mother's recipe, my hands, forty years. That's what people come for.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Antonio:** Nothing changes. My problems are the price of oil, the price of cheese, the rent and the bank. Not AI.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Antonio:** Same as always. Open six days, keep my kids in the kitchen, pay the bank every month. Maybe my daughter puts the menu online with one of those tools.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Antonio:** Fine. Busy, but fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Computers don't make ragù. My mother's recipe, my hands, forty years.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes; his worries are food costs, rent and the bank, not AI.
- **Does:** Opens six days, keeps his kids in the kitchen, pays the bank, and lets his daughter put the menu online.
- **Feels:** Fine, busy.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Antonio:** I'd say good food is good food. The young people will come back to real things.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Antonio:** Slow, I don't feel it. Maybe the booking, the ordering of supplies, the accounts get easier. That I'd like, if somebody else sets it up.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Antonio:** Let my son try some ordering software if it's cheap. No new loans for it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Antonio:** Calm. It's not my world.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Good food is good food. Young people will come back to real things.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change he won't feel, though easier bookings and accounts would be welcome if someone else sets them up.
- **Does:** Lets his son try cheap ordering software but takes no new loans.
- **Feels:** Calm, not his world.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Antonio:** I'd say people should work hard and learn a trade, like I did. The government argues, nothing happens. Same as always.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Antonio:** Half my lunch customers are from the offices, the bank, the insurance, the town hall. If they're laid off, they bring sandwiches from home. Thin margins get thinner.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Antonio:** Make a cheaper lunch menu. Talk to the bank about the payments before it's late. Maybe cut one day, close Mondays.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Antonio:** A little worried. I've seen hard years before.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People should work hard and learn a trade. The government argues and nothing happens.”
- **Thinks:** Half his lunch trade is office workers; if they're laid off his thin margins get thinner.
- **Does:** Adds a cheaper lunch menu, talks to the bank early, and considers closing Mondays.
- **Feels:** A little worried, has seen hard years before.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Antonio:** I'd say a town needs its restaurant. We'll be here. We always are.

**Interviewer:** You say you'll always be here, but you sound worried. Which one is it?

**Antonio:** Both. I say it because people need to hear it, and I need to hear it. Inside, I count the money every night.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Antonio:** But between us, my kids work here. If the offices empty out, the town has less money, and I feed my whole family from this place. The debt worries me. The shares going up, that's not me. I have no shares.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Antonio:** Cut costs everywhere. Me and my wife take less pay so the kids keep theirs. Do catering for weddings and funerals, more people do those at home now. Talk to the bank again.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Antonio:** Worried. For my children more than me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A town needs its restaurant. We'll be here. We always are.”
- **Thinks:** With his kids on the payroll, business debt and no shares, a poorer town threatens the whole family.
- **Does:** Cuts costs, takes less pay himself so the kids keep theirs, pushes catering, and talks to the bank.
- **Feels:** Worried, more for his children.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Antonio:** I'd say nobody wants food from a robot. Food is love. You can taste the hands.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Antonio:** The robot won't come to my kitchen. I don't have the money and I don't want it. But the chains will have them, and they'll sell pasta for half my price. People with no work will choose cheap. That's the danger.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Antonio:** Make us the place for real food, the family place, birthdays, Sundays. Raise some prices, not lower. Tell my son to learn the business side, so he can run it when it's harder. Keep the debt from growing.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Antonio:** Anxious. I'm proud, but pride doesn't pay the bank.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Nobody wants food from a robot. Food is love; you can taste the hands.”
- **Thinks:** No robot will come to his kitchen, but robot chains would sell pasta at half his price to people with no work.
- **Does:** Positions the restaurant for family occasions, raises some prices, has his son learn the business side, and keeps debt from growing.
- **Feels:** Anxious and proud, but pride doesn't pay the bank.
- **Mood:** Anxious
