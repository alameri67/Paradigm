# P030 · Hassan Raza

Customs broker, self-employed · Wholesale trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Hassan, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Hassan:** Sure. I'm a customs broker, on my own. I get importers' goods through customs: the forms, the tariff codes, the rules. It's all paperwork, which is why I'm curious about your questions.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Hassan:** I'd say it's a great help. I already use it every day to check tariff codes and draft letters to clients.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hassan:** It means I can take on more clients without hiring anyone. Good for me.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Hassan:** I'd sign up five or six more small importers and set up templates so the AI drafts the routine filings for me to check.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hassan:** Good. Confident.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a great help. I use it every day for tariff codes and client letters.”
- **Thinks:** I can take on more clients without hiring anyone. Good for me.
- **Does:** Signs up five or six more small importers and sets up templates so AI drafts routine filings for him to check.
- **Feels:** Confident.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Hassan:** I'd say smart brokers will use it and lazy ones will lose out. Same as when everything went online.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hassan:** Ten years gives me time. The tasks go, but the licence and the responsibility stay with me. If a shipment's held at the port, the client wants a person who answers for it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Hassan:** I'd keep building my own setup so one of me can do the work of two. I'd also stop thinking about hiring an assistant, which I'd been planning.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hassan:** Mostly calm. I'm watching it closely, though.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Smart brokers will use it and lazy ones will lose out, like when everything went online.”
- **Thinks:** Tasks go, but the licence and the responsibility stay with me. Clients want a person to answer for a held shipment.
- **Does:** Builds his own AI setup to do the work of two, and drops plans to hire an assistant.
- **Feels:** Calm but watchful.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Hassan:** I'd say the rules on who can file with customs need to stay strict. You need a licensed person signing off, or mistakes will be very expensive.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hassan:** Privately, this is the threat side of the coin. If an AI assistant can file entries on its own, big importers will do it in-house, and the software companies will offer brokerage for a monthly fee. My small clients will be the last to switch, but they'll switch.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Hassan:** I'd go after the messy work: food, chemicals, goods with special permits, disputes with customs. That's where the AI makes mistakes. And I'd build three months of extra savings, because with three kids I can't be caught short.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hassan:** Anxious. I can see it coming toward me.

**Interviewer:** You'd argue for strict rules in public. Is that partly to protect your own business?

**Hassan:** Of course it is. I won't pretend. But it's also true. A wrong code can cost a client a fortune.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The rules on who can file with customs must stay strict. A licensed person should sign off.”
- **Thinks:** Big importers will file in-house and software firms will sell brokerage by the month. My small clients will switch last, but they'll switch.
- **Does:** Shifts toward hard cases like food, chemicals, permits and disputes, and builds three more months of savings.
- **Feels:** Anxious, seeing it come toward him.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Hassan:** I'd say people need time to adjust. Help with retraining, lower taxes on small business, not just big cheques from the government.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hassan:** If AI matches a skilled broker in two or three years, my business is mostly finished. The hard cases won't be enough to feed five people. I'm good at what I do, but good isn't enough when the machine is good too and costs nothing.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Hassan:** I'd try to partner with a software firm as their licensed person, the one who signs off. I'd cut the family budget hard and look at whether my wife can pick up more work. And I'd start learning something new, maybe trade compliance for big firms.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hassan:** Alarmed. Three kids, a city that's expensive, and my whole trade disappearing.

**Interviewer:** You've built your life around paperwork. Is there any part of this you'd still use to your advantage?

**Hassan:** Maybe. If I move fast I could be the human face on an AI service. But a lot of brokers will try the same thing. There aren't many seats.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People need time to adjust: retraining and lower taxes on small business, not just big government cheques.”
- **Thinks:** My business would mostly be finished. Being good isn't enough when the machine is good too and costs nothing.
- **Does:** Tries to partner with a software firm as their licensed sign-off, cuts the family budget hard and starts learning trade compliance.
- **Feels:** Alarmed for his three kids and his trade.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Hassan:** Same as before. Give people time and training. Don't let the big tech firms write all the rules.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Hassan:** The robots don't add much for me. My trade was already in trouble. What it does take away is the backup plan. I'd half thought of driving or warehouse work if it came to that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Hassan:** Same as the last one, but I wouldn't count on any physical fallback. I'd push harder to get a seat with a software firm, and I'd teach my oldest to think hard about what he studies.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Hassan:** Still alarmed. A bit more trapped.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Give people time and training, and don't let big tech write the rules.”
- **Thinks:** Robots add little to my own risk, but they take away the physical work I'd half thought of as a fallback.
- **Does:** Pushes harder for a role with a software firm, drops any physical-work fallback and talks to his oldest about what to study.
- **Feels:** Alarmed, and more trapped.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
