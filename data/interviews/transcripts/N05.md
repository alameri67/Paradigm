# N05 · Aiden Kowal

High school student · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hi Aiden, thanks for doing this. I'm with the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, and we're not linked to your school, any company or the government. What you say won't be tied to your name. I'll describe five ways AI could change things over the next five years and ask how you'd react.

**Aiden:** Yeah, cool. I use AI pretty much every day, mostly for homework, not gonna lie. I want to make games when I'm older.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at school, to friends, or online?

**Aiden:** I'd say it's basically what we have now. Everyone at school already uses it for essays. Teachers pretend they can tell.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Aiden:** That's fine for me. I'd still go do game design at uni or college and get a job at a studio.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with school and your plans for after? Be as specific as you can.

**Aiden:** Keep messing around in Unity at weekends, try to finish a small game. Probably keep using AI for the boring homework.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Aiden:** Pretty chill. Nothing changes.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's basically what we have now, and everyone at school already uses it for essays.”
- **Thinks:** His plan to study game design and join a studio still works.
- **Does:** Keeps building games in Unity at weekends, aims to finish a small one, and keeps using AI for dull homework.
- **Feels:** Pretty chill, nothing changes.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at school, to friends, or online?

**Aiden:** I'd say it sucks for people starting out, I guess. But ten years is ages.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Aiden:** Fewer beginner jobs is kind of bad, because that's me in a few years. But if I have a game out already, I'm not really a beginner, right?

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with school and your plans for after? Be as specific as you can.

**Aiden:** Actually try to finish a game and put it online, so I've got something to show. Maybe join a game jam, where you make a game in a weekend.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Aiden:** A bit of a nudge to get going, I guess. Not stressed.

**Interviewer:** You said it's bad for you, but also that you're not stressed. How do those fit?

**Aiden:** I mean, it's a while away. And I reckon I'll figure it out. My dad says I leave everything to the last minute, which, fair.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's tough for people starting out, but ten years is ages.”
- **Thinks:** Fewer beginner jobs would hit him in a few years, but a released game might mean he's not a real beginner.
- **Does:** Tries to finish a game and put it online, and joins a game jam to build something to show.
- **Feels:** A nudge to get going, not stressed.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at school, to friends, or online?

**Aiden:** I'd say AI agents are kind of sick, honestly. But I'd feel bad for people's parents losing jobs.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Aiden:** Game studios have loads of office people too, so maybe fewer jobs there. But I'd use the agents to build stuff faster. A small team could make a big game.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with school and your plans for after? Be as specific as you can.

**Aiden:** Learn to use the AI coding tools properly, not just for homework. Maybe pick computer science as a subject. Check how my mum and dad's jobs are doing.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Aiden:** Kind of excited and kind of worried at the same time.

### Summary

- **Says:** “AI agents are pretty impressive, but he'd feel bad for parents losing their jobs.”
- **Thinks:** Studios might hire fewer people, but he could use the agents to build games faster with a small team.
- **Does:** Learns AI coding tools properly, considers picking computer science, and keeps an eye on his parents' jobs.
- **Feels:** Excited and worried at the same time.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at school, to friends, or online?

**Aiden:** I'd say it's mad. Like, why go to uni if AI's as good as the people who went? Everyone at school would be asking that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Aiden:** Studios might not need many people at all. But maybe I don't need a studio. I could make games on my own with AI and sell them. That's either amazing or I'm kidding myself.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with school and your plans for after? Be as specific as you can.

**Aiden:** Skip the expensive uni plan, maybe. Make games and put them out, try to get some money from it. Get a weekend job too, maybe something physical, since those are safe.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Aiden:** Kind of nervous. It's my whole plan.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Why go to university if AI's as good as the people who went?”
- **Thinks:** Studios might barely hire, but he might make and sell games alone with AI, though he's not sure if that's realistic.
- **Does:** Rethinks the costly university plan, releases games to earn some money, and gets a physical weekend job.
- **Feels:** Nervous, it's his whole plan.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at school, to friends, or online?

**Aiden:** I'd say, okay, so what's left? Robots doing the building and the cooking too. People would be freaking out.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Aiden:** For me it's the same as last time really, games. But the weekend job idea's gone. And I'd worry about my parents, because if they both lost work we'd have to move.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, with school and your plans for after? Be as specific as you can.

**Aiden:** Keep making games, try to earn something from them. Help out at home more. Probably stop spending money on stuff.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Aiden:** Uneasy. More for my family than me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots doing the building and cooking too, so what's left?”
- **Thinks:** His games plan is unchanged, but the backup weekend job is gone and his family could have to move if his parents lost work.
- **Does:** Keeps making games to earn something, helps more at home and cuts his own spending.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, more for his family than himself.
- **Mood:** Anxious
