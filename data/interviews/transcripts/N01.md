# N01 · Ray Dawson

Unemployed 8 months, laid-off claims processor (finance) · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making the time, Ray. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group. We don't sell anything or campaign for anyone. Nothing you tell me today will be linked to your name. I'll read you five short pictures of how AI might change things over the next five years, and ask how you'd react to each.

**Ray:** Fine. Not sure what I've got to tell you, though. I've been out of work eight months and I'm sending out applications every day. Nobody writes back.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at the job centre, to friends, or online?

**Ray:** I'd say what I say now. The jobs aren't there for guys my age, AI or no AI. Half the places I apply to, the computer bins you before a person ever sees it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ray:** Honestly? If AI's just a search engine, then it wasn't AI that took my job. It was the company cutting corners. Or cheap labour. One week I think one thing, the next week the other.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ray:** Keep applying. Anything going, warehouse, call centre, stacking shelves. I'd sell the car if I had to, but I need it to get to interviews.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ray:** Tired. I'm already in the hole, so this one doesn't make it worse. It just doesn't make it better either.

**Interviewer:** You said AI didn't take your job in this picture. Do you think it took it in real life?

**Ray:** Somebody told me the claims system they brought in did half our work. I never saw it myself. They just said restructure and handed us letters.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The jobs aren't there for guys my age, AI or no AI.”
- **Thinks:** If AI's only a tool, then it was the company cutting costs or cheap labour that did him in, not AI.
- **Does:** Keeps applying for anything going, warehouse, call centre or shelves, and holds on to the car for getting to interviews.
- **Feels:** Tired, already in the hole, no better and no worse.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at the job centre, to friends, or online?

**Ray:** There you go, that's what's happening already. They don't hire the young ones and they don't hire the old ones. Who's left?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ray:** Slow is better than fast for me. Gives me a few years to find something before it gets worse. But I'm 51. Nobody's training me up for anything new.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ray:** Same as now. Applications, the job centre, and I'd ask my brother-in-law about driving work. Maybe a forklift ticket if the job centre pays for it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ray:** Like treading water. You're not going under, but you're not getting anywhere.

### Summary

- **Says:** “They don't hire the young ones and they don't hire the old ones, so who's left?”
- **Thinks:** Slow gives him a few years, but at 51 he doesn't expect anyone to retrain him.
- **Does:** Keeps applying, asks his brother-in-law about driving work and tries to get a forklift licence paid for by the job centre.
- **Feels:** Like treading water.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at the job centre, to friends, or online?

**Ray:** I'd say they should've thought about the people first. Politicians arguing while guys like me can't pay the rent. And they still let everyone in from abroad on top of it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ray:** Off the record? Any office job I could go back to is gone. Claims, admin, the lot. And the new AI jobs won't be for someone with no degree.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ray:** Stop applying for office work. Go for anything with your hands. I'd ask the council about a cheaper place and probably move back in with my sister for a bit.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ray:** Sick to my stomach. I'm not sleeping as it is.

**Interviewer:** You mentioned immigration and AI in the same breath. Which one worries you more in this picture?

**Ray:** Today, the AI. Ask me next week, I'll probably tell you the other. Truth is, I don't know who to blame. I just know it's not me.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Politicians argue while guys like him can't pay rent, and they keep letting people in from abroad on top of it.”
- **Thinks:** Any office job he could return to is gone, and the new AI jobs won't go to someone without a degree.
- **Does:** Stops applying for office work, goes for hands-on jobs, asks the council about cheaper housing and may move in with his sister.
- **Feels:** Sick to his stomach, already not sleeping.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at the job centre, to friends, or online?

**Ray:** I'd say the people at the top are getting rich off it and the rest of us get nothing. House prices going up? I can't even afford rent.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ray:** If the office folk are all losing their jobs, they'll all be after the warehouse jobs too. Younger, with degrees. I'd be at the back of the queue again.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ray:** Grab the first hands-on job I can, nights, whatever. Get a forklift licence any way I can. Not turn anything down.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ray:** Scared, if I'm honest. But at least there'd still be some kind of work out there.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The people at the top get rich off it and the rest get nothing.”
- **Thinks:** Laid-off office workers will crowd into the warehouse jobs he's counting on, and he'd be at the back of the queue.
- **Does:** Takes the first hands-on job he can get, nights included, and gets a forklift licence any way he can.
- **Feels:** Scared, but there's still some work out there.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at the job centre, to friends, or online?

**Ray:** I'd say it's finished, isn't it. Robots in the warehouses as well. What are we meant to do, sit at home?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Ray:** Then there's nothing for me. I couldn't do the office work and now I can't do the lifting either. I'd be on benefits till the pension, if there is one.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Ray:** I don't know. Sign on, move in with my sister for good, sell the car. I wouldn't know where to start.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Ray:** Like the floor's gone. No way out.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's finished, robots in the warehouses as well, so what are people meant to do?”
- **Thinks:** With office and hands-on work both gone, he sees nothing left for him but benefits until pension age.
- **Does:** Signs on for benefits, moves in with his sister for good and sells the car, without a clear plan beyond that.
- **Feels:** Like the floor's gone, with no way out.
- **Mood:** Panicked
