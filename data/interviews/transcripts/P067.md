# P067 · Jason Park

Outsourced call centre agent · Administrative and support services

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Jason. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Jason:** Yeah, fine. Honestly, you're asking the right guy. I watch this happen every week.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jason:** I'd say, "handy tool"? Come sit in my call centre. The voice bots took three desks near me last month.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jason:** Even if it stops here, it's already too late for jobs like mine. It doesn't need to get smarter to answer "where's my parcel".

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jason:** Keep my head down, hit my numbers, take the angry calls the bot can't handle. Pay a bit off the credit card if I can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jason:** Already anxious. This one isn't the future for me, it's now.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Handy tool? The voice bots took three desks near me last month.”
- **Thinks:** It's already too late for jobs like his. AI doesn't need to get smarter to answer simple customer calls.
- **Does:** Hits his numbers, takes the angry calls the bot can't handle and pays down his credit card where he can.
- **Feels:** Already anxious. For him this is now, not the future.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jason:** I'd say slow for who? It's not slow in call centres.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jason:** Slow might buy me a couple of years. They'd keep a few of us for the hard calls. But there's nothing else in this town, so when it goes, it goes.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jason:** Look at an online course, maybe IT help desk. Keep living with my parents and throw everything at the debt.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jason:** Same knot in my stomach. Maybe a bit looser.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow for who? It's not slow in call centres.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change might buy him a couple of years, but there's nothing else in his town when it goes.
- **Does:** Looks at an online IT help desk course, stays with his parents and puts everything into the debt.
- **Feels:** Same knot in his stomach, a little looser.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jason:** I'd be online saying it straight: companies fire people to please shareholders, then politicians shout and do nothing. Tax the bots. Give people a basic income.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jason:** My job's done. Office work with a script is the first thing to go. And the new AI jobs? They're not coming to my town, and they're not for people with a two-year degree.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jason:** Sign up for whatever unemployment help there is. Apply for jobs in the city an hour away. Get my parents to help me look at the debt, which I've been too ashamed to show them.

**Interviewer:** You said moving to the city. Is that realistic with the debt?

**Jason:** Not really. Rent there would eat me alive. More likely I'd drive in every day and hope the car holds up.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jason:** Scared. Angry too. Mostly scared.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Companies fire people to please shareholders while politicians shout. Tax the bots and give people a basic income.”
- **Thinks:** His job is done, and the new AI jobs won't come to his town or to people with a two-year degree.
- **Does:** Signs up for unemployment help, applies for city jobs he'd have to drive to, and shows his parents the debt.
- **Feels:** Scared and angry, mostly scared.
- **Mood:** Alarmed

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jason:** I'd go to protests, probably. Share prices booming while people like me lose everything? That's the whole system telling you who it's for.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jason:** I'd be unemployed with debt in a town with no jobs. Physical work's still there, so maybe a warehouse, but even that's not in my town. I'd be thirty-something living in my childhood bedroom with no way out.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jason:** Take any warehouse or delivery shift I can reach. Look into debt relief. Join a group pushing for a basic income.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jason:** Like drowning slowly.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Share prices boom while people like me lose everything. That tells you who the system is for.”
- **Thinks:** He'd be unemployed, in debt, in a town with no jobs, living in his childhood bedroom.
- **Does:** Takes any warehouse or delivery shift he can reach, looks into debt relief and joins a basic income campaign.
- **Feels:** Like drowning slowly.
- **Mood:** Panicked

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Jason:** Honestly? I'd say I told you so. People laughed when call centre guys complained. Now it's coming for the warehouses too.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Jason:** The warehouse shift was my lifeboat. Without it there's nothing. At least now it's so many people that something has to change. Or everything breaks.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Jason:** Push for a basic income, loud. Look at the army. Stay with my parents and hope.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Jason:** Past scared. Just angry and empty.

### Summary

- **Says:** “I told you so. People laughed when call centre workers complained, and now it's the warehouses too.”
- **Thinks:** The warehouse shift was his lifeboat. With so many people hit, either something changes or everything breaks.
- **Does:** Campaigns hard for a basic income, looks at the army and stays with his parents.
- **Feels:** Past scared. Angry and empty.
- **Mood:** Panicked
