# P051 · Kevin Murphy

Residential estate agent · Real estate

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Kevin. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group with no ties to AI companies, employers or government. Everything you tell me stays confidential and won't be linked to your name.

**Kevin:** No worries. Happy to have a chat, as long as you don't mind me checking my phone if a buyer rings.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kevin:** I'd say it's a good tool and leave it there. I use it to write up listings already. Saves me half an hour a house.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kevin:** Honestly, it's already shaving bits off my job. Buyers turn up knowing more than I do about the street. But if that's as far as it goes, I'll live.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kevin:** Same as now. Work the phones, get to every open home, use the tools to save time on the boring bits.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kevin:** Fine, mostly. The market worries me more than the AI does in this one.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a handy tool. It writes my listings and saves me half an hour a house.”
- **Thinks:** The tools already take a bit off what he adds, but he can live with it if it stops there.
- **Does:** Keeps working the phones and open homes, and uses AI for the paperwork.
- **Feels:** Fine. The market worries him more than the tech.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kevin:** I'd tell people the job's about trust. Nobody hands over the keys to their family home because a computer said so.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kevin:** Slow is fine for me. Ten years out I'm 53 and still selling. It's the young ones coming into real estate who'll struggle to get a start.

**Interviewer:** You said earlier the tools already shrink what you add. Does slow really change that?

**Kevin:** Fair point. It still chips away. But chipping away over ten years I can outrun. I've outrun worse markets.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kevin:** Probably nothing different. Maybe I'd try one of those AI follow-up tools so I stop losing leads.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kevin:** Pretty relaxed about it. Slow I can handle.

### Summary

- **Says:** “People don't hand over their family home because a computer said so. It's about trust.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change still chips away at him, but he thinks he can outrun it until he's done.
- **Does:** Tries an AI tool to follow up leads and otherwise carries on.
- **Feels:** Fairly relaxed. Slow he can handle.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kevin:** I'd say good luck getting a robot to talk a nervous couple through their first auction. Then I'd probably have a go at the politicians, because they'll muck it up like they muck everything up.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kevin:** If office people are getting laid off, they're not buying houses. That's my problem. Fewer sales, and the online platforms will push their cheap AI agents harder.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kevin:** Put every spare dollar aside, which isn't many. Push harder on property management leads, because rent rolls pay every month even when sales dry up.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kevin:** Tight in the chest. Commission's lumpy at the best of times.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Good luck getting a computer to calm a nervous couple at their first auction. And don't let the politicians anywhere near it.”
- **Thinks:** Laid-off office workers don't buy houses, and the online platforms will push cheap AI agents harder.
- **Does:** Saves what little he can and chases property management work for steady monthly income.
- **Feels:** Tight in the chest. Commission is lumpy already.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kevin:** Half of me would be saying it's a great time to sell, prices are flying. That's the job. You talk up the market.

**Interviewer:** So in public it's a great market. What would you say to a mate over a beer?

**Kevin:** I'd say the money's good for now and I don't know how long it lasts. My wife's the only one who'd hear more than that.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kevin:** Rising prices mean bigger commissions, sure. But who's buying? Investors with share money, and they'll want to pay one per cent, not two, or do it online with no agent at all.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kevin:** Grab every listing I can while prices are high. I'd stash some of it this time instead of upgrading the car. And I'd look at getting my property management licence sorted properly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kevin:** Odd. Good weeks and scary weeks. Like surfing a wave you know will dump you.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's a great time to sell, prices are flying.”
- **Thinks:** Bigger prices help, but buyers with share money will want to pay less commission or skip the agent altogether.
- **Does:** Grabs every listing while prices are high, saves part of the commission and gets his property management licence sorted.
- **Feels:** Like surfing a wave he knows will dump him.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Kevin:** I'd say the whole country's gone mad. If tradies and nurses are getting pushed out too, there's no one left in my suburb on a steady wage.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Kevin:** My buyers are tradies, nurses, warehouse blokes, young couples. If they're out of work, the suburbs go quiet. The boom's all in the city and among people with shares, and that's not my patch.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Kevin:** Cut back hard at home. Talk to my wife about her picking up more hours. Maybe look at a job with a builder or a bank, anything with a base salary.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Kevin:** Scared, to be honest. Two kids and less than a month in the bank.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The whole country's gone mad. If tradies and nurses get pushed out too, no one in my suburb has a steady wage.”
- **Thinks:** His buyers are tradies, nurses and young couples. If they lose work the suburbs go quiet and the boom passes him by.
- **Does:** Cuts spending at home, talks with his wife about her extra hours and looks for any job with a base salary.
- **Feels:** Scared. Two kids and under a month of savings.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
