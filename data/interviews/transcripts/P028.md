# P028 · Greg Thompson

Owner, family food distributor · Wholesale trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Greg, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Greg:** All right. I've run this business for a long time. Food distribution, trucks, a warehouse, a lot of good people. My son works with me. Ask what you like.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Greg:** I'd say it's my son's toy. He uses it, I don't, and the trucks still leave on time.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Greg:** It means nothing for me. We're in the food business. People eat. A better search engine doesn't change that.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Greg:** Nothing new. Maybe let my son try it on the paperwork if he stops pestering me about it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Greg:** Fine. I don't lose sleep over search engines.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's my son's toy. The trucks still leave on time.”
- **Thinks:** Nothing changes. People eat, and a better search engine doesn't alter that.
- **Does:** Carries on as normal, maybe letting his son try it on the paperwork.
- **Feels:** Untroubled.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Greg:** I'd say slow is fine with me. You don't tear up something that works.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Greg:** If it's ten years, I'm 70 by then and my son's running the place. So it's his problem, really. I just don't want him betting the farm on gadgets.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Greg:** I'd let him run a small trial in the office, with a budget I set. I'd want to see the numbers before he gets another penny.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Greg:** Calm. Slow I can live with.

**Interviewer:** You say it's his problem later. Does any part of you want to be the one who decides?

**Greg:** Well, sure. I built it. Letting go is the hard part, not the computers.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Slow is fine with me. You don't tear up something that works.”
- **Thinks:** In ten years my son runs the place. Letting go is the hard part, not the computers.
- **Does:** Lets his son run a small office trial on a budget he sets, and wants numbers before spending more.
- **Feels:** Calm, though letting go is hard.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Greg:** I'd say we're a family business and we look after our people. We're not going to sack the girls in the office because a computer says so.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Greg:** Between us, if a program can do the ordering and invoicing, my son will show me the numbers and he'll be right. I'd hate it. Some of those people have been with me twenty years.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Greg:** I'd sit down with my son and my office manager and work out who could move to the warehouse or the phones instead of letting anyone go. I'd agree to the software, slowly.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Greg:** Uneasy. Not about money. About my people, and about not being the one calling the shots anymore.

**Interviewer:** You'd say you won't cut office staff, but you think your son's numbers would win. Which way would it go?

**Greg:** I'd keep who I can, move who I can, and not replace anyone who retires. That's how it'd really go.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're a family business. We look after our people and won't sack office staff because a computer says so.”
- **Thinks:** My son's numbers will be right and I'll hate it. I'd keep who I can and not replace anyone who retires.
- **Does:** Works out with his son and office manager who can move to the warehouse or phones, and agrees to the software slowly.
- **Feels:** Uneasy for his people and his control.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Greg:** I'd say the country's lost its mind, but people still need food on the table and trucks to bring it. We'll be here.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Greg:** Honestly, I'd be fine. The property's worth more, the warehouse is worth more, and drivers and pickers aren't touched in this one. My worry is the restaurants and cafés we sell to, if their office customers lose jobs and stop eating out.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Greg:** I'd watch who's late paying their bills and tighten credit terms. I wouldn't sell anything while prices are up. I'd tell my son to focus on schools and hospitals, the steady accounts.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Greg:** Uneasy. The business is solid, but I'd watch every customer closely.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The country's lost its mind, but people still need food on the table and trucks to bring it.”
- **Thinks:** I'd be fine: the property's worth more and my drivers are safe. My worry is restaurants losing their office customers.
- **Does:** Tightens credit terms, holds on to property and steers his son toward steady accounts like schools and hospitals.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, watching every customer.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Greg:** I'd say robots can't build relationships with a chef at 5 a.m. That's what we do.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Greg:** This one hits home. Warehouse robots would be the first thing my son wants. The competitors would buy them, their prices would drop, and I'd have to follow or go under. Then it's my son's business, not mine, and half my guys are out of work.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Greg:** I'd finally let my son take the lead on it. I'd look at selling a share of the business to a bigger firm if the cost of robots is too much. And I'd try to keep the drivers who've been with me longest, for as long as I could.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Greg:** Anxious. It's the first time I'd feel like what I built is slipping out of my hands.

**Interviewer:** You said robots can't build relationships, but you'd consider selling a share. Do you believe the first part?

**Greg:** I want to. But if their prices are fifteen percent lower, the chef will pick the cheaper truck. I know that.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots can't build a relationship with a chef at 5 a.m. That's what we do.”
- **Thinks:** Competitors buy warehouse robots and undercut me. I follow or go under, and then it's my son's business, not mine.
- **Does:** Hands his son the lead on automation, considers selling a share to a bigger firm, and tries to keep his longest-serving drivers.
- **Feels:** What he built is slipping out of his hands.
- **Mood:** Anxious
