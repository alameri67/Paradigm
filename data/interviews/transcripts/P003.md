# P003 · Dale Hoffman

Owner, custom harvesting and spraying service · Agriculture (logging and farm support)

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Dale. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Dale:** Go ahead. I've got a sprayer waiting, so let's keep it moving.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Dale:** Nothing. A better search engine? I already have one of those. It tells me the weather.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dale:** Means nothing. I use it now and then to read a chemical label or check a part number. That's it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Dale:** Run the business. Work out whether that new auto-steer upgrade pays for itself. Argue with the bank, same as every year.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dale:** Fine. Business as usual.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Dale:** Slow is how farming does everything. I'd tell people not to panic, and to keep the government's nose out of it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dale:** Fewer beginners doesn't bother me. I can't find good young operators now anyway. If the machine does more of the thinking, I need fewer of them.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Dale:** Price out the self-driving kits every winter. The day the numbers work, I buy one. Not before.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dale:** Calm. If anything, a bit interested.

**Interviewer:** What does 'the numbers work' mean for you?

**Dale:** Pays back in three seasons or less. I can't wait longer than that. All my money is in iron.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Farming does everything slowly. Don't panic, and keep the government's nose out of it.”
- **Thinks:** I can't find good young operators now. If the machine does more thinking, I need fewer of them.
- **Does:** Prices self-driving kits every winter and buys the day one pays back in three seasons or less.
- **Feels:** Calm, maybe a bit interested.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Dale:** The politicians arguing, that's what I'd complain about. Every time they argue, somebody writes a new rule, and somebody like me has to fill it out.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dale:** If AI does the paperwork, good. Half my headaches are forms. Spray records, permits, insurance. Let the computer fight the computer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dale:** Annoyed at the politics. The tech I'm fine with.

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Dale:** Nobody's entitled to anybody else's gains. You want to tax the people who built something to pay people who lost a desk job? Not on my dime.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dale:** Land going up helps me on paper. But a fight like that only ends one way, with bigger government. That scares me more than any computer.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Dale:** Get my land and equipment in order. Sit down with the accountant about passing things on before some new tax hits. The succession thing I keep putting off, I'd have to face it.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dale:** Uneasy. Not about the work. About the people in charge.

**Interviewer:** You've put off the succession question for a while. Why would this push you to face it?

**Dale:** Because if the rules on handing down a business change, I want it done before they do. And I'm not getting any younger.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Nobody's entitled to somebody else's gains. Not on my dime.”
- **Thinks:** Land prices help me on paper, but a fight like that ends in bigger government. That scares me more than any computer.
- **Does:** Sits down with his accountant to sort out handing on the business before any new tax arrives.
- **Feels:** Uneasy about the people in charge, not the work.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Dale:** Bring them on. If a robot sprayer works all night and never calls in sick, I want one. And don't let the government slow it down.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Dale:** Here's what I wouldn't say out loud. If a farmer can buy his own robot combine, or rent one from the dealer by the acre, why call me? My whole business is that they don't own the big gear.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Dale:** Talk to the dealer about being their operator, the guy who runs their robot fleet in this county. And pay down debt, because I can't be stuck holding old machines nobody wants.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Dale:** Like I'm racing to buy the thing that might put me out of business.

**Interviewer:** So you'd welcome the robots and fear them at the same time?

**Dale:** Yep. Doesn't change what I'd do. You buy or you get bought.

- **Thinks:** If farmers can rent their own robot combine by the acre, they don't need me.
- **Does:** Pitches the dealer on running their robot fleet in the county, and pays down debt so he isn't stuck with old machines.
