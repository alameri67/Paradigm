# N03 · Walter Gibbs

Long-term unemployed, ex-factory worker, on disability benefit · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for having me in, Walter. I'm from the Work Futures Panel. We're an independent research group, nothing to do with the government or the benefits office. Whatever you tell me stays private and won't have your name on it. I'm going to describe five ways computers and this AI stuff might change things over the next five years, and ask what you make of each one.

**Walter:** Not the benefits office? Good. I don't have much to say about AI. I've never used it. I see it on the telly, that's about it.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it down the pub, to friends, or online?

**Walter:** I'd say it's all hype. Every few years it's something new that's going to change the world. Nothing changes for people like me.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Walter:** Doesn't touch me. I'm not working, I'm on the sick. A better search engine won't make my back better.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Day to day, what would change for you?

**Walter:** Nothing. Same as now. Telly, the shop, sign my forms.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Walter:** Couldn't care less, to be honest.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's all hype, nothing changes for people like him.”
- **Thinks:** He's on disability benefit and not working, so a better search tool doesn't touch him.
- **Does:** Carries on as now: TV, the shop and his benefit forms.
- **Feels:** Couldn't care less.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it down the pub, to friends, or online?

**Walter:** Same thing. The young ones can't get jobs now anyway. That's not computers, that's the bosses.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Walter:** Like I said, it doesn't matter to me. I'm done with work. My worry is them cutting the benefit, not robots.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Day to day, what would change for you?

**Walter:** Nothing different. Keep my head down.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Walter:** Not much. Bit sorry for the kids.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Young people can't get jobs now anyway, and that's the bosses, not computers.”
- **Thinks:** He's done with work, so his real worry is benefit cuts, not AI.
- **Does:** Changes nothing and keeps his head down.
- **Feels:** Not much, a bit sorry for the young ones.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it down the pub, to friends, or online?

**Walter:** I'd say it's planned. They want us all on the dole and dependent, then they control everything. Saw a programme about it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Walter:** What it means for me is more people claiming. Then they come after the ones already on it. They'll be looking at my file again.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Day to day, what would change for you?

**Walter:** Make sure my doctor's notes are up to date. I'd keep every letter from the benefits office. Maybe ask the advice centre if I'm safe.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Walter:** Bit worried. They always come for us first.

**Interviewer:** You said it's planned. Who do you think is behind it?

**Walter:** The big companies and the ones in charge. I don't know exactly. You just get a feeling they're not telling us everything.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It's planned, to get everyone on the dole and dependent so they control everything.”
- **Thinks:** More people claiming means the benefits office will come after people already on it, like him.
- **Does:** Gets his doctor's notes up to date, keeps every benefit letter and asks the advice centre whether he's safe.
- **Feels:** A bit worried, they always come for people like him first.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it down the pub, to friends, or online?

**Walter:** Told you so. The rich get richer, house prices go through the roof, and the rest of us get crumbs. Same as always.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Walter:** If all them office people are on benefits, there won't be enough to go round. I could lose mine. I've got nothing else.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Day to day, what would change for you?

**Walter:** Cut back on the gas and the food shop. Go to the food bank if I have to. Keep my papers in order.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Walter:** Worried. I don't sleep well anyway, this wouldn't help.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The rich get richer, house prices go through the roof, and the rest get crumbs, same as always.”
- **Thinks:** With so many office workers claiming, there may not be enough to go round and he could lose his benefit.
- **Does:** Cuts back on heating and food, uses the food bank if needed and keeps his papers in order.
- **Feels:** Worried, sleeping even worse.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it down the pub, to friends, or online?

**Walter:** Robots in the factories, I'd say that's been coming for years. That's what did for my old place, machines. Now it's everywhere.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Walter:** My mates still on the line would be finished. I'm out of it already, so it's more about them. And the benefits again, there'd be millions on it.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Day to day, what would change for you?

**Walter:** Same as before, cut back, hang on. Maybe check on a couple of the lads from work.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Walter:** Sad for the lads. For me, worried, but I'm used to being worried.

**Interviewer:** You said machines did for your old factory. Was that something you saw, or something you heard?

**Walter:** Bit of both. They brought new machines in and we got laid off a year after. Nobody said why.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Robots in the factories have been coming for years; machines did for his old place, and now it's everywhere.”
- **Thinks:** His friends still in factories would be finished, and with millions claiming his benefit is at risk again.
- **Does:** Keeps cutting back and hangs on, and checks in on a couple of old workmates.
- **Feels:** Sad for his old mates, worried, but used to it.
- **Mood:** Anxious
