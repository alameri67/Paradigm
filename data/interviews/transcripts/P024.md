# P024 · Claire Dubois

Plant manager, food processing · Manufacturing

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making time, Claire. I'm a researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how people see changes at work, and nothing you tell me will be linked to your name.

**Claire:** Thank you. I talk to the board and the local paper in one voice, so it's a relief to use another.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Claire:** I'd say we're using AI to improve planning and food safety, and we're proud of our people. That's what I say now.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Claire:** Even in this version, the board keeps pushing automation on the line to protect margins. AI as a tool doesn't stop that. It just doesn't speed it up.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Claire:** Keep doing what I'm doing. Bring in one automation project a year, slowly, and move people into other roles rather than letting them go.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Claire:** Uneasy. The same pressure I already feel.

- **Says:** “We're using AI to improve planning and food safety, and we're proud of our people.”
- **Thinks:** The board keeps pushing automation to protect margins anyway. This version just doesn't speed it up.
- **Does:** Brings in one automation project a year and moves people into other roles instead of letting them go.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Claire:** I'd say we're adopting new tools carefully and keeping our workforce at the heart of it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Claire:** Slow gives me room. I can let retirements do the cutting instead of layoffs. Fewer young hires is a problem, though. In a town like this, the plant is where young people start.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Claire:** Keep an apprentice scheme going even if the board questions it. Let numbers fall through retirements rather than layoffs.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Claire:** Uneasy, but I can see a path.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We're adopting new tools carefully, with our workforce at the heart of it.”
- **Thinks:** Slow gives me room to cut through retirements. But in this town, the plant is where young people start.
- **Does:** Keeps an apprentice scheme going despite the board, and lets numbers fall through retirements rather than layoffs.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, but she can see a path.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Claire:** I'd say our office functions will change, and we'll support everyone through it.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Claire:** The office side of the plant would get cut first. Planning, payroll, the quality paperwork. Those are people I know by name, with kids at school with mine. And the board would ask why I haven't done it already.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Claire:** Move office staff into line or quality roles where I can. Fight for a decent severance package for the rest. Get ahead of the board with my own plan so they don't hand me theirs.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Claire:** Heavy. I'd be carrying their names around.

**Interviewer:** Your public line is that you'll support everyone. Can you?

**Claire:** Not everyone. Some. I'd say everyone because the room needs to hear it.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Our office functions will change, and we'll support everyone through it.”
- **Thinks:** The office staff go first. I know them by name, and the board would ask why I haven't cut them already.
- **Does:** Moves office staff into line or quality roles where she can, fights for decent severance and gets her own plan to the board first.
- **Feels:** Heavy. Carrying their names around.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Claire:** I'd say this is a time for responsible leadership, and companies doing well have a duty to their towns. I'd mean the second part more than the board would like.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Claire:** If AI matches professionals, a lot of my own job, the planning and the reporting, could be done by a machine. The board might decide they want a cheaper site manager with an AI behind her. But the bigger weight is still the town.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Claire:** Make the case to the board for keeping jobs in money terms, because that's the language they hear. Quietly update my CV. Make sure the money for the kids' education is safe.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Claire:** Anxious. Two worries at once, mine and the town's.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Companies doing well have a duty to their towns.”
- **Thinks:** A machine could do much of my planning and reporting, and the board might want someone cheaper. But the bigger weight is the town.
- **Does:** Makes the money case to the board for keeping jobs, quietly updates her CV and secures the kids' education fund.
- **Feels:** Anxious. Her worry and the town's at once.
- **Mood:** Anxious

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Claire:** Publicly? That we'll manage the transition responsibly and work with the community.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Claire:** Privately, this is the one I dread. Food processing is all hands-on. If general-purpose robots arrive, the board will want most of the line replaced within a couple of years, and I'd be the one signing the letters. Hundreds of people, in a town with nowhere else to go.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Claire:** Fight for a phased plan, with retraining money and the longest notice I can get. Talk to the mayor and the local college early. And if the board won't listen, think hard about whether I stay.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Claire:** Sick about it. I wouldn't sleep.

**Interviewer:** Would you resign rather than sign those letters?

**Claire:** I don't know. If I go, someone who cares less signs them. That's what keeps me there.

### Summary

- **Says:** “We'll manage the transition responsibly and work with the community.”
- **Thinks:** This is the one I dread. The board will want most of the line replaced, and I'd sign the letters for hundreds of people in a town with nowhere else to go.
- **Does:** Fights for a phased plan with retraining money and long notice, brings in the mayor and local college early, and weighs whether to stay.
- **Feels:** Sick about it. Not sleeping.
- **Mood:** Alarmed
