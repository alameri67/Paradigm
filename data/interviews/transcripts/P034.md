# P034 · Raj Patel

Owner, two convenience stores · Retail trade

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Hello Raj, thanks for making time. I'm a field researcher with the Work Futures Panel, an independent group that studies how changes at work affect people's lives. Nothing you tell me will be linked to your name, so feel free to tell me what you'd say in public and what you really think.

**Raj:** Okay, but I only have twenty minutes, I'm due back at the shop. I own two convenience stores. I work most hours God sends, so be quick with me.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Raj:** I'd say fine, good. I use it sometimes for the supplier emails.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Raj:** It means nothing. My problem isn't emails. My problem is finding someone reliable for the night shift.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Raj:** Nothing different. Same seventy hours.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Raj:** Normal. Tired, but that's not the AI.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Fine, good. I use it sometimes for supplier emails.”
- **Thinks:** It changes nothing. My real problem is finding someone reliable for the night shift.
- **Does:** Carries on with the same seventy-hour weeks.
- **Feels:** Normal, just tired.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Raj:** I'd say the market will sort it out. It always does if government stays out.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Raj:** Slow is fine for me. Maybe the ordering software gets smarter and saves me an hour a week. That's an hour with my kids.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Raj:** I'd try one of the stock ordering apps for one store first, see if it cuts waste. If it works, both stores.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Raj:** Calm. Maybe a little hopeful.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The market will sort it out if government stays out.”
- **Thinks:** Smarter ordering software might save me an hour a week. That's an hour with my kids.
- **Does:** Trials a stock ordering app in one store, then rolls it out to both if it cuts waste.
- **Feels:** Calm, a little hopeful.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Raj:** I'd say office people have had it easy for a long time. Now they know how a shopkeeper feels.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Raj:** For me, honestly, more people looking for work means I can finally find good night staff. But if my customers lose their jobs, they spend less on coffee and cigarettes and lottery tickets.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Raj:** I'd hire a better night manager while people are available, and I'd watch my takings week by week. I'd cut stock of the expensive lines first.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Raj:** Mostly fine. One eye on the till.

**Interviewer:** You'd say office people had it easy. Is that something you'd really say out loud, in the shop?

**Raj:** In the shop, no, never. They're my customers. At home, with family, yes.

### Summary

- **Says:** “Office people have had it easy. Now they know how a shopkeeper feels.”
- **Thinks:** I'd only say that at home, never in the shop. More jobseekers means good night staff, but customers who lose jobs will spend less.
- **Does:** Hires a better night manager while people are available, tracks takings weekly and cuts back on expensive lines.
- **Feels:** Mostly fine, one eye on the till.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Raj:** I'd say the government will use this crisis to tax small business to death. Watch.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Raj:** My shops sell to people. If lots of people lose good jobs, they still buy milk, but they stop buying the extras where I make my money. And a political crisis means new taxes. That's always where it lands.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Raj:** I'd go through every cost line, renegotiate with suppliers and cut my own pay first. I'd hold off on the third store I was thinking about.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Raj:** Uneasy. I've been through bad times before. I know how to tighten.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government will use this crisis to tax small business to death.”
- **Thinks:** People still buy milk but stop buying the extras where I make my money. And new taxes always land on me.
- **Does:** Goes through every cost, renegotiates with suppliers, cuts his own pay first and shelves plans for a third store.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, but he knows how to tighten.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it at work, to friends, or online?

**Raj:** I'd say about time. Let a robot stack shelves at 3 a.m. and let me sleep.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Raj:** This is what I've wanted for years. But the big chains get the robots first, cheaper, and then their prices fall and mine don't. If I can afford one robot for the night shift, I'm saved. If I can't, I'm finished.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year? Be as specific as you can.

**Raj:** I'd find out the price on day one. I'd ask the bank about a loan, or a lease, like with the fridges. I'd rather owe money on a robot than lose to the supermarket down the road.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Raj:** Hopeful and worried at the same time. Like a big bet.

**Interviewer:** Your staff do the work a robot would do. Have you thought about them?

**Raj:** Yes. I'd keep my day staff, they know the customers. The night shift, I'm sorry, but nobody wants that shift anyway. I've been doing it myself.

### Summary

- **Says:** “About time. Let a robot stack shelves at 3 a.m. and let me sleep.”
- **Thinks:** The chains get robots first and cheaper. If I can afford one for the night shift, I'm saved. If not, I'm finished.
- **Does:** Gets prices on day one, asks the bank about a loan or lease, and plans to keep day staff while automating the night shift.
- **Feels:** Hopeful and worried, like a big bet.
- **Mood:** Uneasy
