# N12 · Margaret Hughes

Retiree, ex-teacher, in assisted living · Outside paid work

_Simulated interview. The person is a made-up persona, and the answers are a model's guess at how someone like them would react. They aren't real quotes._

## Opening

**Interviewer:** Thanks for making the time, Margaret. I'm with the Work Futures Panel, an independent research group. We don't sell anything or campaign for anyone. Nothing you tell me today will be linked to your name. I'll read you five short pictures of how AI might change things over the next five years, and ask how you'd react to each.

**Margaret:** How lovely to have a visitor. I taught for thirty-odd years, so I'm used to asking the questions, not answering them. I've never used AI myself. The young carers here sometimes show me things on their phones.

## S0 · No major impact

**Interviewer:** Picture this. AI stays a handy tool, like a better search engine. Hiring, pay and prices carry on the way they would have anyway. If this happened, what would you say about it to the other residents or your family, to friends, or online?

**Margaret:** I'd say every generation has its new gadget. When I started teaching it was the overhead projector. The good ones help, and life carries on.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Margaret:** It wouldn't change my days at all. Breakfast, the crossword, my friend down the corridor, and the girls who look after us.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, in your daily life here and with your money? Be as specific as you can.

**Margaret:** Nothing, really. Perhaps ask one of the carers to show me how to talk to it, out of curiosity.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Margaret:** Perfectly content.

- **Thinks:** It wouldn't change her days at all.

## S1 · Capable but slow

**Interviewer:** Now picture this. Very capable AI exists, but it spreads slowly, over ten years or more. Some tasks disappear, but few whole jobs do. Companies hire fewer beginners. If this happened, what would you say about it to the other residents or your family, to friends, or online?

**Margaret:** I'd say it's a pity for young people. You learn a job by doing the easy bits first. If a machine takes the easy bits, how do you learn?

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Margaret:** Slow change suits me. I'd think of my former pupils, though. Some are just starting out now. I hope someone gives them a chance.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, in your daily life here and with your money? Be as specific as you can.

**Margaret:** Nothing much. Perhaps write to one or two of my old pupils, ask how they're getting on.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Margaret:** A little sad for the young ones. Otherwise fine.

### Summary

- **Says:** “You learn a job by doing the easy bits first, so it's a pity if machines take them.”
- **Thinks:** Slow change suits her, but she hopes her former pupils starting out get a chance.
- **Does:** Changes little, and writes to a couple of former pupils to ask how they're doing.
- **Feels:** A little sad for the young ones.
- **Mood:** Calm

## S2 · Moderately fast

**Interviewer:** Next one. Within 5 years, AI assistants that can act on their own handle most routine office work. There are layoffs and pay pressure in office jobs. Some new AI-related jobs appear, and politicians argue loudly about what to do. If this happened, what would you say about it to the other residents or your family, to friends, or online?

**Margaret:** I'd say the government must look after the people who lose their jobs. That's what it's there for. I do think they'd find a way, once they stop shouting at each other.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Margaret:** My pension is safe, I should think. It's my nephew I'd think of. He works in an office, and he's a worrier.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, in your daily life here and with your money? Be as specific as you can.

**Margaret:** Ring my nephew more often. Keep an eye on what my pension pays, because the fees here go up every year.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Margaret:** A small worry. Nothing that keeps me up.

### Summary

- **Says:** “The government must look after people who lose their jobs, and she trusts it will once politicians stop shouting.”
- **Thinks:** Her pension is probably safe, but her nephew in an office job would be at risk.
- **Does:** Rings her nephew more often and watches her pension against rising care fees.
- **Feels:** A small worry, not keeping her up.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S3 · Fast

**Interviewer:** Now a faster version. Within 2 to 3 years, AI matches skilled professionals at most thinking work. Lots of office and professional workers lose their jobs. Share and house prices boom. There's a political crisis over who should get the gains. Physical jobs are mostly untouched. If this happened, what would you say about it to the other residents or your family, to friends, or online?

**Margaret:** I'd say it isn't fair if a few people get very rich and lots of families lose their living. The gains ought to be shared, and I'd say so at our residents' meeting.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Margaret:** If house prices go up, this place might get dearer. I've only my pension and a little saved. I'd worry about being able to stay.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, in your daily life here and with your money? Be as specific as you can.

**Margaret:** Ask the manager about the fees for next year. Speak to my nephew about whether he could help if needed, though I'd hate to ask.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Margaret:** Uneasy. I've always paid my own way.

### Summary

- **Says:** “It isn't fair for a few to get very rich while families lose their living; the gains ought to be shared.”
- **Thinks:** Rising prices could push up her care home fees, and with only her pension and small savings she worries about staying.
- **Does:** Asks the manager about next year's fees and, reluctantly, talks to her nephew about whether he could help.
- **Feels:** Uneasy, she's always paid her own way.
- **Mood:** Uneasy

## S4 · Fast AI and fast robots

**Interviewer:** Last one. Everything in the previous picture happens, and on top of that, general-purpose robots arrive in warehouses, kitchens, building sites, farms and care homes within 3 to 5 years. Hands-on work is no longer a safe place to be. If this happened, what would you say about it to the other residents or your family, to friends, or online?

**Margaret:** I'd say a care home is about people, not chores. You can't hug a robot. I'd say it to anyone who'd listen.

**Interviewer:** And just between us, off the record, what do you really think it means for you?

**Margaret:** I'll be honest, part of me can see it. A robot to help me out of the bath without waiting, I wouldn't mind that. But if they replace the girls who sit and chat with me, that's the best part of my day gone.

**Interviewer:** What would you actually do in the first year, in your daily life here and with your money? Be as specific as you can.

**Margaret:** Speak up at the residents' meeting, ask the manager what they plan. Make more effort with friends here, and ask my nephew to visit more.

**Interviewer:** How does it feel, picturing that?

**Margaret:** Worried. Afraid of being lonely, more than anything.

**Interviewer:** You said you can't hug a robot, but also that you'd like one for the bath. How do those sit together?

**Margaret:** Quite easily, really. Let it do the lifting. Just leave me the people. It's the company I'd miss, not the help.

### Summary

- **Says:** “A care home is about people, not chores, and you can't hug a robot.”
- **Thinks:** She'd welcome a robot for lifting and bathing, but losing the carers who chat with her would take the best part of her day.
- **Does:** Raises it at the residents' meeting, asks the manager about plans, makes more effort with friends and asks her nephew to visit more.
- **Feels:** Worried, mostly afraid of being lonely.
- **Mood:** Anxious
